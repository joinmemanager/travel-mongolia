Dadal sum

Dadal sum, Khentii: Chinggis Khaan's Birthplace and Sights

Dadal sum lies in the far northeast of Khentii Province (aimag), in the Onon River basin. History and legend remember it as the birthplace of the Great Emperor Chinggis Khaan (Genghis Khan). The sum was founded in 1923. Today you can see pine forests, clear lakes, sacred mountains and many sites linked to legends of Chinggis Khaan's childhood, all in one place. You can also learn about Buryat daily life, traditional food and crafts.

## Where is Dadal sum, and how far is it from Ulaanbaatar?

Dadal sum is in the northeastern corner of Mongolia. It is 600 km from the capital, Ulaanbaatar, and 270 km from the provincial center, Undurkhaan. The road is rough, so prepare well for the long trip. The sum borders Russia to the north. To the east it borders Bayan-Uul sum in Dornod Province. To the south it borders Norovlin sum, to the southwest Bayan-Adarga sum, and to the west Binder sum, all in Khentii Province. For other sums and sights in the province, see the [Khentii Province page](/province/khentii). Geographically, the sum covers the eastern foothills of the Khentii Range, the Eree Range and the Onon River basin. The sum center is in Bayan-Ovoo bag (the smallest administrative unit). It lies on the slopes of scenic Bayan-Ovoo Mountain, near the legendary Deluun Boldog, south of the Balj River.

## How many people live in Dadal, and what are its bags?

Dadal sum has four bags: Bayan-Ovoo, Agats, Onon and Balj. In 2017, the sum had 890 households and 2,891 residents. Most residents belong to the Buryat ethnic group. You can see this in daily life, customs, food and crafts.

## What is the nature and climate like in Dadal?

The sum lies 800 m above sea level. The lowest point is where the Onon and Balj rivers meet. The highest point is Bugat Mountain (1,513 m) at the head of Suugt, on a bend of the Balj River. Here the southern edge of the Siberian taiga gives way to the Daurian steppe. This border position supports many animal and plant species, but the nature is very fragile. The climate is humid and cool. Over the last 10 years, average precipitation was 3.2 mm in winter, 12.2 mm in spring, 104.8 mm in summer and 17.6 mm in autumn. Winter snow cover is about 40 cm deep. Wildlife includes the very rare white-naped crane, taimen and the river's khaliu fish. Gray wolves, foxes, corsac foxes, lynx, wild boar and mountain hares also live here. Rare plants such as the derevgar jirgeruu and the white tseene grow here. Berries and medicinal plants are plentiful, including Asian bird cherry, wild strawberry, nokhoin khoshuu, dolgoono, ukhriin nud, tekhiin shee, khad, blueberry and urel. The sum's ecotourism plan relies on the nearby [Onon-Balj Protected Area](</destination/protected/onon-balj>).

## What are Buryat culture and the Altargana Festival?

The people of Dadal sum continue to develop the traditional livestock herding, crafts and cultural heritage of the Buryat people. They have long used horse carts and wooden troughs. They also carve bowls, ladles, spoons and plates from wood and tree roots, and sell them as souvenirs. The Altargana Buryat song festival was first organized here in 1994 to support Buryat folk culture and art. It later grew into a major international festival. When the festival's 20th anniversary was held in Dadal sum, more than 10,000 guests and delegates took part. Contests covered 13 types of art and sport showing Buryat culture and tradition. Among the Dadal participants, A. Saikhantamir Dangina, a student at the Mongolian University of Science and Technology, took second place and won a silver medal. In the Buryat song composition category, the singer N. Ganzolboo also took second place and won a silver medal with her song "Galttaimni".

## What are Dadal sum's famous products?

**Buryat bread.** Famous across the country, this bread is baked without yeast, and the dough rises on its own. It was named best of the best three times at Khentii's autumn festival and trade fair. **Cream, zuukhii and urum.** Selling milk and dairy products is herders' main source of income. Cream from Dadal residents was named best of the best twice at the Khentii Province trade fair. Zuukhii, made by churning milk, is the main brand product of Buryat herders. Urum is made by mixing churned cream with Asian bird cherry or other fruit. "Dadal urum with Asian bird cherry" is well known in the sum and province. **Cattle herd.** Since 1964, the sum has worked to improve its cattle breed and now has Simmental cattle. Today cattle make up 33.1 percent of all livestock in the sum.

## What about farming and jobs in Dadal?

Dadal sum has a total of 7,119 hectares of crop rotation land. Three business entities operate there. Two of them, Dadal Budai LLC and Enkh Nogoon Tal LLC, provide seasonal temporary jobs for 25–30 people and permanent jobs for 8 people. Potatoes and vegetables are also grown on 25–30 hectares. 46 residents grow fruit and berries on 12 hectares to meet their households' needs. The sum's own harvest fully covers the vegetable needs of public institutions, and 20 seasonal workers work temporarily on this task.

## What are the sum's goals for the future?

The sum's main development goal is to reduce unemployment and poverty in a real way and improve residents' livelihoods. It aims to develop agriculture, small and medium-sized industry and tourism, use natural resources wisely, and become a model sum that keeps its nature in its original state. This goal is divided into several areas. **Green economy.** The sum will support green production and services based on modern eco-technology. It will develop a multi-sector economy that combines agriculture, production of natural products, tourism, and rest and spa services. It will develop intensive agriculture with low greenhouse gas emissions and little waste, using technology safe for human health and the environment, and increase the output of natural products. In this way, residents are expected to earn a stable income from business. **Ecotourism.** The sum plans to develop ecotourism in three directions, "History – Nature – Spa and Rest", with standard services that meet environmental and hygiene requirements, and to promote the Dadal travel brand at home and abroad. Based on the resources and capacity of the Onon-Balj Protected Area and its surroundings, it has set a goal of developing responsible tourism with at least 5 types and routes, with local participation and no negative effect on nature. These include photo tours to watch flowers, butterflies, rare insects, birds and animals, nature and history tours to historic sites, health tours, sport hunting tours, tours to learn about Buryat daily life and customs, and tours with cultural and sports events. The sum also aims to expand the eco zone by setting up tourist camps, campsites and rest stops with services and technology that do not harm nature. **Citizen participation and a green lifestyle.** The sum believes that every resident's awareness, knowledge and skills are important for becoming a model green-development sum, and it will train them step by step. In this work, it will build a citizenry that is proud of the homeland of the Great Khan and lives a green lifestyle. It will involve the state, citizens and non-governmental organizations together and form new partnerships. Teams will give residents advice on health, education and business. The sum has set goals to introduce healthy living habits to extend residents' average life expectancy, run lifelong education programs, offer training and awareness campaigns suited to every age, and launch a movement to learn a green lifestyle. It will also support jobs that use technology safe for human health and the environment, and reduce the number of unemployed and low-income households. **Environment, settlements and waste.** The sum aims to become a green area that keeps its nature in its original state by building eco-friendly attitudes and habits among the public and by protecting and using resources thriftily and efficiently. It will develop the network of national and local protected areas and improve protection management to keep biodiversity and ecosystem services stable. It will reduce the effects of climate change and strengthen its ability to prevent dangerous weather events, natural disasters and forest and steppe fires. In the sum center and other settlements, it will support sustainable production and consumption that use advanced technology and innovation, and increase clean-technology investment. It will strengthen nature protection with citizen participation, create a healthy and pleasant living environment, and improve waste management.

## What is there to see in Dadal sum?

The sum has many places to see, including sites linked to legends of Chinggis Khaan and his ancestors, places named in The Secret History of the Mongols, museums, religious monuments, mineral springs and caves. GPS coordinates for each place are given in brackets.

### Places linked to Chinggis Khaan

- [**Deluun Boldog**](</destination/landscapes/deluun-boldog>)**.** On July 20, 1990, for the 750th anniversary of the publication of The Secret History of the Mongols, a ceremony to worship the sky was held on Eree Tolgoi hill at Boldog, and a monument of light was installed. The monument carries a Mongolian-script inscription that reads: "Chinggis Khaan was born here at Deluun Boldog on the 16th day of the first summer month in the Year of the Water Horse, 1162." (N49°03’04.7 E111°38’36.3)
- **Chinggis monument of light.** It was built on the northern terrace of Dund Lake, one of the Gurvannuur lakes, on May 31, 1962, for the 800th anniversary of Chinggis Khaan's birth. S. Tumur-Ochir, a member of the Politburo of the Mongolian People's Revolutionary Party, led the work, and the sculptor L. Makhbal designed it. The monument has the shape of a blooming flame, is 11.7 m tall and has a base 10 m wide. The front shows Chinggis Khaan standing and is carved with this teaching: "If my body tires, let it tire; may my state never be lost." It is 2 km east of the sum center. (N49°02’00.40 E111°39’16.70)
- **Temujin Gate.** A gate built in the shape of a stupa in 2012 with donations from residents of the sum. (N48°59’33.27 E111°36’41.21)
- **Energy ovoo (sacred stone cairn).** People pray at this ovoo in the belief that they receive vital energy from the Eternal Blue Sky. It is 0.5 km southwest of Deluun Boldog. (N49°02’58.3 E111°38’28.6)
- **Khajuu Spring.** It lies at the left side of the Black Banner ovoo, northeast of the sum. Legend says that when Temujin was born, he was washed with water from this spring, and Mother Hoelun used it to make her tea and drink. It is 2 km northwest of Deluun Boldog. (N49°02’45.9 E111°36’53.0)
- **Tunkhelug Brook.** This brook is often mentioned in The Secret History of the Mongols. It is said that Alan Gua met Dobun Mergen near it. Bodonchar is said to have spoken his famous teaching here, "It is good to have a leader, as a body has a head," and Dondogdulam and Genenpil, queens of Mongolia's last khan, lived here. It is 4 km west of Khajuu Spring. (N49°02’50.82 E111°38’28.69)
- **Dobun Mergen memorial.** A very ancient monument made of several upright stones. Locals have long said it is the memorial and grave of Duva Sokhor and Dobun Mergen. It is 1.5 km northwest of Tunkhelug Brook. (N49°03’17.18 E111°33’09.11)
- **Bodonchar's grass hut.** Article 24 of The Secret History of the Mongols says that Bodonchar the Simple, the first ancestor of the Khiad Borjigin clan of Chinggis Khaan's golden lineage, lived here on Balj Island in a hut built of grass. He is said to be the first person to speak the wise saying, "A man has elder brothers, a deel (traditional robe) has a collar." It is 1.5 km northwest of the khirigsuur (burial mound) of Dobun Mergen and Duva Sokhor. (N49°03’31.5 E111°31’07.0)
- **Chinggis's Black Banner ovoo.** According to tradition, Chinggis Khaan used to raise his great black banner on the summit of this mountain before going to war. (N49°02’11.4 E111°35’54.50)
- **Seal Stone.** According to Altan Tobchi (the Golden Summary), a small bird sang "Chinggis, Chinggis" on a black rock at the foot of the mountain. The rock split, and the jade seal of the Mongol state came out of it. The split rock is still called "Chinggis's Seal Stone." (N49°06’17.63 E111°39’18.12)
- **Santkhaan Mountain.** A sacred mountain north of the meeting point of the Onon and Agats rivers, with the "Santkhaan ovoo" on top. Legend says two male foals were born here from a mare of Chinggis Khaan's horse herd. It is said that a foal born here becomes fast, so people worship the Santkhaan ovoo and make offerings before preparing racehorses for Naadam. Herders still bring their horses to graze and foal on this mountain today. (N49°12’40.9 E111°50’28.9)
- **Degen Jigen (Zeeren Gorge).** A historic place recorded in Article 129 of The Secret History of the Mongols as the Zeeren Gorge on the Onon River. (N49°14’23.0 E112°01’13.3)
- **Burkhan Khaldun – Bayankhaan Mountain.** Locals regard Bayankhaan Mountain, with its three peaks Burkhant, Shurgadai and Orgoit, as Burkhan Khaldun Mountain. They say Chinggis Khaan hid here and escaped the Merkit, and made a vow to worship it for generations. The mountain is covered with forest and rocky scree. On its rear slope fruit trees are plentiful, and rare animals such as moose, red deer, bears and khuder, along with many kinds of birds, live there. (N49°01’11.00 E111°18’46.08)

### Museums and religious monuments

- **Temujin's Childhood Museum.** Opened in 2006 for the 800th anniversary of the founding of the Mongol state. It holds stone, bronze and iron weapons found by archaeologists from ancient people who lived in the Onon and Balj basin, straw figures showing Temujin's childhood, a seated figure of Chinggis Khaan, the black banner, and a cart-borne palace ger. (N49°01’12.86 E111°37’26.81)
- **Local History Museum.** It was first founded in 1959 on the initiative of young herders in the sum who wanted to share experience. It has added to its collection since 1971 and now holds 346 historic exhibits. (N49°01’12.80 E111°37’30.03)
- **Hunter Baavai Museum.** A ger museum built from the lifelong collection of D. Zunduidagva, a local native, war veteran, hunter and holder of the Order of the Red Banner of Battle, including his works and mounted animals. Historic exhibits showing Buryat customs are also on display. It is 1 km southwest of the site of Queen Genenpil's ger. (N49°01’53.98 E111°33’10.37)
- **Site of Queen Genenpil's ger.** A place known as the homeland of the Khiad Borjigin golden lineage, where the white horse herd of the Mongol state is said to graze. Genenpil, the last queen of the 8th Bogd Khan, who held both religious and state power, spent summers on this terrace until she was falsely accused in a political case and arrested. It is 2 km southeast of Bodonchar's grass hut. (N49°02’16.04 E111°33’12.71)
- **Three stupas of the root lamas.** Stupas topped with the three root deities Janraisig, Manjushri and Vajrapani. They were built to increase the merit of the Dharma and purify sins, in memory of ancestors who died before their time in the political repression of the 1930s. (N49°01’05.18 E111°36’45.09)
- **Ruins of Gilber Monastery.** The remains of a monastery that was a major cultural center of Yellow Hat Buddhism, with about 300 lamas, from 1700 to 1937. It is said that on September 20, 1920, the Tsarist Russian general Baron Ungern passed through this monastery with his Asian Cavalry Division as he went down the Gilber River into Bogd Khan's Mongolia. (N49°11’24.46 E111°24’09.07)

### Nature, mineral springs and caves

- **Gurvannuur.** Three small lakes lie close together on a flat clearing in a pine forest and are known as the "Gurvannuur mineral spring". The lake water and mud are used to treat stomach, liver, gallbladder and eczema conditions. Dund Lake has leeches, so it is said to help with joint disease. Water from Zuun Lake is drunk for internal and intestinal illness. To learn about the sanatorium here, read the [Gurvannuur Mineral Spring Sanatorium page](</things-to-do/wellness/gurvannuuryn-rashaan-suvilal>). (N49°01’54.94 E111°39’15.38)
- **Tsagaanchuluut.** A place said to have the power to heal all kinds of illness by traditional and natural methods. You could call it a natural physical therapy complex. (N49°05’23.70 E111°03’19.80)
- **Suugt Nukhtiin Cave.** It lies on the west side of the Suugt River valley, a tributary of the Galttai River. It is hidden from direct view on the short back slope of a rocky mountain, on the left branch of the Jivkheestei River. The cave has a vertical, well-like shape and is 72 m deep. (N49°10’20.78 E111°58’35.61)

@@ FIELD body richText
Tes sum is in the northern part of Zavkhan Province (aimag), among the spurs of the Tagna Range and the Khan Khukhii Range. It once had a large crop-farming enterprise that supplied flour and fodder to the western provinces. Today it is an interesting area of mountains, rivers, forests, many kinds of wildlife and birds, and many monuments that have not yet been studied.

## Where is Tes sum?

The sum lies more than 1,300 m above sea level. It borders [Bayantes](</province/zavkhan/bayantes>) and [Bayankhairkhan](/province/zavkhan/bayankhairkhan) sums in Zavkhan Province, Zuunkhangai sum in Uvs Province, and the Tuva Republic of Russia. Its total area is 87,229 hectares. The sum center is at a place called Zur, 301 km from the provincial center. To learn about the province's other sums and sights, read the [Zavkhan Province page](/province/zavkhan).

## How many people live in Tes and what are its bags?

Tes sum has four bags (the smallest administrative unit): Doono, Niseg, Bayan-Uul and Zur. About 3,386 people in 894 households live in the sum, and it has 49,200 head of livestock.

## What is the history of Tes sum, from banner to sum?

The administrative history of this area begins in the 17th century. In 1655, the banner (khoshuu) of Erdenedureech Wang, part of the Khotgoid Zasagt Khan Province, was established here. Under Qing rule, it was called the Left Wing Banner of the Left Center Wing of the Western Route of Khalkha. In 1924–1931 it was the Delgerkhan Uul banner of Khantaishir Uul Province. In 1931–1959 it was part of Bayan-Uul sum. The present sum dates back to 1958. At that time, residents of the Doono, Bayan-Uul, Berkh and Niseg bags of Bayan-Uul sum said that their land was too far from the sum center and did not suit them. After the proposal was checked on site, a decree of the Presidium of the State Great Khural of the Mongolian People's Republic, dated May 31, 1959, separated the territory and residents of these bags into a new sum named Tes, based on the "Delgerekh" cooperative. So in 1959, the new sum was created at the Tes fodder farm settlement in Bayan-Uul sum. At first, the new sum had 341 households, 1,507 people, 52,400 head of livestock and 2,298 hectares of land. Soon after, as part of the nationwide virgin lands campaign, a crop-farming enterprise (TAA) was founded in Tes in 1969. But it was decided that livestock pasture and crop fields did not suit each other, so in 1974 the "Delgerekh" cooperative of Tes sum was dissolved. It was merged with the "Jargalant-Amidral" cooperative of Bayan-Uul sum to form the "Delgerekh-Amidral" cooperative, and placed under Bayan-Uul sum. Tes sum then lost its status. It was renamed the Tes TAA of Bayantes sum, or Tes khoroo (sub-district), and Ch. Yadamjav was appointed head of the khoroo administration and of the TAA. At that time, Tes had 205 households, more than 700 people and 23,000 hectares of crop fields. This did not last long. By Decree No. 138 of the Presidium of the State Great Khural of the Mongolian People's Republic in 1977, Tes khoroo became a sum again. In the 1990s, the Doono and Niseg bags of Bayantes sum, which had once belonged to the old Tes sum, moved back to Tes sum. This set the sum's borders as they are today.

## How was the "Delgerekh" cooperative founded?

The "Delgerekh" cooperative, which operated in the Tes area, was founded in 1956 on the initiative of G. Ish, G. Ichinnorov, I. Damdinbazar and L. Magsar. At first, 23 members from 17 households in the 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 9th bags of Bayan-Uul sum pooled more than 2,000 head of livestock. I. Damdinbazar was elected the cooperative's first chairman. The cooperative later grew to three livestock brigades, more than 500 members, fixed assets of MNT 3 million and an annual income of MNT 1 million. By 1972, it had more than 2,500 members from over 500 households, 67,700 head of livestock, fixed assets of MNT 4 million and an annual income of MNT 2 million. In 1974, when the sum was dissolved because of the Tes crop-farming enterprise, the cooperative was merged into the "Jargalant-Amidral" cooperative of Bayan-Uul sum.

## What was the Tes crop-farming enterprise?

As a result of the virgin lands campaign of the 1960s, construction of the Tes crop-farming enterprise began with Soviet help, under Resolution No. 157 of the Council of Ministers of the Mongolian People's Republic in 1969. At this time, the livestock machine station (MAAMS) of Bayan-Uul sum was dissolved. More than 20 pieces of equipment, including 9 tracked and wheeled tractors, farm trailers and swather machines, with total assets of MNT 2 million, were transferred to the Tes TAA. In 1969, the first plowing was done at Tevkhen, Tsagaanchuluut, Toont and other places. A total of 3,056 hectares were worked, and 3,100 tons of hay were prepared, which started production. In 1972, the Russian side handed over the completed enterprise, with fixed assets of MNT 35 million. It included the following facilities:

- a fodder plant able to produce 10,000 tons of fodder a year;
- a farm machinery repair shop able to service 120 tractors;
- a motor garage for about 70 vehicles;
- a power station with a capacity of 900 kW;
- a steam boiler producing 10,000 kcal of heat a year;
- a workers' canteen seating 35 people, a bakery and a bathhouse;
- housing for 50 families.

At first, the Tes TAA had no livestock at all and only grew crops. In 1973, it took over 200 cattle and 220 horses from the Baruunturuun state farm in Uvs Province. In 1975, it took over more than 8,000 sheep and goats, 37 herders and 13 winter camps from Bayantes sum. Soon it also ran side operations raising pigs, chickens and rabbits. The enterprise combined crop farming, livestock herding and fodder production. Of its total output, 88.6 percent came from crops, 5.2 percent from livestock and 6.2 percent from fodder production. As of 1987, the Tes TAA had 2,900 people in 529 households and 32,000 hectares of crop fields. Each year it sowed grain on 19,500 hectares and harvested 19–28 thousand tons. This harvest not only met the region's flour needs but also supplied wheat to the flour mills in Ulaangom and Murun. It also prepared about 10,000 tons of pressed livestock fodder each year and delivered it to the five western provinces. This crop production stopped during the hard market transition of the 1990s.

## What are the climate and soil like?

Tes sum has a harsh continental climate. Summer temperatures average 23–32 °C. Winters drop to −35 to −42 °C. The area gets 180–210 mm of precipitation a year. The soils are fertile dark brown and sandy.

## What are the mountains, rivers, plants and animals in Tes?

**Mountains.** Ikh Berkh, Baga Berkh, Baruun Doono, Zuun Doono, Khalban, Zur, Zos, Dormon, Nart, Navkhan, Ongon, Kholboo, Talyn Uvuljin, Aambagaa, Jaambagaa, Arts Suuri, Argalant and Suman Rock. **Rivers and streams.** Tes, Zaigal, Tsereg, Mogoi and Khatguurantai. You can read more about the Tes River in the [page on the Tes River area](</destination/protected/tesiin-gol-orchmyn-nutag>). **Plants.** Grasses and herbs include degd, yumduzhin, ajigtseren, mekheer, wild onion, mangin, gonid, tseene and tagsh. Berries and nuts include pine nuts, sea buckthorn, toshloi, ukhriin nud, chonyn khel and gishuune. Trees and shrubs include Siberian pine, black wood, aspen, spruce, willow, nokhoin khoshuu, tavilgana and yargai. **Wildlife.** Animals living here include red deer, boar, roe deer, badger, chipmunk, mountain hare, rabbit, wolf, lynx, fox, corsac fox, Pallas's cat, marmot, zuram, uyen, solongo, steppe polecat, dog-like fox, and steppe sable. **Birds.** Birds found here include black grouse, hur, raven, golden eagle, vulture, magpie, turliakh, kharzaga, sar, owl, capercaillie, falcon, uvuulj, Daurian partridge, cuckoo, swan, goose, ruddy shelduck, crane and bustard.

## What is there to see in Tes sum?

The sum has natural and historical sites. The best known are **Suman Rock**, **Dormon Mountain**, **Khuulgyn Cave** and the **Zur Turkic inscription**. Dozens of other monuments in this area have not yet been studied.

Khanbogd sum lies in Umnugovi Province (aimag) on the border with China. It is known for Galbyn Gobi, the monasteries founded by Noyon Khutagt Danzanravjaa, and the Oyu Tolgoi mine. In this area of steppe and desert, you will find scenic natural formations and historical and religious sites in one place.

## Where is Khanbogd sum and how far is it?

The sum is 260 km from the provincial center and borders China to the south. Its area is 1,515.2 km². It has 4 bags (the smallest administrative unit). Six budget-funded organizations operate in the sum, employing more than 100 civil servants. To learn about the province's other sums and sights, read the [Umnugovi Province page](/province/umnugov). The sum is a busy transport hub. The Gashuun Sukhait border crossing operates here year-round. The main paved road linking Oyu Tolgoi and the Gashuun Sukhait crossing with Ulaanbaatar passes through a four-way junction of the provincial centers of Umnugovi, Dornogovi and Govisumber.

## How did Khanbogd get its name?

The sum was founded in 1924 under the name "Galba" at a place called Khar Ovoo buuts in Khadan Khoshuu. In 1941 it took the name of Khanbogd Mountain and became Khanbogd sum. Next to the Three Galba Monasteries, a marker was erected to commemorate the founding of the sum under the name Galba in 1924.

## What natural resources does Khanbogd have, and what is Oyu Tolgoi?

Khanbogd's subsoil holds rich reserves of gold and copper. Rare finds also turn up here, including remains of giant lizards and reptiles from early geological eras, and petrified wood. Oyu Tolgoi, considered one of the world's major copper and gold mines, operates 40 km west of the sum. It is a huge project expected to bring great benefits to Mongolia's economy. Its concentrator plant went into operation on December 27, 2017.

## What wildlife and birds live in Khanbogd?

Galbyn Gobi once had large herds of hoofed steppe animals such as khulan (Mongolian wild ass) and goitered gazelle. As mining roads and traffic increased, they moved into Dornogovi Province. Even so, the Houbara Bustard (Chlamydotis undulata), which is rare worldwide, still lives here. The Mongolian Ground Jay (Podoces hendersoni) and the Saxaul Sparrow (Passer ammodendri) are also common. In recent years, a large pond formed from the surplus water of the Oyu Tolgoi company's plant has become a major stopover for migrating waterbirds. Argali and Siberian ibex are fairly common on Khanbogd Mountain, the main sacred site for local people. Meeting goitered gazelle and khulan along the way lifts the spirits of travelers.

## What is there to see in Khanbogd sum?

**Natural formations.** This sum is home to the Undai River, the Moving Rock (Khudulduk Tsokhio), Frog Rock, Ikh Bulag, Baga Bulag, Baishin Tsav, Chinggis Khaan's Tent Peg Hole, Three Gazelles Cave, the 3000 Elms, the Sevkhuul Sands, the Khaalga Mineral Spring and [Galbyn Gobi](</destination/landscapes/galbyn-govi>), one of the nine wonders of Umnugovi. **Monasteries of Galbyn Gobi.** In Galbyn Gobi, immortalized in songs and poems, 12 km southwest of the sum, lie the ruins of the three famous monasteries founded by Noyon Khutagt Danzanravjaa. Nearby are other places of natural and historical interest: the ruins of Tsogzol Monastery, Altangerel's Wheel, Lovonchomb's Chavg Cave, which is said to heal, the stomach and eye springs, and the liver and gallbladder stone. Demchog Monastery was rebuilt with funding from the Umnugovi-based Ajnai corporation, which opened a new option for domestic travel. About 6 km northwest of the monastery, the "World Energy Center" tourist ger camp has been built. To read more about the monasteries, see the [Three Galba Monasteries page](</destination/heritage/place/galbyn-gurvan-khiid>).

![image](asset:tic-img-8b5e1c8a865da194)

## How can I spend one day around Demchog Monastery?

Most people picture Demchog Monastery, known as the "World Energy Center," as a place to gather energy, with the Bayajikh Cave, Mother's Womb and Frog Rock. But there are many interesting places nearby that few people know or visit. Embuu Rock, the Khutagt's Meditation Seat, Chinggis Khaan's Tent Peg Hole, Dombon Tsokhio and the Stone Factory each have their own history and legends. Visiting them all takes a full day. **Ereet Monastery.** The second of the Three Galba Monasteries, Ereet Monastery once housed more than 300 lamas. It lies 3 km north of Demchog Monastery (the first monastery). Its ruins resemble those of Ongi Monastery. On the rock east of the ruins, an image of the Red Jamsran deity appears naturally. Southeast of the monastery is a rock shaped like a woman lying face up. It is said to face the Erdene Rock (Boovon Rock) of Demchog Monastery. Legend says that men come here to pray for a spouse and children. **Tsagaan Tolgoi Monastery.** Travel 3 km east of Ereet Monastery to see the ruins of Tsagaan Tolgoi Monastery, the third of the Three Galba Monasteries. It is said that the three monasteries are arranged in a pyramid shape for a reason. The surrounding landscape is beautiful, with water, springs and canyons. **Sights to the southeast.** Heading southeast from the sum, you can see the ruins of Tsogzol Monastery and the Moving Rock (Khudulduk Tsokhio), which has an unusual shape. You can also see the image of the protective deity Yulkha, created by the Khutagt, which shows a boy riding a red goat and guards against all evil.

## How do I get to Khanbogd sum and Demchog Monastery?

You can reach Demchog Monastery from the Khanbogd sum center by two roads. The direct road is only 12 km, but it is a rocky, rough mountain track. The other is the Oyu Tolgoi road, a straight white road of about 40 km. Both roads take the same time and fuel, so if you do not know the mountain track, take the Oyu Tolgoi road. If you want a longer trip, you can travel 150 km to Khanbogd along this route: "Uush Mankhan – Morin Tolgoi Tsav – Uekhii – Sevkhuul – Mogoit – Khoyor Ulziit Mountain – Khanbogd Mountain – Bayangol – Amtgai – Argalant – Suikhent – Ulgii Monastery – Three Galba Monasteries."

## Where can I stay in Khanbogd?

Khanbogd is a large, comfortable sum with plenty of light and buildings. Compared with other sums in Mongolia, it has many comfortable hotels. In the sum center, quite a few hotels and restaurants are connected to clean water and sewage lines. There is potential to develop tourism near the border in the future.

## What livestock do herders raise in Khanbogd?

Since 2006, Khanbogd has led the country year after year for its Galbyn red camels. Herders also raise select breeds, including Gavilud-strain sheep with extra vertebrae and Gobi Gurvan Saikhan-breed goats with fine cashmere.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Khanbogd sum from the provincial center?

It is 260 km from the center of Umnugovi Province. Oyu Tolgoi is 40 km west of the sum, and the monasteries of Galbyn Gobi are 12 km southwest.

### Which road should I take to Demchog Monastery?

You can take the direct 12 km rough mountain road from the sum center, or the roughly 40 km white road of Oyu Tolgoi. Both take the same time and fuel, so if you do not know the way, choose the Oyu Tolgoi road.

### What rare birds live in Khanbogd sum?

The Houbara Bustard, which is rare worldwide, lives here, along with the Mongolian Ground Jay and the Saxaul Sparrow. Migrating waterbirds stop at the pond formed from Oyu Tolgoi's surplus water.

Bayantumen sum lies on the edge of Choibalsan, the provincial center of Dornod Province (aimag), just 12 km away. It has developed as a farming area supplying the city with food, milk and vegetables. The sum center is the settlement of Tsagaan Ders. Many locals call the sum "Khan Uul."

## Where is Bayantumen sum?

The sum center, Tsagaan Ders, is 645 km from Ulaanbaatar and 12 km from [Choibalsan (Kherlen sum)](/province/dornod/kherlen). The sum gets its electricity from Choibalsan's power system. Of this, 478,411 hectares are pasture, 33,523 hectares are hayfields, and 324 hectares are cropland.

## How did Bayantumen sum begin?

The sum traces its origin to the Khudulmur cooperative (negdel), founded in January 1950. S. Batmunkh, then head of the Choibalsan city administration, took the initiative. The cooperative was set up in the city's 6th khoroo (sub-district) by a group of 7 people, including O. Gansukh, D. Danzan, Tumur and Namst. Its goal was to supply the provincial center with vegetables. In 1952, the cooperative moved from Choibalsan to a place called "Tsagaan Ders." In the 1940s, this site had held a vegetable field and auxiliary farm center of the Soviet 17th Army. The cooperative settled around the few small buildings that remained. In 1958, the Bayan cooperative of Kherlenbayan sum merged with the Khudulmur cooperative. By decision of the Presidium of the Mongolian People's Republic's Great Khural, Bayantumen sum was founded in March 1959, and the cooperative was renamed "Bayasgalant Khudulmur." S. Batmunkh served as head of the sum and the cooperative, and J. Ulziikhutag as secretary. In its founding year, the sum opened a primary school (director Yu. Sumyaa), a hospital (assistant doctor A. Jambaa), a kindergarten (manager B. Nadmid) and a trade and procurement unit (head Ishjamts). In 1960, the State Artificial Insemination Station and a veterinary branch were established.

## What kind of farming area is Bayantumen?

Bayantumen developed as a farming area near the city. Between 1974 and 1990, major agricultural facilities were built here, including a mechanized dairy farm for 400 cows and an irrigation system covering 1,000 hectares. The sum planted silage and green fodder crops on 500–1,000 hectares. It supplied one million liters of milk a year to Choibalsan's food factory. It also grew grain, potatoes and vegetables on 1,000–1,500 hectares. As the population and livestock grew each year, the cooperative's fixed assets reached MNT 41.4 million in 1990. At the exchange rate of that time, it earned MNT 7.8 million in cash income a year. During the shift to a market economy, the cooperative, some state organizations, and the dairy farm's livestock and property passed to businesses and private individuals.

## What are the nature and climate like?

The sum's terrain is hilly, with low mountains such as Galyn Shil (893 m), Mankhtai (856 m) and Bor Undur (850 m). Its soils are brown steppe-valley soils and a complex of saline soils. The Kherlen River flows through the sum, and there are small lakes such as Tsaidam and Khotont. The climate is continental. The average temperature is −22 to −24 °C in January and +20 to +22 °C in July. About 250 mm of precipitation falls each year. Wildlife includes Mongolian gazelle, wolf, fox, corsac fox and Pallas's cat. The steppe has dense vegetation dominated by feather grass, and other steppe plants such as khiagt, mangir and taana. Locals worship mountains and ovoo (sacred stone cairn) sites such as Enkh, Munkh, Khutag and Khan Uul.

## How many people and livestock does Bayantumen have?

Bayantumen sum has a population of 2,100 and 3 bags (the smallest administrative unit). It has 95,000 head of livestock in total: 670 camels, 15,400 horses, 9,800 cattle, 43,000 sheep and 26,700 goats.

Songinokhairkhan District lies in the western part of Ulaanbaatar. It takes its name from sacred Songinokhairkhan Mountain, which rises southwest of the city. The district is home to this mountain, said to be the fiercest of the four mountains of Khuree (the old Ulaanbaatar). ## When was Songinokhairkhan District founded?

Songinokhairkhan District of the capital was founded on October 1, 1992, under Resolution No. 18 of the State Great Khural of Mongolia, dated 1992. The district lies on the western side of the capital. It borders [Bayangol District](/province/ulaanbaatar#bayangol) and the Altanbulag, Bayanchandman, Bornuur and Batsumber sums of Tuv Province. Its area is 120.1 hectares. As of 2016, it had a population of 315,802 and 73,576 head of livestock. To read about the capital's other districts, see the [Ulaanbaatar page](/province/ulaanbaatar).

## What is the story behind Songinokhairkhan Mountain and its name?

Songinokhairkhan is a sacred mountain with rocky ravines, elm trees and caragana shrubs. Its three sharp peaks look imposing from far away. Long ago, wild red onions grew thickly in the blue ravines on the mountain's slopes. For this reason it was called "Songinot Khairkhan" (Onion Sacred Mountain), which later became Songinokhairkhan. Legend says a fiery black fox lives on this mountain, so snow does not settle in winter and the ground stays bare. It is said that anyone who sees the small fox, jet black with a pure white tip on its tail, becomes dazed. Not everyone can see this fox. People avoid even thinking of frightening it, let alone killing it. According to the learned lama Luvsanperenleijamts, the mountain has a guardian spirit: a blue old man riding a blue ox, with crossed fangs and a fondness for strong drink. This spirit is very fierce. It is said to quickly fulfill requests for difficult undertakings, and it has the power to appear as a military lord. So people who set out on hard tasks, such as joining the army or going to war, would entrust themselves to the old man of the mountain and pray to be saved from harm. For ages, people have worshiped him with offerings of the first of the milk and food. Because it is said to be the fiercest of the four mountains of Khuree, people did not touch the wildlife here, not even the marmots or the ground squirrels.

## What wildlife lives around Songinokhairkhan?

Marmots, ground squirrels, foxes, corsac foxes, Pallas's cats, lynx, hares and wolves live in the smaller mountains around Songinokhairkhan. In dzud (a harsh winter that kills livestock) years, red deer, roe deer, argali and Siberian ibex sometimes migrate here. Birds said to have lived here include black grouse, Daurian partridge, capercaillie, owls, ravens, magpies, golden eagles and vultures.

## What is the history of the Tuul River valley?

**Ancient finds.** The historian Dr. Navaan found arrowheads, whistling plaques and a belt with gold decoration in tombs at the foot of the mountain. **The willow people.** A group of families known as the "willow people" once lived along the Tuul River, which flows past the mountain's foot. They made willow-branch ger walls, roof frames, tent-roof rings, tent-pole ends, and saddle frames for camels, hybrid cattle and yaks, and sold them. **Birthplace of the Second Bogd.** The Second Bogd was born at a bend called Ugumur's Yellow Corner in Songinokhairkhan, while Darkhan Chin Wan Dondovdorj lived in the area. People used to sing: "In the yellow bend of Ugumur, flowers of every color bloom. In the palace of a noble and high-born family, our elder Khutagt was born." After the Khutagt's birth, Urtnasan gavj built two small temples at a place called Bayart Tolgoi on the northern terrace of the Tuul. He also had eight finely carved stone stupas made. These eight stupas collapsed. In 1950 the monk Danigai, a gavj (senior monk), brought them back and placed them around the white stupa of [Gandantegchinlen Monastery](</destination/heritage/place/gandantegchenlin-khiid>). **Mongolia's first veterinary medicine factory.** In 1915, during the Bogd Khan period, a 25-year-old agreement was signed at a place called Morin Uul Gatsaa with private veterinarians of the Russian tsarist government. Under the agreement, medicines were to be produced to keep the herds of the Tusheet Khan, Zasagt Khan and Tsetsen Khan provinces healthy. This was the first private Russian veterinary medicine factory built in Mongolia. The factory ruins stand on a small rise on the bank of the Tuul River, north of the Bio Kombinat bridge. **Ruins of Wang Khan's Lower Capital.** In the distant past, people grew grain and vegetables in the Bukhug and Turgen river basins. Just above where these two rivers meet, near the western end of Songinokhairkhan Mountain, an old city's ruins lie on a terrace of the Tuul River. It is regarded as the Lower Capital in the Tuul's black forest, where Toghrul Wang Khan of the Kereit camped. It is mentioned several times in The Secret History of the Mongols. **Khirigsuurs and noble tombs.** There are many ancient khirigsuurs and tombs near the upper ravine at the western end of Songinokhairkhan Mountain, the northeast spring of Burkhant Mountain, and the slopes of Dadar and Sevleg mountains beside Bulgat Khairkhan. Noble tombs from the 12th–13th centuries have been found on Dadar Mountain. **Mongolia's first airfield.** From 1938, Mongolia's first bomber repair shop, airfield, military units, factories and service facilities began to be built in the Tuul River basin and the Bukhug and Turgen river valley.
