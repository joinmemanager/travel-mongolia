Dadal sum lies in the far northeast of Khentii Province (aimag), in the basin of the Onon River, and is remembered in history and legend as the birthplace of Chinggis Khaan (Genghis Khan). The sum was founded in 1923. Today visitors can see pine forests, clear lakes, sacred mountains and many sites linked to legends of Chinggis Khaan's childhood, all in one place. You can also get to know Buryat daily life and culture, traditional food and handicrafts.

## Where is Dadal sum and how far is it from Ulaanbaatar?

Dadal sum is in the far northeast of Mongolia. It is 600 km from the capital, Ulaanbaatar, and 270 km from the provincial center, Undurkhaan. The road is rough, so prepare well for a long trip. To the north the sum borders Russia. It borders Bayan-Uul sum of Dornod Province to the east, Norovlin sum of Khentii Province to the south, Bayan-Adarga to the southwest and Binder to the west. For other sums and sights of the province, see the [Khentii Province page](/province/khentii). Geographically, the sum covers the eastern mountains of the Khentii Range, the Ereen Range and the Onon River basin. The sum center is in Bayan-Ovoo bag. It sits at the foot of the scenic Bayan-Ovoo Mountain, near the legendary Deluun Boldog, south of the Balj River.

## How many people live in Dadal and how is it divided?

Dadal sum has four bags: Bayan-Ovoo, Agats, Onon and Balj. In 2017 the sum had 890 households and 2,891 residents. Most residents are Buryat, and this shows clearly in the sum's daily life, customs, food and handicrafts.

## What are the nature and climate like?

The sum lies 800 meters above sea level. Its lowest point is the confluence of the Onon and Balj rivers, and its highest is Bugat Mountain (1,513 m) at the head of the Suugt, in the bend of the Balj. Here the southern edge of the Siberian taiga gives way to the Daurian steppe. Thanks to this border position, the area has many species of animals and plants, but its nature is very fragile. The climate is humid and cool. Averaged over the last 10 years, precipitation is 3.2 mm in the winter months, 12.2 mm in spring, 104.8 mm in summer and 17.6 mm in autumn. Winter snow cover is about 40 cm deep. Very rare animals include the white-naped crane, the taimen and the Eurasian otter. Gray wolves, foxes, corsac foxes, lynx, wild boar and mountain hares also live here. Rare plants include the large-flowered lady's slipper and the white peony. Berries and medicinal plants are plentiful: Asian bird cherry, wild strawberry, dog rose, hawthorn, currant, honeysuckle, lingonberry, blueberry and cowberry. The sum's eco-tourism plan is based on the nearby [Onon-Balj National Park](</destination/protected/onon-balj>).

## What is Buryat culture and the Altargana festival?

The people of Dadal sum continue to develop the traditional herding, handicrafts and cultural heritage of the Buryat people. They have long used horse carts and wooden troughs in daily life, and they carve cups, ladles, spoons and plates from wood and tree roots to sell as souvenirs. To support Buryat folk culture and art, the Altargana Buryat song festival was first organized in this sum in 1994. It has since grown into a major international festival. When the festival's 20th anniversary was celebrated in Dadal sum, more than 10,000 guests and delegates took part, and contestants competed in 13 arts and sports events showcasing Buryat culture and traditions. That year, A. Saikhantamir, a student of the Mongolian University of Science and Technology from Dadal, won second place and a silver medal in the Dangina contest. In the Buryat song category, singer N. Ganzolboo also took second place and a silver medal with the song "Galttaimni".

## What products is Dadal sum known for?

**Buryat bread.** This bread, famous across the country, is special because it rises on its own without yeast. It has been named the best of the best three times at Khentii's autumn fair. **Cream, clotted cream and urum.** The herders' main source of income is selling milk and dairy products. Dadal's cream has twice been named the best of the best at the Khentii Province fair. Separated cream (zuukhii) is the signature product of Buryat herders. Urum (clotted cream) is made by mixing separated cream with bird cherry or other berries, and "Dadal urum with bird cherry" is well known in the sum and province. **Cattle herds.** Since 1964 the sum has worked to improve its cattle breeds and now raises Simmental cattle. Today cattle make up 33.1 percent of all livestock in the sum.

## What farming and jobs are there?

Dadal sum has 7,119 hectares of crop rotation land in total. Three companies farm it. Two of them, Dadal Buudai LLC and Enkh Nogoon Tal LLC, provide seasonal jobs for 25–30 people and permanent jobs for 8. Potatoes and vegetables are also grown on 25–30 hectares. Forty-six residents grow fruit and berries on 12 hectares to meet their households' needs. The vegetable needs of the sum's public institutions are fully met by local harvests, which give seasonal work to 20 people.

## What does the sum aim for in the future?

The sum's overall development goal is to really reduce unemployment and poverty and to improve people's livelihoods. It aims to become a model sum that develops agriculture, small and medium businesses and tourism, uses its natural resources wisely and keeps its nature in its original state. This goal is broken down into several directions. **Green economy.** Support green production and services based on modern eco-technology. Develop a diverse economy combining agriculture, production of natural products, tourism, and spa and sanatorium services. Develop intensive agriculture with technology that produces few greenhouse gases and little waste and poses no risk to human health or the environment, and increase the output of natural products. In this way residents are expected to earn a steady income from business. **Eco-tourism.** The sum plans to develop eco-tourism in three directions, "History – Nature – Spa and Wellness", with standard services that meet environmental and hygiene requirements, and to promote the Dadal travel brand at home and abroad. It has set the goal of developing at least five types of responsible tours and routes, based on the resources and carrying capacity of Onon-Balj National Park and its surroundings, without harming nature and with the participation of local people. These include photo tours to watch flowers, butterflies, rare insects, birds and animals; nature and history tours of historical sites; health tours; sport hunting tours; tours to learn about Buryat life and customs; and tours with cultural and sporting events. It also aims to build tourist camps, campsites and camping spots with nature-friendly technology and services and to expand the eco-zone. **Community participation and a green lifestyle.** Believing that every resident's awareness, knowledge and skills are key to becoming a model green sum, the sum will train its people step by step. In raising citizens who are proud of the Great Khaan's birthplace and live a green lifestyle, it will ensure the joint participation of government, citizens and non-governmental organizations and build new partnerships. Advisory teams on health, education and business will work with residents. The goals include introducing healthy habits to extend life expectancy, running lifelong learning programs, offering training and outreach suited to every age, and starting a movement to learn a green lifestyle. The sum will also support work with technology that is safe for human health and the environment and reduce the number of unemployed and low-income households. **Environment, settlements and waste.** The aim is to become a green land that keeps its nature intact by building nature-friendly attitudes and habits among the public and by using and protecting resources efficiently. The sum will develop its network of national and local protected areas, improve their management, and sustainably preserve biodiversity and ecosystem services. It will reduce the impact of climate change and strengthen its ability to prevent dangerous weather, natural disasters and forest and steppe fires. In the sum center and other settlements it will support sustainable production and consumption with advanced technology and innovation and increase investment in clean technology. It will strengthen community-based nature conservation, create a healthy living environment and improve waste management.

## What is there to see in Dadal sum?

The sum has many sights: places from the legends of Chinggis Khaan and his ancestors and from "The Secret History of the Mongols", museums, religious monuments, mineral springs and caves. The GPS coordinates of each place are given in brackets.

### Places linked to Chinggis Khaan

- [**Deluun Boldog**](</destination/landscapes/deluun-boldog>)**.** On July 20, 1990, for the 750th anniversary of "The Secret History of the Mongols", a sky worship ceremony was held on Ereen Hill at Boldog and a memorial stele was erected. The stele says in traditional Mongolian script: "Chinggis Khaan was born here at Deluun Boldog on the 16th day of the first summer month of the Water Horse year, 1162." (N49°03’04.7 E111°38’36.3)
- **Chinggis Khaan memorial.** Built on May 31, 1962, on the northern terrace of Middle Lake of the Three Lakes, for the 800th anniversary of Chinggis Khaan's birth. The work was led by S. Tumur-Ochir, a member of the Political Bureau of the Mongolian People's Revolutionary Party, and designed by the sculptor L. Makhbal. The memorial is shaped like a blazing flame, 11.7 m tall, with a base 10 m wide. Its front shows a standing figure of Chinggis Khaan and is carved with his teaching: "Though my body may tire, let my nation never fall." It is 2 km east of the sum center. (N49°02’00.40 E111°39’16.70)
- **Temujin Toono Gate.** A gate in the shape of a stupa, built in 2012 with donations from the sum's residents. (N48°59’33.27 E111°36’41.21)
- **Energy Ovoo.** An ovoo (sacred stone cairn) where people pray, believing they receive energy from the heavens. It is 0.5 km southwest of Deluun Boldog. (N49°02’58.3 E111°38’28.6)
- **Khajuu Spring.** Found at the eastern side of the Khar Tug Ovoo, northeast of the sum. Legend says that when Temujin was born he was washed with water from this spring, and that Mother Hoelun used it to make tea and to drink. It is 2 km northwest of Deluun Boldog. (N49°02’45.9 E111°36’53.0)
- **Tunkheleg Stream.** By this stream, often mentioned in "The Secret History of the Mongols", Queen Alan Gua is said to have met Dobun Mergen. Bodonchar the Holy is said to have spoken his famous saying here, "It is good when a body has a head and a robe has a collar", and the last queens of Mongolia's last Khan, Dondogdulam and Genenpil, lived in this area. It is 4 km west of Khajuu Spring. (N49°02’50.82 E111°38’28.69)
- **Dobun Mergen's monument.** A very ancient monument made of several standing stones. Locals have long called it the monument and grave of Duwa Sokhor and Dobun Mergen. It is 1.5 km northwest of Tunkheleg Stream. (N49°03’17.18 E111°33’09.11)
- **Bodonchar's grass hut.** Paragraph 24 of "The Secret History of the Mongols" tells how Bodonchar the Simple, founder of the Kiyad Borjigin clan of Chinggis Khaan's Golden Lineage, lived in a grass hut on Balj Island, which is this place. He was the first to say the wise words "A person needs an elder, a robe needs a collar." It is 1.5 km northwest of the khirigsuur (burial mound) of Dobun Mergen and Duwa Sokhor. (N49°03’31.5 E111°31’07.0)
- **Chinggis Khaan's Khar Tug Ovoo.** It is said that before setting out on campaigns, Chinggis Khaan raised his great black banner on top of this mountain. (N49°02’11.4 E111°35’54.50)
- **Tamga Stone.** According to "Altan Tobchi" (the Golden Summary), when a small bird sang "Chinggis, Chinggis" on a large black rock on the mountain, the rock split open and the swastika seal of the Mongol state came out of it. The split rock is still called "Chinggis's Seal Stone". (N49°06’17.63 E111°39’18.12)
- **Santkhaan Mountain.** A sacred mountain north of the meeting of the Onon and Agats rivers, with the "Santkhaan Ovoo" on top. Legend says two male foals were born here to a gray mare of Chinggis Khaan's horse herd. Foals born here are said to become fast racehorses, so before training horses for Naadam people worship the Santkhaan Ovoo and make offerings. Herders still graze their horses and let them foal on this mountain. (N49°12’40.9 E111°50’28.9)
- **Degen Jigen (Zeeren Gorge).** A historic place recorded as the Zeeren Gorge of the Onon River in paragraph 129 of "The Secret History of the Mongols". (N49°14’23.0 E112°01’13.3)
- **Burkhan Khaldun – Bayankhaan Mountain.** Locals consider Bayankhaan Mountain, with its three peaks Burkhant, Shurgadai and Orgoit, to be Burkhan Khaldun Mountain, where Chinggis Khaan hid from the Merkits, survived, and vowed to worship it "for generations to come". The forested and rocky back slopes are rich in fruit trees and home to rare animals such as moose, red deer, bears and musk deer, as well as many kinds of birds. (N49°01’11.00 E111°18’46.08)

### Museums and religious sites

- **Temujin's Childhood Museum.** Opened in 2006 for the 800th anniversary of the founding of the Mongol state. Exhibits include stone, bronze and iron artifacts of ancient people of the Onon and Balj basins, straw collages showing Temujin's childhood, a seated figure of Chinggis Khaan, the black banner, and a palace tent on a cart. (N49°01’12.86 E111°37’26.81)
- **Local History Museum.** First founded in 1959 on the initiative of the sum's young herders to share their experience. It has expanded its collection since 1971 and now holds 346 historical exhibits. (N49°01’12.80 E111°37’30.03)
- **Hunter Baavai's Museum.** A ger museum with the lifelong collection of works and stuffed animals of D. Zunduidagva, a local veteran, hunter and holder of the Order of the Red Banner of Military Valor. It also has historical exhibits on Buryat customs. It is 1 km southwest of the site of Queen Genenpil's ger. (N49°01’53.98 E111°33’10.37)
- **Site of Queen Genenpil's ger.** Land of the Kiyad Borjigin Golden Lineage, famous as the home of the keepers of the Mongol state's sacred white horse herd. Genenpil, the last queen of the Eighth Bogd Khan, who held both religious and state power, spent her summers on this terrace until she was arrested on false political charges. It is 2 km southeast of Bodonchar's grass hut. (N49°02’16.04 E111°33’12.71)
- **Three Stupas of the Lineage.** Stupas topped with the three lineage deities Avalokiteshvara, Manjushri and Vajrapani. They were built to increase merit and cleanse sins, in memory of ancestors who died before their time in the political repressions of the 1930s. (N49°01’05.18 E111°36’45.09)
- **Ruins of Gilber Monastery.** The remains of a monastery that was a major center of Gelug Buddhist culture with about 300 monks from 1700 to 1937. It is said that on September 20, 1920, the Russian general Baron Ungern passed by this monastery with his Asiatic Cavalry Division as he came down the Gilber River into Bogd Khan's Mongolia. (N49°11’24.46 E111°24’09.07)

### Nature, mineral springs and caves

- **Gurvannuur (Three Lakes).** Three small lakes close together on a flat area inside a pine forest, famous as the "Gurvannuur mineral springs". The lake water and mud are used to treat stomach, liver, gallbladder and eczema problems. Middle Lake has leeches and is said to help joint diseases, while the water of East Lake is drunk for internal and bowel conditions. Read about the sanatorium here on the [Gurvannuur Spa Sanatorium page](</things-to-do/wellness/gurvannuuryn-rashaan-suvilal>). (N49°01’54.94 E111°39’15.38)
- **Tsagaanchuluut.** A place that could be called a natural physiotherapy complex, believed to have the power to heal all kinds of illness by traditional and natural methods. (N49°05’23.70 E111°03’19.80)
- **Nukht Cave of Suugt.** Hidden from view on a short back slope of a rocky mountain on the east fork of the Jivkheestei River, on the west side of the valley of the Suugt River, a tributary of the Galttai River. It is a vertical, well-like cave 72 m deep. (N49°10’20.78 E111°58’35.61)

Tes sum lies in the north of Zavkhan Province (aimag), between the Tannu-Ola Range and the branch mountains of the Khan Khukhii Range. It once had a large state farm that supplied flour and fodder to the western provinces, and today it is an interesting place for its mountains, rivers, forests, many kinds of wildlife and birds, and many monuments that have not yet been studied.

## Where is Tes sum?

The sum lies more than 1,300 meters above sea level. It borders [Bayantes](</province/zavkhan/bayantes>) and [Bayankhairkhan](/province/zavkhan/bayankhairkhan) sums of Zavkhan Province, Zuunkhangai sum of Uvs Province, and the Republic of Tuva in Russia. Its total area is 87,229 hectares. The sum center is at a place called Zur, 301 km from the provincial center. For other sums and sights of the province, read the [Zavkhan Province page](/province/zavkhan).

## How many people live in Tes and what are its bags?

Tes sum has four bags: Doono, Niseg, Bayan-Uul and Zur. The sum has 3,386 people in 894 households and 49,200 head of livestock.

## How did Tes become a sum?

The administrative history of this area begins in the 17th century. In 1655 the banner (khoshuu) of Erdeneduuregch Van of Zasagt Khan aimag, one of the Khotogoid banners, was founded here. Under Qing rule it was called the left-wing banner of the middle left of the western road of Khalkha. From 1924 to 1931 it was Delgerkhan Uul banner of Khantaishir Uul aimag, and from 1931 to 1959 it was part of Bayan-Uul sum. The present sum dates back to 1958. That year the people of Doono, Bayan-Uul, Berkh and Niseg bags of Bayan-Uul sum proposed a change, saying their land was too far from the sum center and did not fit well. After the proposal was checked on the ground, a resolution of the Presidium of the People's Great Khural of the Mongolian People's Republic on May 31, 1959, separated the land and people of these bags into a new sum named Tes, based on the "Delgerekh" cooperative. In this way a new sum was created in 1959 on the site of the Tes fodder farm of Bayan-Uul sum. At first the new sum had 341 households, 1,507 people, 52,400 head of livestock and 2,298 hectares of land. Soon after, as part of the nationwide virgin lands movement, a state farm was founded in Tes sum in 1969. But because livestock pastures and crop fields were considered a poor fit side by side, in 1974 the "Delgerekh" cooperative of Tes sum was dissolved and merged with the "Jargalant-Amidral" cooperative of Bayan-Uul sum into the "Delgerekh-Amidral" cooperative under Bayan-Uul sum. The status of Tes sum was then cancelled, and it was renamed the Tes State Farm of Bayantes sum, or Tes Committee, with Ch. Yadamjav appointed head of both the committee and the state farm. At that time Tes had 205 households, more than 700 people and 23,000 hectares of crop rotation land. This did not last long, however. Decree No. 138 of the Presidium of the People's Great Khural of the Mongolian People's Republic in 1977 made the Tes Committee a sum again. In the 1990s Doono and Niseg bags of Bayantes sum, which had once belonged to the old Tes sum, moved back to Tes sum, and the sum took on its present borders.

## How was the "Delgerekh" cooperative founded?

The "Delgerekh" cooperative, which worked on the land of Tes, was founded in 1956 on the initiative of G. Ish, G. Ichinnorov, I. Damdinbazar and L. Magsar. It began with 23 members from 17 households of bags I, II, III and IX of Bayan-Uul sum pooling more than 2,000 head of livestock, and I. Damdinbazar was elected its first chairman. Later the cooperative grew to three livestock brigades, more than 500 members, fixed assets of MNT 3 million and an annual income of MNT 1 million. By 1972 it had more than 2,500 members in more than 500 households, 67,700 head of livestock, fixed assets of MNT 4 million and an annual income of MNT 2 million. When the sum was dissolved in 1974 because of the Tes state farm, the cooperative was merged into the "Jargalant-Amidral" cooperative of Bayan-Uul sum.

## What was the Tes state farm?

As a result of the virgin lands movement of the 1960s, and under Resolution No. 157 of the Council of Ministers of the Mongolian People's Republic in 1969, construction of the Tes state farm began with help from the Soviet Union. At that time the livestock machinery station of Bayan-Uul sum was closed, and more than 20 machines worth MNT 2 million in total, including 9 tracked and wheeled tractors, farm trailers and dump trucks, were transferred to the Tes state farm. In 1969 the first plowing was done at Tevkhen, Tsagaanchuluut and Tuunt, 3,056 hectares were cultivated and 3,100 tons of hay were made, and production began. In 1972 the Soviet side handed over the farm, with fixed assets of MNT 35 million. It included the following facilities:

- a fodder plant able to produce 10,000 tons of fodder a year;
- a farm machinery repair shop able to service 120 tractors;
- a garage for about 70 vehicles;
- a 900 kW power station;
- a steam boiler producing 10,000 Gcal of heat a year;
- a 35-seat workers' canteen, a bakery and a bathhouse;
- apartments for 50 families.

At first the Tes state farm had no livestock at all and only grew crops. In 1973 it received 200 cattle and 220 horses from the Baruunturuun state farm in Uvs Province, and in 1975 more than 8,000 sheep and goats, 37 herders and 13 winter camps from Bayantes sum. Soon it also ran side businesses raising pigs, chickens and rabbits. The farm combined crop growing, livestock and fodder production: 88.6 percent of its total output came from crops, 5.2 percent from livestock and 6.2 percent from fodder. In 1987 the Tes state farm had 2,900 people in 529 households and 32,000 hectares of crop rotation land. Each year it sowed grain on 19,500 hectares and harvested 19,000–28,000 tons. This harvest not only met the region's flour needs but also supplied wheat to the flour mills of Ulaangom and Murun. Each year it also prepared about 10,000 tons of compound livestock feed and delivered it to the five western provinces. This farming came to a stop during the hard years of the market transition in the 1990s.

## What are the climate and soil like?

Tes sum has a harsh continental climate. Summer temperatures average 23–32 °C, and winters drop to −35 to −42 °C. Annual precipitation is 180–210 mm. The soils are fertile dark brown and sandy soils.

## What nature will I find: mountains, rivers, plants and animals?

**Mountains.** Ikh Berkh, Baga Berkh, Baruun Doono, Zuun Doono, Khalban, Zur, Zos, Dormon, Nart, Navkhan, Ongon, Kholboo, Talyn Uvuljin, Aambagaa, Jaambagaa, Arts Suuri, Argalant and Suman Rock. **Rivers and streams.** Tes, Zaigal, Tsereg, Mogoi and Khatguurantai. You can read more about the Tes River on the [Tes River Area page](</destination/protected/tesiin-gol-orchmyn-nutag>). **Plants.** Plants include burnet, yellow sweetvetch, wild garlic, lily, wild onion, mangir onion, wild chives, peony and tagsh. Berries and nuts are plentiful: pine nuts, sea buckthorn, crowberry, currant, wild raspberry and bird cherry. Trees and shrubs include Siberian pine, larch, aspen, spruce, willow, dog rose, cinquefoil and ephedra. **Wildlife.** Red deer, wild boar, roe deer, badger, jirkh, mountain hare, hare, wolf, lynx, fox, corsac fox, Pallas's cat, marmot, squirrel, ermine, Siberian weasel, steppe polecat, nokhoi zeekh and steppe marten live here. **Birds.** Black grouse, hazel grouse, raven, golden eagle, vulture, magpie, turliakh, hawk, sar, owl, capercaillie, lark, hoopoe, Daurian partridge, cuckoo, swan, goose, ruddy shelduck, crane and bustard are seen here.

## What is there to see in Tes sum?

The sum has natural and historical sites. The best known are **Suman Rock**, **Dormon Mountain**, **Khuulga Cave** and the **Zur Turkic inscription**. Besides these, dozens of monuments here have not yet been studied.

Khanbogd sum is in Umnugovi Province (aimag), on the border with China, and is famous for the Galbyn Gobi, the monasteries founded by Noyon Khutagt Danzanravjaa, and the Oyu Tolgoi mine. In this land, where steppe meets the Gobi desert, you will find striking natural formations and historical and religious monuments in one place.

## Where is Khanbogd sum and how far is it?

The sum is 260 km from the provincial center and borders China to the south. It covers 1,515.2 km² and has 4 administrative bags. Six public institutions work in the sum, with more than 100 civil servants. For other sums and sights of the province, read the [Umnugovi Province page](/province/umnugov). The sum sees a lot of traffic. The Gashuun Sukhait border crossing here is open year-round. The main paved road linking Oyu Tolgoi and the Gashuun Sukhait crossing with Ulaanbaatar passes the junction of four roads to the centers of Umnugovi, Dornogovi and Govisumber provinces.

## Why was Galba renamed Khanbogd?

The sum was founded in 1924 under the name "Galba" at a place called the Khar Ovoo camp of Khadankhoshuu. In 1941 it took the name of its Khanbogd Mountain and became Khanbogd sum. A marker has been placed next to the three monasteries of Galba to record that the sum was founded under the name Galba in 1924.

## What lies underground, and what is Oyu Tolgoi?

The land of Khanbogd holds rich reserves of gold and copper. Rare finds such as the remains of giant lizards and reptiles of the early geological periods, and petrified wood, are also found here. Oyu Tolgoi, counted among the world's large copper and gold mines, operates 40 km west of the sum. It is a giant project that brings huge benefits to Mongolia's economy, and its concentrator plant opened on December 27, 2017.

## What wildlife and birds live here?

The Galbyn Gobi once had large herds of steppe hoofed animals such as the Mongolian wild ass (khulan) and the goitered gazelle. But as mining roads and traffic increased, they moved away to Dornogovi Province. Even so, the Houbara bustard (Chlamydotis undulata), rare worldwide, still lives in this area. The Mongolian ground jay (Podoces hendersoni) and the saxaul sparrow (Passer ammodendri) are also common. In recent years a large pond formed from surplus water from the Oyu Tolgoi plant has become a key stopover for migrating waterbirds. Argali and Siberian ibex are often seen on Khanbogd Mountain, the sum's main sacred mountain. Meeting goitered gazelles and khulan along the way lifts the spirit of any trip.

## What is there to see in Khanbogd sum?

**Natural formations.** The sum is home to the Undai River, the Moving Cliff, Frog Rock, Ikh Bulag and Baga Bulag springs, the Baishin Gorge, Chinggis's Uurga Hole, the Cave of Three Chestnut Horses, the 3,000 elms, the Sevkhuul sands, the Khaalga mineral spring, and the [Galbyn Gobi](</destination/landscapes/galbyn-govi>), one of the Nine Wonders of Umnugovi. **Monasteries of the Galbyn Gobi.** Twelve kilometers southwest of the sum, in the Galbyn Gobi celebrated in songs and poems, lie the ruins of three famous monasteries founded by Noyon Khutagt Danzanravjaa. Nearby are other interesting natural and historical sites: the ruins of Tsogzol Monastery, the Altangerel wheel, the Chavga Cave of Lovonchomboo, which is said to bring wealth, the Stomach and Eye mineral springs, and the liver and gallbladder stones. Demchog Monastery was rebuilt with funding from the Umnugovi company Ajnai, opening new opportunities for domestic travel. The "World Energy Center" tourist ger camp was built about 6 km northwest of the monastery. Read more about the monasteries on the [Three Monasteries of Galba page](</destination/heritage/place/galbyn-gurvan-khiid>).

![image](asset:tic-img-8b5e1c8a865da194)

## How can I spend a day around Demchog Monastery?

Most people think of Demchog Monastery, known as the "World Energy Center", only as a place to receive energy, with the Wealth Cave, the Mother's Womb and Frog Rock. But there are many interesting places there that few people know or visit. Ingot Rock, the Khutagt's meditation seat, Chinggis's Uurga Hole, Dombo Cliff and the Stone Workshop all have their own stories and legends, and it takes a whole day to see them all. **Ereet Monastery.** Ereet Monastery, the second of the three monasteries of Galba, where more than 300 lamas once held services, is 3 km north of Demchog Monastery (the first monastery). Its ruins look much like those of Ongi Monastery. On the east side of the ruins, a figure of the deity Red Jamsran appears naturally on a rock. Southeast of the monastery lies a rock shaped like a woman lying on her back. This rock is said to face the Erdene Rock (Boovon Rock) of Demchog Monastery, and men come here to pray for a partner and for children. **Tsagaan Tolgoi Monastery.** Three kilometers east of Ereet Monastery you can see the ruins of Tsagaan Tolgoi Monastery, the third monastery of Galba. People say there is a reason why the three monasteries stand in the shape of a pyramid. The surrounding landscape is beautiful, with water, springs and gorges. **Monuments to the southeast.** Heading southeast from the sum, you can see the ruins of Tsogzol Monastery and the oddly shaped Moving Cliff. There is also a figure of the deity Yulkha, created by the Khutagt, showing a boy riding a red goat, a protector against all evil.

## How do I get there?

There are two roads from the center of Khanbogd sum to Demchog Monastery. The direct road is only 12 km, but it is a rocky, rough mountain track. The other is a straight dirt road of about 40 km known as the Oyu Tolgoi road. Both take exactly the same time and fuel, so if you do not know the mountain road, it is better to take the Oyu Tolgoi road. If you want a longer trip, you can travel 150 km to Khanbogd on the route "Uush dunes – Morin Tolgoi Gorge – Uekhii – Sevkhuul – Mogoit – Khoyor Ulziit Mountain – Khanbogd Mountain – Bayangol – Amtgai – Argalant – Suikhent – Ulgii Monastery – the three monasteries of Galba".

## Where can I stay?

Khanbogd is a large, comfortable sum with plenty of lights and buildings. Compared with other sums in Mongolia, it has many comfortable hotels. In the sum center, quite a few hotels and restaurants are connected to water and sewage lines. There is potential to develop border-area tourism in the future.

## What livestock is Khanbogd known for?

Since 2006 Khanbogd has led the country year after year with its Galba red camels. It also raises select breeds such as Gaviluud sheep with an extra vertebra and Gobi Gurvan Saikhan goats with fine cashmere.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Khanbogd sum from the provincial center?

It is 260 km from the center of Umnugovi Province. Oyu Tolgoi is 40 km west of the sum, and the monasteries of the Galbyn Gobi are 12 km southwest.

### Which road should I take to Demchog Monastery?

From the sum center there is a direct 12 km rough mountain track, or the Oyu Tolgoi dirt road of about 40 km. Both need the same time and fuel, so if you do not know the way, choose the Oyu Tolgoi road.

### What rare birds live in Khanbogd sum?

The Houbara bustard, rare worldwide, lives here, as well as the Mongolian ground jay and the saxaul sparrow. Migrating waterbirds stop at the pond formed from Oyu Tolgoi's surplus water.

Bayantumen sum lies just 12 km from Choibalsan, the center of Dornod Province (aimag), and grew up supplying the city with food, milk and vegetables. The sum center is the settlement of Tsagaan Ders. Many locals call this sum "Khan Uul".

## Where is Bayantumen sum?

The sum center, the settlement of Tsagaan Ders, is 645 km from Ulaanbaatar and 12 km from [Choibalsan (Kherlen sum)](/province/dornod/kherlen). The sum gets its electricity from Choibalsan's power grid. For other sums and sights of the province, read the [Dornod Province page](/province/dornod). The sum covers 832,080 hectares in total, of which 478,411 hectares are pasture, 33,523 hectares are hayfields and 324 hectares are cropland.

## How did the sum come about?

The sum grew out of the "Khudulmur" cooperative, founded in January 1950. It was started by S. Batmunkh, then head of the Choibalsan city administration, with seven people including O. Gansukh, D. Danzan, Tumur and Namst, in the city's 6th district. The cooperative's aim was to supply the provincial center with vegetables. In 1952 the cooperative moved from Choibalsan to a place called "Tsagaan Ders". In the 1940s this had been the vegetable fields and supply farm of the Soviet 17th Army, and the settlers moved into the few small buildings left behind. In 1958 the "Bayan" cooperative of Kherlenbayan sum merged with the "Khudulmur" cooperative. Then, by decision of the Presidium of the Little Khural, Bayantumen sum was founded in March 1959, and the cooperative was renamed "Bayasgalant Khudulmur". S. Batmunkh served as head of both the sum and the cooperative, and J. Ulziikhutag as secretary. In the year the sum was founded, a primary school (principal Yu. Sumiyaa), a clinic (physician assistant A. Jambaa), a kindergarten (head B. Nadmid) and a trade and procurement unit (head Ishjamts) opened. In 1960 a state artificial insemination station and a veterinary branch were set up.

## Why is it called a suburban farming sum?

Bayantumen developed as a farming sum serving the nearby city. Between 1974 and 1990, large farm facilities were built here, including a mechanized dairy farm for 400 cows and a 1,000-hectare irrigation system. The sum grew silage and green fodder on 500–1,000 hectares and supplied one million liters of milk a year to Choibalsan's food plant. It also grew grain, potatoes and vegetables on 1,000–1,500 hectares. The population and livestock grew year by year, and in 1990 the cooperative's fixed assets reached MNT 41.4 million. At the exchange rate of the time, it earned MNT 7.8 million a year. During the transition to a market economy, the cooperative, some institutions, and the livestock and property of the dairy farm passed to companies and individuals.

## What are the nature and climate like?

The sum is mostly flat, with low mountains such as Galyn Shil (893 m), Mankhtai (856 m) and Bor Undur (850 m). The soils are brown steppe soils and saline soils. The Kherlen River and small lakes such as Tsaidam and Khotont are in the sum. The climate is continental. January temperatures average −22 to −24 °C, and July temperatures +20 to +22 °C. Average annual precipitation is 250 mm. Wildlife includes the Mongolian gazelle, wolf, fox, corsac fox and Pallas's cat. The dense steppe vegetation is dominated by feather grass, wild rye, wild onion and tana onion. Locals worship mountains and ovoos (sacred stone cairns) such as Enkh, Munkh, Khutag and Khan Uul.

## How many people and livestock does the sum have?

Bayantumen sum has 2,100 people and 3 administrative bags. It has 95,000 head of livestock in total, including 670 camels, 15,400 horses, 9,800 cattle, 43,000 sheep and 26,700 goats.

Songinokhairkhan District lies in the western part of Ulaanbaatar and takes its name from the sacred Songinokhairkhan Mountain that towers southwest of the city. The district is home to many places with interesting history and legends: this mountain, said to be the fiercest of the four sacred mountains around the old capital Khuree, the ruins of an ancient town in the Tuul River valley, khirigsuurs (burial mounds) and graves, and the birthplace of the Second Bogd.

![image](asset:tic-img-7ce78920dee40bdb)

## When was Songinokhairkhan District founded?

The capital's Songinokhairkhan District was founded on October 1, 1992, by Resolution No. 18 of 1992 of the State Great Khural of Mongolia. The district lies on the western wing of the capital and borders [Bayangol District](/province/ulaanbaatar#bayangol) and Altanbulag, Bayanchandmani, Bornuur and Batsumber sums of Tuv Province. It covers 120.1 hectares. In 2016 it had 315,802 residents and 73,576 head of livestock. For the capital's other districts, read the [Ulaanbaatar page](/province/ulaanbaatar).

## How did Songinokhairkhan Mountain get its name, and what are its legends?

Songinokhairkhan is a sacred mountain of rocks and scree, with elm and peashrub growing on it. Its three sharp peaks look majestic from afar. Long ago, wild red onions (songino) grew thickly on the blue scree of its slopes, so it was called "Songinot Khairkhan" (Onion Sacred Mountain), which later became Songinokhairkhan. Legend says a fiery black fox lives on this mountain, so snow never settles and the winters stay snowless. Old stories tell of the few people who have seen a small jet-black fox with a pure white tail tip. Not everyone can see that fox, and people avoid even thinking of frightening it, let alone killing it. According to the learned lama Luvsanperenleijamts, Songinokhairkhan Mountain has a guardian spirit: a blue old man riding a blue ox, with crossed fangs and a love of strong liquor. This guardian is very fierce, is said to grant hard tasks quickly when asked, and sometimes has the power to appear as a military lord. For this reason, since ancient times people setting out for the army, war or other hard tasks have offered liquor and the first portions of food to the mountain's spirit, believing that if they entrust themselves to him and pray, they will be saved from harm. Because it is said to be the fiercest of the four mountains around Khuree, people did not touch its animals, not even its marmots and squirrels.

## What wildlife and birds live here?

Marmots, squirrels, foxes, corsac foxes, Pallas's cats, lynx, hares and wolves live in the branch mountains of Songinokhairkhan. In harsh dzud winters, red deer, roe deer, argali and Siberian ibex sometimes migrate here. Birds once included black grouse, Daurian partridges, capercaillie, owls, ravens, magpies, golden eagles and vultures.

## What is the history of the Tuul River valley?

**Ancient finds.** The historian Dr. Navaan found arrowheads, whistling buttons and a belt with gold ornaments in a grave on the south side of the mountain. **The willow-herd people.** Along the Tuul River, which flows at the southern foot of the mountain, lived a group of families known as the "willow-herd people". They made and sold ger lattice walls, frames, crossbars, roof-pole tips and nose pegs for camels, khainag and yaks from willow. **Birthplace of the Second Bogd.** The Second Bogd was born at a bend of Songinokhairkhan called Uguumuriin Shar Bulan, while Darkhan Chin Van Dondovdorj lived there. People used to sing: "Flowers of every color bloom at the yellow bend of Uguumur; our old Khutagt was born in the palace of a noble high lineage." After the Khutagt's birth, Urtnasan Gavj had two small temples built at a place called Bayart Tolgoi on the northern terrace of the Tuul, and eight finely carved stone stupas made. In 1950 the lama Gevsh Danigai brought these eight ruined stupas and placed them around the white stupa of [Gandantegchinlen Monastery](</destination/heritage/place/gandantegchenlin-khiid>). **Mongolia's first veterinary medicine factory.** In 1915, in the time of Bogd Khan's Mongolia, a 25-year contract was signed at a place called Morin Uul Gatsaa with private veterinarians from Tsarist Russia. Under the contract they were to make medicines to keep the herds of Tusheet Khan, Zasagt Khan and Tsetsen Khan aimags healthy, and so the first private Russian veterinary medicine factory was founded in Mongolia. The factory's ruins lie on a small rise at the edge of the Tuul River, north of the Biokombinat bridge. **Ruins of Wang Khan's Lower Capital.** Long ago, crops and vegetables were grown in the basins of the Bukhug and Turgen rivers. A little above the meeting of these two rivers, near the western end of Songinokhairkhan Mountain, are the ruins of an old town on a terrace of the Tuul River. They are thought to be the ruins of the Lower Capital in the Black Forest of the Tuul, where Toghrul Wang Khan of the Kereit spent his summers, mentioned several times in "The Secret History of the Mongols". **Khirigsuurs and noble tombs.** There are many ancient khirigsuurs and graves at the upper valley of the western end of Songinokhairkhan Mountain, at the northeastern spring of Burkhant Mountain, and on the slopes of Dadar and Sevleg mountains beside Bulgat Khairkhan. Noble tombs from the 12th–13th centuries have been found on Dadar Mountain. **Mongolia's first airfield.** From 1938, Mongolia's first bomber repair shop, airfield, military unit, factories and service facilities began to be built in the Tuul River basin and the valley of the Bukhug and Turgen rivers.
