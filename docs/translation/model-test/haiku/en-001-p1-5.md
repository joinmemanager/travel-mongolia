Khanbogd sum

Khanbogd sum, Umnugovi: Galbyn Gobi, Demchig Monastery

Khanbogd sum is a large, well-developed sum in Umnugovi Province. It borders China to the south and is known for the Galbyn Gobi, the monasteries founded by Noyon Khutagt Danzanravjaa, and the Oyu Tolgoi mine. Steppe and desert landscapes come together here, along with natural wonders, history, and religious heritage.

## Where is Khanbogd sum and how far is it?

The sum is 260 km from the provincial center. Its southern side borders China. The area covers 1,515.2 km². Khanbogd has 4 bags. The sum has 6 budget-funded organizations, which employ more than 100 public servants. For other sums and sights in the province, read the [Umnugovi province page](/province/umnugov). Traffic in the sum is busy. The Gashuun Sukhait border crossing operates all year. The main paved road from Ulaanbaatar to Oyu Tolgoi and the Gashuun Sukhait crossing passes through a four-way junction of the Umnugovi, Dornogovi, and Govisumber provincial centers.

## How did the name change from Galba to Khanbogd?

The sum was founded in 1924 as "Galba," at a place called Khar Ovoo Buuts in Khadankhoshuu. In 1941, it took the name of its mountain, Khanbogd, and became Khanbogd sum. Next to the three Galbyn monasteries, a marker was built to commemorate the founding of the sum as Galba in 1924.

## What natural resources are there, and what is Oyu Tolgoi?

The ground beneath Khanbogd holds rich gold and copper reserves. The area also yields rare finds from the early Quaternary period, including giant lizard and reptile remains and petrified wood. About 40 km west of the sum is Oyu Tolgoi, one of the largest copper and gold mines in the world. It is a major project that brings great economic benefits to Mongolia. Its processing plant began operating on December 27, 2017.

## What wildlife and birds can you see?

The Galbyn Gobi once had large herds of khulan and black-tailed gazelles. However, mining roads, traffic, and human activity pushed them into Dornogovi Province. Even so, the Houbara Bustard (Chlamudotis undulata), a bird considered rare in the world, still lives here. The Mongolian ground jay (Podoces hendersoni) and the saxaul sparrow (Passer ammodendri) are also common. In recent years, a large pond formed from excess water at the Oyu Tolgoi plant has become a key stopover for migratory waterbirds. Argali and Siberian ibex are often spotted on Khanbogd Mountain, the locals' most revered mountain. Spotting black-tailed gazelles and khulan along the way can lift your spirits.

## What is there to see in Khanbogd sum?

**Natural sites.** The sum has Undain River, Khuduldug Tsokhio, Melkhii Rock, Ikh Bulag, Baga Bulag, Baishin Tsav, Chinggisiin Uurgan hole, Gurvan Zeerdiin Cave, 3000 Khailaast, Sevkhuuliin Sand Dunes, Khaalgany mineral spring, and Galbyn Gobi, one of the Nine Wonders of Umnugovi. The Galbyn Gobi is linked to Khanbogd sum.

**Galbyn Gobi monasteries.** About 12 km southwest of the sum lies the Galbyn Gobi, celebrated in songs and poems. Here are the ruins of three famous monasteries founded by Noyon Khutagt Danzanravjaa. Nearby, you can see the ruins of Tsogzol Monastery, Altangerel's Circle, the Chavga Cave, known for its wealth-giving qualities, and the mineral springs for stomach and eye ailments. There is also a gallstone rock. Each site has its own natural and historical interest.

Demchig Monastery was rebuilt with funding from the Ajnai Corporation of Umnugovi. This opened new opportunities for domestic travel. About 6 km northwest of the monastery is the "World Energy Center" tourist ger camp. To learn more about the monasteries, read the [Galbyn Gurvan Khiid page](</destination/heritage/place/galbyn-gurvan-khiid>).

## What can you do on a one-day trip around Demchig Monastery?

Many people know Demchig Monastery, known as the "World Energy Center," as a place to recharge. They also associate it with Bayajikhiin Cave, Ekhiin Umai, and Melkhii Rock. However, there are many interesting places nearby that few people know or visit. Yembuu Rock, Khutagt's meditation seat, Chinggisiin Uurgan hole, Dombon Tsokhio, and the stone workshop each have their own history and legends. Visiting them all takes a full day.

**Eeret Monastery.** The second of the three Galbyn monasteries, Eeret Monastery once hosted more than 300 lamas. It is 3 km from Demchig Monastery (the first monastery). Its ruins resemble those of Ongiin Monastery. East of the ruins, on a cliff, is a naturally formed image of Ulaan Jamsran. Southeast of the monastery, a rock shaped like a woman lies face up. Across from it is the Erdene Rock (Boovon Rock) of Demchig Monastery. Men are said to come here to pray for their marriages and their children's futures.

**Tsagaan Tolgoi Monastery.** Go 3 km east of Eeret Monastery to see the ruins of Tsagaan Tolgoi Monastery, the third of the Galbyn monasteries. The three monasteries are said to be arranged in a pyramid shape for a reason. The surrounding landscape has water, springs, and canyons, and it is beautiful.

**Southeast sites.** Going southeast from the sum, you can visit the ruins of Tsogzol Monastery and the unusual Khuduldug Tsokhio. There is also a statue of Yulkha, a protector deity, made by Khutagt. It shows a boy riding a red goat, and it protects against all misfortune.

## How do I get to Khanbogd sum?

You can travel from the Khanbogd sum center to Demchig Monastery by two routes. The direct road is only 12 km, but it is a rocky, rough mountain road. The other route is a straight white road of about 40 km, known as the Oyu Tolgoi road. Both take the same time and fuel, so if you are not familiar with the mountain road, take the Oyu Tolgoi road. For a longer trip, you can travel about 150 km to Khanbogd by following this route: Öösh Manhan – Morin Tolgoin Tsav – Öekhii – Sevkhuul – Mogoit – Khoyor Elzii Uul – Khanbogd Mountain – Bayangol – Amtgai – Argalant – Suikhent – Ölgii Monastery – Galbyn Gurvan Khiid.

## Where can I stay in Khanbogd sum?

Khanbogd is a large, comfortable sum with plenty of lights and buildings. Compared with other sums in Mongolia, it has many hotels, and they are comfortable. Several hotels and restaurants connected to clean and wastewater lines operate in the sum center. The sum also has potential for developing tourism near the border.

## How is livestock farming in Khanbogd?

Since 2006, Khanbogd has led the country in Galbyn red camels for several years in a row. The sum also raises specialized breeds, including sheep with extra vertebrae from the Gavilud clan and cashmere goats of the Gobi Gurvan Saikhan breed.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Khanbogd sum from the provincial center?

It is 260 km from the Umnugovi provincial center. Oyu Tolgoi is 40 km west of the sum, and the Galbyn Gobi monasteries are 12 km to the southwest.

### Which road should I take to Demchig Monastery?

From the sum center, you can take the direct 12 km rocky mountain road or the 40-km-long white road via Oyu Tolgoi. Both take the same time and fuel, so if you don't know the mountain road, choose the Oyu Tolgoi road.

### Which rare birds live in Khanbogd sum?

The Houbara Bustard, a bird considered rare in the world, lives here, along with the Mongolian ground jay and the saxaul sparrow. Migratory waterbirds also visit the pond formed from excess water at Oyu Tolgoi.

Bayantumen sum

Bayantumen sum, Dornod: 12 km from Choibalsan

Bayantumen sum lies 12 km from Choibalsan in Dornod Province, with its center at Tsagaan Deres. Explore its history, nature, population and livestock.

Bayantumen sum lies on the edge of Choibalsan, the center of Dornod Province (aimag), just 12 km away. The sum grew up around farming, supplying food, milk and vegetables to the city. The sum center is the settlement of Tsagaan Deres. Many local people call this sum "Khan Uul."

## Where is Bayantumen sum?

The sum center, Tsagaan Deres, is 645 km from Ulaanbaatar and 12 km from [Choibalsan city (Kherlen sum)](/province/dornod/kherlen). The sum gets its electricity from the Choibalsan power system. For other sums and places to see, read the [Dornod Province page](/province/dornod). The sum's total area is 832,080 hectares. Of this, 478,411 hectares are pasture, 33,523 hectares are hayfields and 324 hectares are farmland.

## How was Bayantumen sum founded?

The sum traces its origins to the Khudulmur herders' cooperative (negdel), founded in January 1950. At that time, S. Batmunkh, head of the Choibalsan city administration, led a group of seven people, including O. Gansukh, D. Danzan, Tumur and Namst, in setting up the cooperative in the territory of the city's 6th khoroo (sub-district). Its goal was to supply the provincial center with vegetables. In 1952, the cooperative moved from Choibalsan to a place called Tsagaan Deres. In the 1940s, the Soviet 17th Army had run a vegetable field and auxiliary farm center there, and the cooperative settled in the few small houses that remained. In 1958, the Bayan cooperative from Kherlenbayan sum merged with Khudulmur. Then, by decision of the Presidium of the State Little Khural, Bayantumen sum was established in March 1959. The cooperative was renamed Bayasgalant Khudulmur. S. Batmunkh served as head of both the sum and the cooperative, and J. Ulziikhutag as secretary. That same year, a primary school (headed by Yu. Sumyaa), a human health clinic (junior doctor A. Jambaa), a kindergarten (managed by B. Nadmid) and a trade and procurement unit (headed by Ishjamts) opened. In 1960, a state artificial insemination station and a veterinary branch were set up.

## How did farming develop around the city?

Bayantumen developed as a farming area serving the city. Between 1974 and 1990, major agricultural facilities were built here, including a mechanized dairy farm with 400 cows and a 1,000-hectare irrigation system. The sum grew fodder crops and green feed on 500–1,000 hectares and supplied Choibalsan's food factory with one million liters of milk each year. It also grew grain, potatoes and vegetables on 1,000–1,500 hectares. As the population and livestock grew year by year, the cooperative's core assets reached MNT 41.4 million in 1990. At the prices of the time, it earned MNT 7.8 million in cash income each year. When the economy moved to a market system, the cooperative, some official organizations, the dairy farm's livestock and other property passed to businesses and private individuals.

## What are the landscape and climate like?

The sum's land is undulating and has low mountains, including Galyn Shil (893 m), Mankhtai (856 m) and Bor Undur (850 m). The soil is steppe brown soil and saline meadow soil. The sum has the Kherlen River and several lakes, including Tsaidam and Khotont. The climate is continental. The average temperature is −22 to −24 °C in January and +20 to +22 °C in July. The sum receives about 250 mm of precipitation a year. Its wildlife includes Mongolian gazelle, wolf, red fox, corsac fox and Pallas's cat. The forest-steppe vegetation is dense, with feather grass, khiagt, mangir and taana growing widely. Local people revere the mountains and ovoo (sacred stone cairn) such as Enkh, Munkh, Khutag and Khan Uul.

## How many people and how much livestock are there?

Bayantumen sum has 2,100 residents and 3 bags (the smallest administrative unit). The sum has 95,000 head of livestock in total, including 670 camels, 15,400 horses, 9,800 cattle, 43,000 sheep and 26,700 goats.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Bayantumen sum from Choibalsan?

The sum center, Tsagaan Deres, is 12 km from Choibalsan and 645 km from Ulaanbaatar.

### When was Bayantumen sum established?

The sum was established in March 1959, by decision of the Presidium of the State Little Khural. Its origins go back to the Khudulmur cooperative, founded in 1950.

Songinokhairkhan District lies in the western part of Ulaanbaatar. It takes its name from the sacred Songinokhairkhan Mountain, which rises to the southwest of the city. Within the district there are many places with history and legend: the mountain, known as the fiercest of the four mountains of Khuree (the old name of Ulaanbaatar); the ruins of an ancient city in the Tuul River valley; khirigsuur burial mounds; and the birthplace of the Second Bogd Khan.

## When was Songinokhairkhan District established?

Under the 18th resolution of 1992 of the State Great Khural, the capital's Songinokhairkhan District was established on October 1, 1992. The district lies in the western wing of the capital. It borders [Bayangol District](/province/ulaanbaatar#bayangol) and the Altanbulag, Bayanchandman, Bornuur, and Batsumber sums of Tuv Province (aimag). The district covers 120.1 hectares. As of 2016, it had a population of 315,802 and 73,576 head of livestock. To learn about the other districts of the capital, read the [Ulaanbaatar page](/province/ulaanbaatar).

## What is the name and legend of Songinokhairkhan Mountain?

Songinokhairkhan is a sacred mountain covered in rock scree, with larch and caragana growing on its slopes. Its three sharp peaks look impressive from afar. Long ago, wild red onions grew abundantly in the blue scree on the mountainside, so it was called Songinot Khairkhan (Onion Sacred Mountain). Later, it became Songinokhairkhan.

A legend says that a fiery black fox lives on this mountain, which is why snow does not settle here and winters stay bare. It is said that anyone who spots the small fox, with a pure white tail tip and a jet-black body, becomes fully alert. The fox is not visible to everyone, and people are forbidden even to think of killing it, let alone frightening it.
