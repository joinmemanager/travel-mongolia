Dadal sum is located in the far northeastern part of Khentii Province (aimag), in the Onon River basin. It is remembered in history and legend as the birthplace of Chinggis Khaan (Genghis Khan). The sum was founded in 1923. Today, visitors can see pine forests, clear lakes, sacred mountains, and many sites linked to the childhood legends of Chinggis Khaan in one area. You can also experience Buryat customs, traditional food, and handicrafts.

## Where is Dadal sum, and how far is it from Ulaanbaatar?

Dadal sum lies in the far northeast of Mongolia. It is 600 km from the capital, Ulaanbaatar, and 270 km from the provincial center, Undurkhaan. The roads are rough, so prepare carefully for a long journey. The sum borders Russia to the north. To the east, it borders Bayan-Uul sum in Dornod Province. To the south, it borders Norovlin sum in Khentii Province. To the southwest, it borders Bayan-Adarga sum, and to the west, Binder sum. For more information on other sums and sites in the province, see the [Khentii Province page](/province/khentii). Geographically, the sum covers the eastern edge of the Khentii Range, the Eeree Range, and the Onon River valley. The sum center is in Bayan-Ovoo bag (the smallest administrative unit). It sits at the foot of the scenic Bayan-Ovoo Mountain, near the legendary Deluun Boldog, and south of the Balj River.

## Who lives in Dadal sum, and how is it administered?

Dadal sum has four bags: Bayan-Ovoo, Agats, Onon, and Balj. In 2017, the sum had 890 households and 2,891 residents. Most residents are Buryat. Their everyday life, customs, food, and handicrafts are clearly visible throughout the sum.

## What is the nature and climate like in Dadal sum?

The sum sits 800 m above sea level. The lowest point is the floodplain of the Onon and Balj rivers. The highest point is Bugat Mountain (1,513 m), at a bend of the Balj River. Here, the southern edge of the Siberian taiga gives way to the Dauria steppe. Because of this border position, the area has many animal and plant species, but its nature is very fragile. The climate is humid and cool. Over the past 10 years, average precipitation has been 3.2 mm in winter, 12.2 mm in spring, 104.8 mm in summer, and 17.6 mm in autumn. Winter snow cover is usually about 40 cm thick. Rare animals include the white-naped crane, taimen, and Onon River grayling. Other animals found here include gray wolves, red foxes, corsac foxes, badgers, wild boars, and mountain hares. Among the plants, you may see the rare Dereveg-jirgeriu and white Tseene. The area is also rich in fruits, berries, and medicinal plants, such as Asian bird cherry, wild strawberry, dogrose, Daurian rhubarb, cowberry, wild mint, bilberry, and pine nuts. The sum's eco-tourism plans rely on the nearby [Onon-Balj National Park](</destination/protected/onon-balj>).

## What is Buryat culture like in Dadal, and what is Altargana Naadam?

Residents of Dadal sum continue to develop Buryat traditions in herding, handicrafts, and cultural heritage. Households have long used horse-drawn carts and wooden tubs. Craftspeople carve cups, ladles, spoons, and plates from wood and tree roots, and sell them as souvenirs. To support Buryat folk arts, the Altargana Buryat Song Naadam was first organized in this sum in 1994. It later grew into a major international festival. For the 20th anniversary of the Naadam, Dadal sum hosted more than 10,000 guests and delegates. Competitions were held in 13 arts and sports categories that showcased Buryat culture and traditions. At that event, A. Saikhantamir, a student at the Mongolian University of Science and Technology from Dadal, won a silver medal in the Dangina contest. In the Buryat song category, singer N. Ganzolboo won a silver medal for his performance of the song "Galttaimni."

## What local products can I buy in Dadal sum?

**Buryat bread.** This bread is famous across Mongolia. It is baked without yeast, which is unique to this area. It has won top prizes three times at the Khentii autumn festival and trade fair. **Cream, butter, and sour cream.** Selling milk and dairy products is the main income source for herders. Cream from Dadal residents was named the best at the Khentii trade fair twice. Butter made from milk is the main brand product of Buryat herders. Sour cream is made from churned cream mixed with sea buckthorn and other berries. "Dadal sea buckthorn sour cream" is well known in the sum and province. **Cattle herds.** Since 1964, the sum has worked to improve cattle breeding and now has Simmental cattle. Today, cattle make up 33.1 percent of the sum's total livestock.

## What is farming and employment like in Dadal sum?

The sum's farmland covers 7,119 hectares. Three businesses operate here. Dadal Buudai LLC and Enkh Nogoon Tal LLC provide seasonal jobs for 25–30 people and permanent jobs for 8 people. Potatoes and vegetables are grown on 25–30 hectares. Forty-six residents grow fruits and berries on 12 hectares to meet their household needs. The sum fully meets the vegetable needs of public institutions through local harvests. This work employs 20 people on a seasonal basis.

## What are Dadal sum's future goals?

The sum's overall development goal is to reduce unemployment and poverty in a real way and to improve residents' living standards. The sum aims to become a model community. It will develop agriculture, small and medium-sized industry, and tourism. It will also use natural resources wisely and protect nature in its original state. This goal is broken down into several areas. **Green economy.** The sum will support green production and services based on modern eco-technology. It will develop a diverse economy that combines agriculture, natural product manufacturing, tourism, and recreation and wellness services. It will also develop intensive agriculture that produces few greenhouse gases and waste and poses no risk to human health or the environment. This will increase the output of natural products. The sum believes this will give residents a stable income from business. **Eco-tourism.** The sum plans to develop eco-tourism with three themes: history, nature, and wellness. Services will meet environmental and health standards. The plan also aims to promote the Dadal tourism brand at home and abroad. The sum has set a goal to develop at least five types of responsible tourism routes. These will rely on the resources and capacity of the Onon-Balj National Park and its surroundings. They will have no negative impact on nature and will involve local residents. These include photo tours for flowers, butterflies, rare insects, birds, and animals; nature and history tours to historic sites; health tours; sport hunting tours; tours to learn about Buryat customs and traditions; and tours linked to cultural and sports events. The sum also plans to build eco-friendly tourist ger camps, campsites, and camping spots that do not harm the environment, and to expand the eco zone. **Resident participation and a green lifestyle.** The sum believes every resident's awareness, knowledge, and skills are key to becoming a green model community. Residents will receive training in stages. The sum aims to raise citizens who are proud of their homeland and live a green lifestyle. The government, citizens, and nonprofit organizations will work together and build new partnerships. Teams will provide advice to residents on health, education, and business. The sum plans to promote healthy habits, extend residents' average life expectancy, implement lifelong learning programs, and run training and awareness campaigns for every age group. It also aims to encourage a green lifestyle movement. The sum will support employment that poses no risk to human health or the environment, and it will reduce the number of unemployed households and households with low income. **Environment, settlements, and waste.** The goal is to build public awareness of environmentally friendly attitudes and habits. By using natural resources efficiently and economically, the sum aims to protect its natural beauty and remain a green region. It will develop a network of state and local protected areas, improve protected area management, and maintain biodiversity and ecosystem services over the long term. It will reduce the effects of climate change and strengthen its ability to prevent extreme weather, natural disasters, and forest and steppe fires. In the sum center and other settlements, it will support sustainable production and consumption using advanced technology and innovation. It will also increase investment in clean technology. The sum will strengthen nature conservation with resident participation, create a healthy living environment, and improve waste management.

## What is there to see in Dadal sum?

The sum has many sites linked to Chinggis Khaan and his ancestors. Some are mentioned in *The Secret History of the Mongols*. Others include museums, religious sites, hot springs, and caves. GPS coordinates for each site are given in parentheses.

### Places linked to Chinggis Khaan

- [**Deluun Boldog**](</destination/landscapes/deluun-boldog>)**.** To mark the 750th anniversary of *The Secret History of the Mongols*, a ritual to honor heaven was held here on July 20, 1990, and a light monument was raised on the Eeren Hill of Boldog. The monument bears an inscription in Mongolian script: "Chinggis Khaan was born here at Deluun Boldog in the Year of the Water Horse, on the 16th day of the first summer month of 1162." (N49°03’04.7 E111°38’36.3)
- **Chinggis Light Statue.** To mark 800 years since Chinggis Khaan's birth, this monument was built on May 31, 1962, on the northern terrace of Dund Nuur, one of the Gurvannuur lakes. The work was led by S. Tumur-Ochir, a member of the Political Bureau of the Mongolian People's Revolutionary Party, and the artist L. Makhbal designed it. The monument is shaped like a flame that burns upward, is 11.7 m tall, and has a 10 m wide base. The front shows Chinggis Khaan standing upright. It is engraved with the saying, "If my body is weary, let it be weary; if my state is strong, let it not be lost." The site is 2 km east of the sum center. (N49°02’00.40 E111°39’16.70)
- **Temuujin Toonot Gate.** Built in 2012 as a stupa-shaped gate, funded by donations from local residents. (N48°59’33.27 E111°36’41.21)
- **Energy ovoo.** People pray here to receive strength from the highest heaven. It is an ovoo (sacred stone cairn). It is 0.5 km southwest of Deluun Boldog. (N49°02’58.3 E111°38’28.6)
- **Side spring.** It is located on the east side of the Khar Tug ovoo, northeast of the sum center. According to legend, Temujin's body was washed with water from this spring when he was born, and Mother Hoelun boiled tea with it for drinking. It is 2 km northwest of Deluun Boldog. (N49°02’45.9 E111°36’53.0)
- **Tunkheleg stream.** The Monggol *Secret History* mentions this stream often. Legend says that Queen Alan Gua met Dobun Mergen beside it. Bodonchar Bogd is said to have spoken his famous saying here: "It is good when the head and the collar are alike." The last queens of the Mongol monarchy, Dondogdulam and Genenpil, are also said to have lived here. It is 4 km west of the side spring. (N49°02’50.82 E111°38’28.69)
- **Dobun Mergen's monument.** A very ancient site made of several upright stones. Local people have long called it the Duva the Blind, Dobun Mergen's monument, or a burial site. It is 1.5 km northwest of Tunkheleg stream. (N49°03’17.18 E111°33’09.11)
- **Bodonchar's grass hut.** The *Secret History of the Mongols* (section 24) describes how Bodonchar, the founder of the Khiad Borjigin clan of Chinggis Khaan's golden lineage, lived in this area by the Balj wetlands, making a shelter from grass. He is the first person said to have spoken the saying, "A person with a brother and a deel with a collar." It is 1.5 km northwest of the Dobun Mergen and Duva the Blind khirigsuur (burial mound). (N49°03’31.5 E111°31’07.0)
- **Chinggis Khar Tug ovoo.** Before setting out on a military campaign, Chinggis Khaan is said to have raised his great black banner on the summit of this mountain. (N49°02’11.4 E111°35’54.50)
- **Seal Stone.** According to the *Altan Tobchi* (Altan Tobchi (the Golden Summary)), a small bird chirped "Chinggis, Chinggis" on a dark stone on the mountain. The stone then split open, and the official seal of the Mongol state appeared from inside. The split stone is still called the "Chinggis Seal Stone." (N49°06’17.63 E111°39’18.12)
- **Santkhaan Mountain.** This sacred mountain lies north of the confluence of the Onon and Agats rivers, and it has a "Santkhaan ovoo" on top. According to legend, two male foals were born here from a mare that was the guardian of Chinggis Khaan's horse herd. It is said that foals born here become fast runners, so before choosing horses for the Naadam, people worship the Santkhaan ovoo and make offerings. Local people still graze and foal their horses on this mountain today. (N49°12’40.9 E111°50’28.9)
- **Degen Jigen (Zeeren Gorge).** A historic site recorded in section 129 of *The Secret History of the Mongols* as the Zeeren Gorge on the Onon River. (N49°14’23.0 E112°01’13.3)
- **Burkhan Khaldun – Bayankhaan Mountain.** Local people regard Bayankhaan Mountain, with its three peaks (Burkhant, Shurgadai, and Orgoit), as Burkhan Khaldun Mountain. Chinggis Khaan is said to have hidden here to escape the Merkit and survived. People have sworn to worship the mountain "for generations to come." The mountain is covered with forest and rock. Its lower slopes have many fruit trees and rare animals, including moose, red deer, bears, and wild boars, and many bird species. (N49°01’11.00 E111°18’46.08)

### Museums and religious sites

- **Temuujin's Childhood Museum.** Opened in 2006, marking 800 years since the founding of the Mongol state. The museum displays stone, bronze, and iron tools from ancient people who lived in the Onon and Balj basins, as well as a straw hut showing Temuujin's childhood, a seated figure of Chinggis Khaan, a black standard, and a chariot-mounted tent. (N49°01’12.86 E111°37’26.81)
- **Local History Museum.** First established in 1959 through an exchange of experience initiated by young herders in the sum. Its collection was expanded in 1971 and now has 346 historical objects. (N49°01’12.80 E111°37’30.03)
- **Hunter Baavai Museum.** A ger museum made up of a collection gathered over a lifetime by D. Zunduidagva, a local veteran and hunter who received the Order of the Red Banner for Military Merit. It also holds a collection of animal taxidermy. The museum displays historical items showing Buryat customs. It is 1 km southwest of the Genenpil Queen's ger foundation. (N49°01’53.98 E111°33’10.37)
- **Genenpil Queen's ger foundation.** This site is known as the homeland of the Khiad Borjigin golden lineage, and the place where the royal silk herd of white horses of the Mongol state grazed. Queen Genenpil, the last wife of the Eighth Bogd Khan, who combined religious and state leadership, lived on this terrace until she was falsely accused of political crimes and arrested. It is 2 km east of Bodonchar's grass hut. (N49°02’16.04 E111°33’12.71)
- **Three Root Stupas.** On top are stupas enshrining three root deities: Janraisig, Manzushir, and Ovchirvaan. They were built to increase the merit of learning and purify sins, and to honor ancestors who died before their time during the political purges of the 1930s. (N49°01’05.18 E111°36’45.09)
- **Gilbert Monastery ruins.** These are the remains of a major center of Yellow Hat Buddhism, which had about 300 monks from 1700 to 1937. On September 20, 1920, Baron Roman von Ungern-Sternberg, a general of Imperial Russia, rode down the Gilbert River with his Asian cavalry division. He is said to have attacked this monastery as he entered the Bogd Khan's Mongolia. (N49°11’24.46 E111°24’09.07)

### Nature, hot springs, and caves

- **Gurvannuur.** Three small lakes located on a flat area inside a pine forest, known as the "Gurvannuur hot spring." The water and mud from the lakes are used to treat stomach, liver, gallbladder, and eczema. People say the mud from the middle lake, which contains leeches, helps with joint disease, while water from the east lake is drunk to treat intestinal illnesses. For more about the spa here, read the [Gurvannuur hot spring sanatorium page](</things-to-do/wellness/gurvannuuryn-rashaan-suvilal>). (N49°01’54.94 E111°39’15.38)
- **Tsagaanchuluut.** Often described as a natural physical therapy complex, this site is said to have healing power for all kinds of illnesses, using traditional and natural methods. (N49°05’23.70 E111°03’19.80)
- **Söögt Nukhtiin Cave.** This cave lies on the right side of the Söögt River valley, a tributary of the Galttai River, and on a short slope behind a rocky mountain on the east fork of the Zhivkheest River. It is not immediately visible. It is a well-shaped vertical cave, like a well, and is 72 m deep. (N49°10’20.78 E111°58’35.61)

Tes sum is located in the north of Zavkhan Province, among the Tagna Range and branches of the Khan Khukh Range. In the past, it was a major farming area that supplied flour and feed to the western provinces. Today it is known for its mountains, rivers, forests, diverse wildlife and birds, and many undocumented historical sites.

## Where is Tes sum located?

The sum lies at an elevation of more than 1,300 m above sea level. It borders the sums of [Bayantes](</province/zavkhan/bayantes>) and [Bayankhairkhan](/province/zavkhan/bayankhairkhan) in Zavkhan Province, Zuunkhangai Sum in Uvs Province, and the Tuva Republic in Russia. The total area is 87,229 hectares. The sum center, Züür, is 301 km from the provincial center. To learn about other sums and sights in the province, read the [Zavkhan Province page](/province/zavkhan).

## Population and bags

Tes sum has four bags: Doono, Niseg, Bayan-Uul and Züür. About 3,386 people live in 894 households, and the sum has 49.2 thousand head of livestock.

## How did Tes go from a banner to a sum?

The administrative history of this area begins in the 17th century. In 1655, the Erdenedüürgch Wang banner of the Zasagt Khan aimag, one banner of Khotogoid, was established here. Under Manchu rule, it was known as the left-hand banner of the middle east of the Khalkha western road. From 1924 to 1931, it was a banner of Delgerkhan Mountain aimag in Khantaishir Mountain, and from 1931 to 1959 it was part of Bayan-Uul sum. The present sum was founded in 1958. At that time, residents of the Doono, Bayan-Uul, Berkh and Nisekh bags of Bayan-Uul sum proposed that their land was too far from the sum center and poorly suited to its needs. After the proposal was checked on site, a resolution of the Presidium of the State Great Khural of the Mongolian People's Republic dated May 31, 1959, separated these bags and their land and people into a new sum named Tes, based on the "Delgereh" cooperative. In 1959, a new sum was therefore formed on the feed farm settlement of Tes in Bayan-Uul sum. The new sum initially had 341 households, 1,507 people, 52,400 head of livestock and 2,298 hectares of land. Soon after, as part of the nationwide virgin land campaign, a farming operation (TAA) was established in Tes sum in 1969. However, because pasture and farmland were judged incompatible side by side, in 1974 the "Delgereh" cooperative of Tes sum was dissolved, merged with the "Jargalant-Amidral" cooperative of Bayan-Uul sum, and attached to Bayan-Uul sum as "Delgereh-Amidral." The Tes sum status was then canceled, and it became the Tes TAA of Bayantes sum, also called Tes khoroo, with Ch. Yadamjav appointed head of the khoroo administration and the TAA. At that time, Tes had 205 households, over 700 people and 23,000 hectares of circulating land. However, this situation did not last long. By decree No. 138 of the Presidium of the State Great Khural of the Mongolian People's Republic in 1977, Tes khoroo was again made a sum. In the 1990s, the Doono and Nisekh bags of Bayantes sum, which had belonged to the former Tes sum, returned to Tes sum, and the sum took on its current boundaries.

## How did the "Delgereh" cooperative form?

The "Delgereh" cooperative, which operated in the Tes area, was founded in 1956 on the initiative of G. Ish, G. Ichinnorov, I. Damdinbazar and L. Magsar. Initially, 23 members from 17 households in bags I, II, III and IX of Bayan-Uul sum began pooling more than 2,000 animals. I. Damdinbazar was elected as the first chairman. Later, the cooperative grew to have three livestock brigades, more than 500 members, a core capital of 3 million MNT and an annual income of 1 million MNT. By 1972, it had more than 2,500 members from over 500 households, 67,700 head of livestock, core capital of 4 million MNT and an annual income of 2 million MNT. In 1974, when the sum was dissolved in connection with Tes farming, the cooperative was merged into the "Jargalant-Amidral" cooperative of Bayan-Uul sum.

## Tes farming operation

Following the virgin land campaign of the 1960s, the Tes farming operation was built with Soviet assistance in 1969, under Council of Ministers Resolution No. 157 of the Mongolian People's Republic. At that time, the Bayan-Uul sum livestock machine station (MAAMS) was dissolved, and more than 20 pieces of equipment, including 9 tracked and wheeled tractors, agricultural trailers and swing machines, worth a total of 2 million MNT, were transferred to the Tes TAA. In 1969, the first plowing took place at Tevkhen, Tsagaanchuluut and Tööent, cultivating 3,056 hectares and producing 3,100 tons of hay, which started production. In 1972, the Soviet side handed over a farm with 35 million MNT in core capital. It included the following facilities:

- a feed factory with capacity to produce 10,000 tons of feed per year;
- an agricultural machinery repair shop capable of servicing 120 tractors;
- an auto garage with about 70 engines;
- a power station with 900 kW capacity;
- steam boilers producing 10,000 Gcal of heat per year;
- a workers' canteen, bakery and hot water facility seating 35 people;
- housing for 50 families.

At first, the Tes TAA had no livestock at all and only grew crops. In 1973, it received 200 cattle and 220 horses from the state farm of Baruunturuun in Uvs Province, and in 1975 it took over more than 8,000 sheep and goats, 37 herders and 13 winter camps from Bayantes sum. Soon it also started a small auxiliary farm raising pigs, chickens and rabbits. The farm combined crop farming, livestock and feed production. Crops made up 88.6 percent of total output, livestock 5.2 percent and feed production 6.2 percent. As of 1987, the Tes TAA had 529 households, 2,900 people and 32,000 hectares of circulating land. Each year, grain was sown on 19.5 thousand hectares, yielding 19,000 to 28,000 tons. This harvest met the flour needs of the region and also supplied wheat to flour mills in Ulaangom and Murun. Each year, about 10,000 tons of livestock feed were also prepared and delivered to the five western provinces. During the difficult market transition of the 1990s, this farm production stopped.

## What are the climate and soil like?

Tes sum has an extreme continental climate. Summers average 23–32 °C, and winters drop to −35 to −42 °C. The sum receives 180–210 mm of precipitation a year. The soil is mainly fertile dark chestnut and sandy.

## Nature: mountains, rivers, plants and animals

**Mountains and ranges.** Ikh Berkh, Baga Berkh, Baruun Doono, Zuun Doono, Khalban, Züür, Zos, Dormon, Nart, Navkhan, Ongon, Kholboo, Talyn Uvulzhin, Aambagaa, Jaambagaa, Arts Surr, Argalant and Suman Rock. **Rivers and streams.** Tes, Zaigal, Tsereg, Mogoi and Khatguurantai. To read more about the Tes River area, see the [Tes River local area page](</destination/protected/tesiin-gol-orchmyn-nutag>). **Plants.** Among the grasses are dagd, yumdujin, ajigtseree, mekheer, onion, mangina, gond, tseene and tagsh. Common berries include pine nuts, sea buckthorn, tosloi, ürgen's eye, wolf's tongue and gishüüne. Trees and shrubs include Siberian pine, black poplar, elm, larch, willow, dog's head shrub, tavilgana and yargai. **Animals.** Red deer, wild boar, roe deer, dorgo, jirkh, mountain hare, hare, wolf, shiluus, red fox, corsac fox, steppe polecat, Pallas's cat, marmot, zuram, üen, solongo, steppe polecat, nokhoi zeekh and steppe gopher live in the area. **Birds.** Black grouse, hur, crow, golden eagle, raven, magpie, turliakh, kharcaga, sar, shar shuvuu, capercaillie, tsorkhiruul, uvuulj, Daurian partridge, cuckoo, swan, goose, ruddy shelduck, crane and tooged are found here.

## What is there to see in Tes sum?

The sum has natural and historical sites. The best known are **Suman Rock**, **Dormon Mountain**, **Khuulga Cave** and **the Züür Turkic inscription**. Beyond these, dozens of sites that have not yet entered research circulation are found in this area.

Khanbogd sum is in Umnugovi Province (aimag) on the border with China. It is known for the Galbyn Gobi, the monasteries founded by Noyon Khutagt Danzanravjaa, and the Oyu Tolgoi mine. Steppe and desert meet here, and you can find natural scenery, history, and religious heritage in one area.

## Where is Khanbogd sum and how far is it?

The sum is 260 km from the province center and shares a border with China to the south. Its area is 1,515.2 km². Khanbogd has 4 bags (the smallest administrative unit). The sum has 6 budget-funded organizations, employing more than 100 public servants. For other sums and sights in the province, read the [Umnugovi Province page](/province/umnugov). Traffic in the sum is busy. The Gashuun Sukhait border crossing, open year-round, is here. The main paved road from Ulaanbaatar to Oyu Tolgoi and the Gashuun Sukhait crossing passes through the intersection of the provincial centers of Umnugovi, Dornogovi, and Govisumber.

## Where did the name Khanbogd come from?

The sum was founded in 1924 as "Galba" at a place called Khar Ovoo Burial Mound in Khadankhoshuu. In 1941 it took the name of its mountain, Khanbogd, and became Khanbogd sum. Next to the three Galba monasteries, a marker was erected to commemorate the sum's founding as Galba in 1924.

## What mineral wealth is there, and what is Oyu Tolgoi?

Khanbogd has rich gold and copper deposits underground. Remains of Early Quaternary giant lizards, reptiles, and petrified wood are also found here, which are rare finds. About 40 km west of the sum, the Oyu Tolgoi mine operates. It is considered one of the world's major copper and gold mines. The project brings large economic benefits to Mongolia, and its processing plant began operating on December 27, 2017.

## What wildlife and birds live here?

The Galbyn Gobi once had large herds of khulan and goitered gazelles. However, with more mining roads and traffic, they moved into Dornogovi Province. Even so, the Houbara Bustard (Chlamudotis undulata), which is rare in the world, still lives in this area. The Mongolian Ground Jay (Podoces hendersoni) and the Saxaul Sparrow (Passer ammodendri) are also common. In recent years, a large pond formed from excess water from the Oyu Tolgoi plant has become a key stopover for migrating waterbirds. Argali and Siberian ibex are not rare on Khanbogd Mountain, the sum's main sacred mountain. On the road, you often see goitered gazelles and khulan, which can lift your mood during the trip.

## What is there to see in Khanbogd sum?

**Natural sites.** Khanbogd sum includes Undai River, Khudeldeg Tsokhio (Moving Rock), Melkhii Khad (Frog Rock), Ikh Bulag (Great Spring), Baga Bulag (Small Spring), Baishin Tsav, Chinggis's Uurga Hole, Three Gazelles Cave, the 3,000 Khailaast, Sevkhuul Sand Dunes, Khaalgan mineral spring, and [Galbyn Gobi](</destination/landscapes/galbyn-govi>), one of the nine wonders of Umnugovi. **Monasteries of the Galbyn Gobi.** About 12 km west-northwest of the sum, in the Galbyn Gobi praised in songs and poems, are the ruins of three famous monasteries founded by Noyon Khutagt Danzanravjaa. Nearby are the ruins of Tsogzol Monastery, Altangerel's Round Stone, Chavgiin Cave, which is linked to Lovonchomb's wealth, the stomach and eye mineral springs, and gallstones. Demchig Monastery was restored with funding from the Ajnai Corporation of Umnugovi, opening new options for domestic travel. About 6 km northwest of the monastery is the World Energy Center tourist ger camp. To learn more about the monasteries, read the [Galbyn Gurvan Khiid page](</destination/heritage/place/galbyn-gurvan-khiid>).

![image](asset:tic-img-8b5e1c8a865da194)

## What is there to see on a day trip around Demchig Monastery?

Demchig Monastery, known as the "World Energy Center," is often thought of as a place to recharge, along with Bayajikh Cave, Mother's Womb Cave, and Melkhii Khad. However, there are many interesting places that few people know or visit. Yembuu Rock, the Khutagt's meditation seat, Chinggis's Uurga Hole, Dombon Tsokhio, and the Stone Factory each have their own history and legends. Exploring all of them takes a full day. **Eereet Monastery.** The second of the three Galba monasteries, once home to more than 300 lamas, is 3 km from Demchig Monastery (the first). Its ruins resemble those of Ongi Monastery. East of the ruins, on a cliff, a figure of Ulaan Jamsran appeared naturally. Southeast of the monastery is a rock shaped like a woman lying face-up. This rock is said to face Erdene Rock (Boovon Rock) at Demchig Monastery. Men are said to come here to pray for their marriages and their children's futures. **Tsagaan Tolgoi Monastery.** Head 3 km east from Eereet Monastery to see the ruins of Tsagaan Tolgoi Monastery, the third of the Galba monasteries. It is said that the three monasteries are arranged in a pyramid shape for a reason. The surrounding landscape has water, springs, and gorges, and it is beautiful. **Sites to the southeast.** Going east-southeast from the sum, you can see the ruins of Tsogzol Monastery and the unusual Khudeldeg Tsokhio. You will also find a statue of Yulkha, the protector from all harm, with the figure of a boy riding a red goat, created by the Khutagt.

## How do I get there?

You can travel from Khanbogd sum center to Demchig Monastery by two routes. The direct route is only 12 km, but it is a rough mountain road with rocks. The other is a straight white road of about 40 km via Oyu Tolgoi. Both take the same time and fuel, so if you do not know the mountain road, take the Oyu Tolgoi road. For a longer trip, you can travel about 150 km to Khanbogd on the route "Uush Manhan – Morin Tolgoi Gorge – Uekhii – Sevkhuul – Mogoit – Khoyor Ulziit Uul – Khanbogd Mountain – Bayangol – Amtgai – Argalant – Suikhent – Ulgii Monastery – Galbyn Gurvan Khiid."

## Where can I stay?

Khanbogd is a large, comfortable sum with many lights and buildings. Compared with other sums in Mongolia, it has many hotels and is cozy. Several hotels and restaurants connected to clean and wastewater lines operate in the sum center. There is still room to develop tourism near the border.

## How is livestock farming done?

Since 2006, Khanbogd has ranked first nationally for years in a row for its Galba red camels. It also raises specialized breeds, including fat-tailed sheep of the Gavi clan and a Gobi Gurvan Saikhan breed of cashmere goats.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Khanbogd sum from the province center?

It is 260 km from the Umnugovi provincial center. Oyu Tolgoi is 40 km west of the sum, and the Galbyn Gobi monasteries are about 12 km west-northwest.

### Which road should I take to Demchig Monastery?

From the sum center, there is a direct 12 km rough mountain road, or an approximately 40 km white road via Oyu Tolgoi. Both take the same time and fuel, so if you are not familiar with the mountain road, choose the Oyu Tolgoi road.

### Which rare birds live in Khanbogd sum?

The Houbara Bustard, which is rare in the world, along with the Mongolian Ground Jay and the Saxaul Sparrow, live here. Migrating waterbirds land on the pond formed from excess water from Oyu Tolgoi.

Bayantumen sum is a farming sum on the edge of Choibalsan, the center of Dornod Province (aimag). It lies just 12 km from the city. Its administrative center is Tsagaan Ders settlement. Many local people call the sum "Khan Uul."

## Where is Bayantumen sum?

The sum center, Tsagaan Ders settlement, is 645 km from Ulaanbaatar and 12 km from [Choibalsan city (Kherlen sum)](/province/dornod/kherlen). The sum gets its electricity from the Choibalsan city power system. To learn about other sums and sights in the province, read the [Dornod Province page](/province/dornod). The sum covers a total area of 832,080 hectares. Of this, 478,411 hectares are pasture, 33,523 hectares are hayfields, and 324 hectares are cropland.

## How was the sum established?

The sum began as the "Khodolmor" herders' cooperative (negdel), formed in January 1950. Then Choibalsan city administration head S. Batmunkh led a group of seven people, including O. Gansukh, D. Danzan, Tumur and Namst, to set up the cooperative in the land of the city's 6th khoroo (sub-district). Its goal was to supply the provincial center with vegetables.

In 1952, the cooperative moved from Choibalsan to a place called Tsagaan Ders. In the 1940s, a Soviet 17th Army vegetable field and auxiliary farm stood there. The settlers used a few small houses that remained. In 1958, the "Bayan" cooperative of Kherlenbayan sum merged with Khodolmor. In March 1959, by a decision of the Mongolian People's Republic Council of Ministers, Bayantumen sum was founded. The cooperative was renamed "Bayasgalant Khodolmor" (Joyful Labor). S. Batmunkh served as sum and cooperative head, and Zh. Uljikhutag as secretary.

That same year, the sum opened a primary school (principal Yu. Sumiya), a human clinic (junior doctor A. Jambaa), a kindergarten (manager B. Nadmid), and a trade and procurement unit (head Ishjamts). In 1960, a state station for breeding and artificial insemination and a veterinary branch were established.

## What was farming like near the city?

Bayantumen developed as a farming sum serving the city. Between 1974 and 1990, it built large agricultural facilities, including a mechanized dairy farm with 400 cows and a 1,000-hectare irrigation system. The sum grew fodder crops on 500 to 1,000 hectares and supplied the Choibalsan food factory with one million liters of milk every year. It also grew grain, potatoes and vegetables on 1,000 to 1,500 hectares.

As the population and livestock grew, the cooperative's core assets reached MNT 41.4 million in 1990. At the exchange rate of the time, it earned MNT 7.8 million in cash income per year. When the economy shifted to markets, the cooperative, some state agencies, the dairy farm's livestock and assets passed to businesses and individuals.

## What are the nature and climate like?

The sum's land is undulating and has low hills, including Galyn Shil (893 m), Mankhtai (856 m) and Bor Undur (850 m). The soil is plain-valley brown soil and saline meadow soil. The sum has the Kherlen River and the Tsaidam and Khotont lakes. The climate is continental. The average temperature in January is −22 to −24 °C, and in July it is +20 to +22 °C. The sum gets about 250 mm of precipitation per year. Wildlife includes Mongolian gazelles, wolves, red foxes, corsac foxes and Pallas's cats. The steppe is covered with thick grasses, including feather grass, Hiagt, Mangir and Taana.

Local people worship certain mountains and ovoos (sacred stone cairns), including Enkh, Munkh, Khutag and Khan Uul.

## How many people and animals live here?

Bayantumen sum has 2,100 residents and three bags (the smallest administrative unit). The sum has 95,000 head of livestock, including 670 camels, 15,400 horses, 9,800 cattle, 43,000 sheep and 26,700 goats.

### How far is Bayantumen sum from Choibalsan?

The sum center, Tsagaan Ders settlement, is 12 km from Choibalsan and 645 km from Ulaanbaatar.

### When was Bayantumen sum founded?

It was founded in March 1959, by decision of the Mongolian People's Republic Council of Ministers. Its origin is the "Khodolmor" cooperative, formed in 1950.

Songinokhairkhan District: history and legends

Songinokhairkhan District is located in the western part of Ulaanbaatar. It takes its name from the sacred Songinokhairkhan Mountain, which rises to the southwest of the city. The district has many interesting places with rich history and legends. These include the mountain, which is known as the most fierce of the Four Sacred Mountains of Khuree, an ancient city ruin in the Tuul River valley, khirigsuur burial mounds (burial mounds), and the birthplace of the Second Bogd Khan.

![image](asset:tic-img-7ce78920dee40bdb)

## When was Songinokhairkhan District established?

By Resolution No. 18 of the State Great Khural of Mongolia in 1992, Songinokhairkhan District of the capital was established on October 1, 1992. The district lies in the western wing of the capital and borders [Bayangol District](/province/ulaanbaatar#bayangol). It also borders the Altanbulag, Bayanchandman, Bornuur and Batsumber sums of Tuv Province (aimag). The district covers 120.1 hectares. In 2016, it had 315,802 residents and 73,576 head of livestock. To learn about the other districts of the capital, read the [Ulaanbaatar city page](/province/ulaanbaatar).

## What are the name and legends of Songinokhairkhan Mountain?

Songinokhairkhan is a sacred mountain with rocky slopes, covered with pine and caragana shrubs. Its three sharp peaks look impressive from a distance. Long ago, wild red onions grew in abundance on the white slopes of the mountain. For this reason, it was called "Songinot Khairkhan," which later became Songinokhairkhan. Legend says that a fiery black fox lives on this mountain, which is why snow does not settle there in winter. It is said that anyone who sees the small fox, with a white-tipped tail and pure black body, will have good fortune. The fox is not seen by everyone, and people are warned not to harm it or even think about frightening it. According to the learned lama Luvsanperenlei, Songinokhairkhan Mountain is a guardian spirit riding a blue ox, with crooked tusks, and fond of arak. This guardian is very fierce and symbolizes quick results when people ask for help with difficult tasks. It is also said to sometimes take the form of a military lord. For this reason, people who go to war or face hard tasks have long prayed to the old guardian of the mountain, offering milk and dairy foods. They believed this would protect them from harm. Because it is the most fierce of the Four Sacred Mountains of Khuree, people said that the animals living there, even marmots and zuram, were not touched.

## What animals and birds live there?

Marmots, zuram, foxes, corsac foxes, Pallas's cats, lynxes, hares and wolves live in the foothills of Songinokhairkhan. In harsh winters (dzud), red deer, roe deer, argali and Siberian ibex sometimes migrate and settle here. Among the birds, it is said that black grouse, Daurian partridges, capercaillies, yellow-billed birds, rooks, magpies, golden eagles and ravens once lived here.

## What is the history of the Tuul River valley?

**Ancient finds.** Historian Dr. Navaan reported that bronze arrowheads, a resonant bronze bell and a belt decorated with gold were found in a grave at the foot of the mountain. **Willow herders.** Along the Tuul River, which flows past the foot of the mountain, a group of families known as the "Burgasan" herders lived. They made walls, frames, buckets, the tips of whips, camel, saddle and yak saddle wood from willow and sold them. **Birthplace of the Second Bogd Khan.** In the Ugoomriin Shar Bulan bend of Songinokhairkhan, the birth of the Second Bogd Khan took place while Darkhan Chin Wang Dondovdorj lived there. People sang, "In the yellow bend of Ugoomor, flowers of every color bloom. In the palace of a noble family, our elder hierarch was born." After the hierarch's birth, Gavj Urtnasan built two small temples on the northern terrace of the Tuul River, at a place called Bayart Tolgoi, and made eight stupas decorated with stone carvings. These eight stupas were collapsed and damaged, but in 1950, Gevsh Danigai, a lama, brought them and placed them around the white stupa of [Gandantegchinlen Monastery](</destination/heritage/place/gandantegchenlin-khiid>). **Mongolia's first veterinary medicine factory.** During the Bogd Khan period, in 1915, a 25-year agreement was signed at Morin Uul Gatsaa with Russian private veterinarians. Under the agreement, medicines to keep the livestock of the Tusheet Khan, Zasagt Khan and Tsetsen Khan provinces healthy were to be produced. This is how Mongolia's first private veterinary medicine factory, under Russian ownership, was founded. The factory ruins are on a small hill near the Tuul River, behind the bridge of the Bio Combine. **Ruins of the Khan's Lower Capital.** In the Bökhög and Türgen river basins, grain and vegetables were grown in ancient times. Just before these two rivers meet, near the western tip of Songinokhairkhan Mountain, on the terrace of the Tuul River, lie the ruins of an old city. It is considered to be the lower capital ruin on the black thicket of the Tuul River, where Toghrul Wang Khan of the Kereit spent the summers. It is mentioned several times in The Secret History of the Mongols. **Khirigsuur and noble tombs.** Near the upper mouth of the western tip of Songinokhairkhan Mountain, the northeast spring of Burkhant Mountain, Dadar beside Bulgat Khairkhan, and the slopes of Sevleg Mountain, there are many ancient khirigsuur and tombs. From Dadar Mountain, noble tombs dating to the 12th–13th centuries were discovered. **Mongolia's first airport.** Since 1938, in the Tuul River basin and the Bökhög and Türgen river valleys, a bomber aircraft repair site, an airport, military units, factories and service facilities were first established in Mongolia.
