Wetlands in Mongolia: Rivers, Lakes and Springs
@@ FIELD seoDescription textarea

@@ FIELD body richText
Mongolia's wetlands include rivers and streams, lakes and ponds, springs and mineral springs. They are densest in the mountainous north and west of the country. They are scarce in the south and the Gobi.

## What are the wetlands of Mongolia? A quick overview

- **Components:** rivers and streams, lakes and ponds, springs, mineral springs.
- **Drainage basins:** the Arctic Ocean, the Pacific Ocean, and the Central Asian internal drainage basin.
- **Deepest lake:** [Khuvsgul Lake](</destination/khuvsgul-nuur>), 262 m.
- **Large lakes in the west:** Uvs, Khyargas, [Khar Us](</destination/protected/khar-us-nuur>), Buun Tsagaan, [Orog](</destination/landscapes/orog-nuur>).
- **Dried up:** 683 rivers and streams, 1,484 springs, 760 lakes and ponds (according to the data at the time the source was written).

## Which ocean do Mongolia's rivers flow to?

Mongolia's rivers belong to three drainage basins: the Arctic Ocean, the Pacific Ocean, and the Central Asian internal drainage basin. The water network is densest in the mountainous north and west. In the south, center and southeast, rivers and streams are few and water is scarce. Their water drains into closed basins and disappears.

## Where are Mongolia's large lakes?

- **The west.** Large lakes such as Uvs, Khyargas, Khar Us, Buun Tsagaan and Orog lie in the Great Lakes Depression and the Valley of the Lakes.
- **The Selenge basin.** Khuvsgul Lake is here. It is the deepest lake (262 m) and holds the largest freshwater reserves.
- **The Orkhon basin.** The largest lake here is [Ugii Lake](</destination/landscapes/ugii-nuur>).

## What feeds Mongolia's lakes?

Lakes in Mongolia are divided into four groups by how they are fed:

- **Rainwater.** Small lakes fed mainly by rain. They dry up from time to time.
- **River water.** Lakes fed mainly by rivers.
- **Groundwater.** Lakes fed mainly by underground water.
- **Mixed sources.** Lakes fed by both river water and groundwater.

## When do lake water levels change?

The source of the water determines a lake's water regime. Most lakes get most of their water in the three summer months. As a result, levels rise sharply in July and August and drop quickly from October.

## Why are rivers and lakes drying up?

Besides drought and dry conditions, human activity has a major impact:

- mining makes water turbid and changes water regimes;
- many families settle at the headwaters of rivers, streams and springs.

According to the surface water census at the time the source was written, 683 rivers and streams, 1,484 springs and 760 lakes and ponds had dried up.

In July and August.

Stone figures with Turkic-period clothing have the deel (traditional robe) front folded the wrong way. Stone figures linked to Mongol history stand out because the front of the deel is folded the right way.

## What are the key facts about the Dadart Mountain Tomb?

- **Location:** Tuul River valley, mouth of the Burkhantyn gorge, slope of Dadart Mountain.

- **Date:** 13th–14th century.

## How was the person buried in the tomb?

At a depth of 1.75 m, the deceased was laid in a niche in the north side of the tomb. The burial included the following items:

- **Right of the head:** A sheep shoulder blade and an iron knife with a wooden handle.

Based on the food remains and other finds from the tomb, it is dated to the 13th–14th century.

## What is the stage like?

## Why is the open-air theater important?

## What is the State Department Store? Quick facts

- **Founded:** 1921, as the "Central Store."

## What was the original Central Store like?

The store opened with about 10 staff members. They included a director, an instructor, a chief accountant, an accounting instructor, a settlement accountant, an accounting apprentice, and a goods specialist.

## What is Mongol Tuurgatan Theater?

- **Location:** Zuunmod soum, 1st bag (the smallest administrative unit), Tuv Province.

## How did the theater develop?

- **1942.** A provincial club with 12 members was founded.

The Memorial to the Ninety Heroes stands in [Khalkhgol soum](/province/dornod/khalkhgol) in [Dornod Province](/province/dornod) (aimag), at the southern tip of Bayantsagaan. It honors the Soviet soldiers who died fighting for Mongolia's freedom and independence.

![image](asset:tic-img-f8cbe84b520e6c7e)

## What is the Memorial to the Ninety Heroes? Key facts

- **Location:** Dornod Province, Khalkhgol soum, southern tip of Bayantsagaan.
- **Built:** 1954, on the 15th anniversary of the Khalkhgol victory.
- **Decision:** the government of the Mongolian People's Republic.
- **Names:** 110 soldiers, including Soviet heroes I. I. Fediuninsky and V. I. Demitri.
- **Renewal:** on the 20th, 25th, 30th and 50th anniversaries of the victory.

## How has the memorial changed over time?

At first there was a single monument. On the 20th, 25th, 30th and 50th anniversaries of the Khalkhgol victory, it was repaired and expanded into a full complex. Some of the smaller statues were moved onto a special platform.

## What is written on the main monument?

The main monument bears an inscription honoring the Soviet army soldiers and Mongolian People's Revolutionary Army troops who died in the Battle of Khalkhgol against the Japanese aggressors. They fought for the independence and security of the peace-loving Mongolian people. Below the inscription, a sword and a rifle are placed crossed.

## What is written on the courtyard gate?

The gate inscription says that the Soviet army's First Group, with 57 thousand men, played a decisive role in the Khalkhgol battle against militarist Japan. It also says that many thousands of Soviet soldiers gave their lives. It notes that the government decided to build the memorial in 1954, on the 15th anniversary of the victory. For other sites linked to the Khalkhgol battle, read about the [Bunkhan Tolgoi Soviet Red Army airmen's memorial](</destination/heritage/place/bunkhan-tolgoi-zuvlultiin-ulaan-armiin-nisegchdiin>) and [Baatar Khot Bayantumen](</destination/landscapes/baatar-khot-bayantumen>).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the Memorial to the Ninety Heroes?

It is in Khalkhgol soum, Dornod Province, at the southern tip of Bayantsagaan.

### When was the memorial built?

It was built in 1954, on the 15th anniversary of the Khalkhgol victory.

### How many soldiers' names are on the memorial?

The memorial lists the names of 110 soldiers.
