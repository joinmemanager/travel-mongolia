## What are the key facts about Bayanzag Settlement?

- **Location:** Umnugovi Province, Bulgan soum, on the road into the Gurvansaikhan Range.
- **Discovered:** 1925, by Berkey and Morris.
- **Excavated by:** Berkey and Nelson.
- **Studied again:** in the 1960s by A. P. Okladnikov, and in 1985 by D. Dorj, A. P. Derevianko and J. Olsen.
- **Notable find:** a core scraper known as the "Gobi core."
- **Period:** Neolithic.

## What was the Andrews expedition looking for in Mongolia?

From the 1920s, scholars such as H. F. Osborn proposed that Central Asia, including Mongolia, might be one of the places where modern humans evolved from apes. In their view, mountain building and a drier climate in Central Asia pushed forest-dwelling apes to adapt to hilly steppe and walk on two legs. To test this idea in the field, the American Central Asiatic Expedition, led by R. C. Andrews, worked in southern Mongolia in 1922–1928. It discovered major Stone Age sites, including Artsbogd and Bayanzag.

## Where is Bayanzag Settlement?

According to the Andrews expedition, the settlement lies northwest of Khaalga, along the trade route from Khaalga to Khovd. The source records this distance as 687.5 miles.

## What was found at Bayanzag?

Thousands of stone tools and pottery fragments were found here.

- **The Gobi core.** The most interesting find is a core scraper that entered archaeology under the name "Gobi core."
- **Stone tools.** Arrowheads, tools with notched edges, blades and scrapers are the most common. Spearhead-like or leaf-shaped weapons also turn up.
- **Pottery.** Fragments are decorated with parallel lines, straight lines, waves and scratched patterns. Reconstruction suggests vessels with a slightly flared rim, a rounded body and a flat base.

## How old is Bayanzag Settlement?

The settlement belongs to the Neolithic period. Attempts to make clay pottery began in the Upper Paleolithic, became more refined in the Mesolithic, and in the Neolithic people began producing decorated pottery. The patterned pottery of Bayanzag shows that it dates to the Neolithic.

Deer stones are remarkable monuments to the history and culture of tribes from the Bronze Age and early Iron Age. They are widespread across most provinces of Mongolia, Tuva in Russia, and the Lake Baikal region.

![image](asset:tic-img-f1040255635467ad)

## What are deer stones? A quick overview

- **Distribution:** Most provinces of Mongolia, Tuva, and the Lake Baikal region.
- **Age:** Bronze Age and early Iron Age. Researchers believe they were made up to the 3rd century BC.
- **Height:** On average, over 1–4 m.
- **Main images:** The sun, the moon, stylized deer, belts, and tools and weapons.
- **Type:** Monumental sculpture.

## What do people mean by a deer stone?

A deer stone is a tall, narrow stone with all four sides carefully dressed. Its carvings are arranged in three sections.

- **Top.** A circular ring (the sun and moon) is usually carved on the front, and sometimes on both the front and back.
- **Main body.** In the classic style, several stylized deer are carved so that they wrap around the stone.
- **Lower section.** A belt is carved beneath the deer, with tools and weapons hanging from it.

Many deer stones are also made from natural stones carved without dressing, and many show no weapons.

## Do deer stones show animals other than deer?

Some stones show deer realistically. Others show horses, gazelles, Siberian ibex, boars, birds or snow leopards in place of deer or between them. These stones are rare and not widespread. Their composition, marks, belts and weapons are not very different from classic deer stones. So researchers think there is no need to classify them separately as "horse stones" or "gazelle stones."

## Where are deer stones located?

- On steppes, in valleys, at the foot of mountains and on the tops of small hills, either alone or in groups.
- Next to khirigsuur (burial mounds) and square burials.
- As standing stones in the outer walls and corners of these burials.
- Sometimes reused as coffin lids or walls.

## How were deer stones made?

Deer stones are on average over 1–4 m tall. Makers combined line drawing, carving and sculpture. For this reason, researchers regard them as monumental sculpture.

## How have deer stones been studied?

Many Mongolian and foreign researchers have studied deer stones, mostly from an archaeological perspective, and have put forward their views. Most of them believe the monuments were made from the early Iron Age up to the 3rd century BC.

## What makes the Sagsai River settlements special?

The settlements contain traces of ancient and relatively recent glacial deposits. They lie 10–220 m above the present ground level of the area. Their heights vary, and these exposed sites are called "open settlements."

## What was found at Sagsai River?

Across the 12 settlements, the following finds were recorded:

- **Location:** The southern edge of Umnugovi Province, near the national border.
- **Studied:** 1960, 1962, and 1967, by Mongolian-Soviet researchers.
- **Find areas:** Six.
- **Raw material:** Dark gray stone.

## What tools were found at Otson Maanit?

The finds are made of dark gray stone. Here are some representative examples:

- **Retouched flake.** 8.9×11×2 cm. Small chips were struck from all edges of the front face, and the edge was retouched evenly all the way around. The back has a large percussion bulb, and the cross-section is slightly convex. It is fully suited for cutting.
- **Round scraper.** 6.5×5.1×1.7 cm, made from a pebble. The main body was left untouched. The right edge was lightly trimmed, and the upper edge was retouched very carefully by striking from one side.
- **Small knife resembling a blade tool.** 4.1×5.6×1.1 cm. It was struck from both sides to form an even, wavy edge. It resembles a blade tool found near Yarkh Mountain, but is somewhat smaller.

## What makes the Otson Maanit cores special?

The round and elongated cores look very ancient. In Europe, South Asia, and Africa, such cores are common in the Middle Paleolithic. From the elongated cores, toolmakers struck off long, oval-triangular blades with very careful, light blows. These blades have a convex back and straight edges. Long, wide blades with an angled striking platform were found in each of the six areas. These cores and blades were made with the same technique as the Levallois method. ## How was the cave discovered?

Dr. E. Avirmed is a cave researcher. In his book "Caves of Mongolia," published in 2008, he wrote that in 1968 a blast was set off in shaft No. 27, where fluorite was being mined. The tremor opened the cave mouth. The cave drops straight down from a pit that had been dug by hand to mine fluorite.

## What makes the Tes River special?

The Tes River begins behind the Bulnai Range and flows into Uvs Lake. It is the largest of the many rivers that feed the lake. Its upper and middle sections run between forested mountains. Its lower reaches, near the mouth, cross sandy, desert-like land. The result is a distinctive and scenic landscape.

### Altan Els

The area has a sand dune called [Altan Els](</destination/landscapes/altan-els>). It is said to have stayed in place since it formed. Groves of black saxaul trees grow on this sand.

## What are the "Five Tes Rivers"?

Maps show the Tes as a single river. It starts in the Bulnai Range, crosses Mongolia and Russia, and flows into Uvs Lake. Mongolians, however, call it the "Five Tes Rivers." In fact, five rivers run through the valley and flow into Uvs Lake, but they were never marked on maps. They are the Jireeg, Ikh Gol, Khar Us, Nariin and Khosht Ereg. The people of the Tes River live in the basin of these five rivers. The source describes it as a land granted by heaven, where the people of Tes soum could live in wealth and happiness.

## What plants grow in the Tes River Area?

The many branches of the Tes River have poplar groves, willow thickets, dense tamarisk brush, mixed-herb and grass meadows, wet meadows and patchy forest. Wild sea buckthorn grows along the river valley. Many berries also grow here, including currants, wild rose, rowan, Asian bird cherry, raspberries and dog rose.

## What animals and birds live in the Tes River Area?

- **Mammals.** These include the critically endangered Eurasian wild boar listed in the Red Book, roe deer, red deer and the Siberian weasel. When the source was written, mink and muskrat had settled here and were breeding.
- **Birds.** These include the critically endangered whooper swan, spoonbill, black-winged stilt, ruddy shelduck, mute swan and whistling swan, relict gull, snow plover, bar-headed goose and great bustard.

## What is depicted on the rock at Bugat?

### Three deer side by side

On the left part of the rock, three deer are packed very close together. Their outlines are pecked along the edges. The artist used the space between the antlers and the back of the middle deer to fit in the chest of the third deer. The deer's sex is not forgotten either. These deer closely resemble the stylized images on deer stones:

- a narrow, long muzzle;
- many branching antlers that sweep along the body;
- a long body and neck;
- a high withers and a rounded rump;
- narrow, long ears.

The hind legs of the third deer are drawn fairly realistically. However, its front legs and the legs of the first two deer are not shown clearly. As on deer stones, you can see a tendency to fit as many deer as possible into a small space.

### Other figures

- **Deer with two-pronged antlers.** In the upper right, a deer with a long muzzle, two-pronged antlers and a large, thick rump is pecked over its entire surface.
- **Siberian ibex.** Below it, an ibex with long curved horns, a short tail and a slender neck is also pecked over its entire surface.
- **Small deer.** At the right edge, a small deer with many-pronged antlers is shown realistically, with its outline pecked.

## What makes the Bugat rock paintings special?

At Bugat, you can see two techniques: pecking an animal over its entire surface and pecking only its outline. You can also see two styles: realistic and stylized. Stylized deer images clearly dominate in size and composition.

Six deer and one Siberian ibex.

### How old are the Bugat rock paintings?

They date to the middle Bronze Age, the second half of the 2nd millennium BC.

Tsagaan Utug is a valley about 18 km northeast of the center of [Sagil soum](</province/uvs/sagil>) in [Uvs Province (aimag)](</province/uvs>). It lies south of the Khar Nars in Zuun Turgen. The rocks at the head of the valley and on its north slope hold many ancient pecked carvings.

## What is Tsagaan Utug? Key facts

- **Location:** Uvs Province, about 18 km northeast of the Sagil soum center, south of the Khar Nars in Zuun Turgen.
- **Technique:** pecked and punched into the rock.
- **Main subjects:** ibex, deer, wolves, dogs and ibex hunts.
- **Highlight:** a rare image of a person riding a deer.
- **Stone tools:** Paleolithic tools are found near the first winter camp in the valley.
- **Nearby site:** [Khad Uzuur rock paintings](</destination/heritage/place/khad-uzuuriin-khadny-zurag>).

## What artifacts have been found at Tsagaan Utug?

Many Paleolithic stone tools made of gray flinty stone are found near the first winter camp and the livestock dung deposits in the valley.

## What is carved on the rocks?

Ancient people mostly carved ibex here, along with deer, wolves and [dogs](/about/nature/wildlife/nokhoi). They emphasized the animals' shape, movement and sex. Scenes of hunters, especially ibex hunts, are common.

- Several figures aim a bow at an ibex that is being chased by a dog or frightened by a rider without a saddle.

### Where are the male ibex and the hunters?

On one rock, a slender ibex with thin, curved horns follows behind another animal. Behind it comes a heavy-bodied male ibex with thick horns, many bends in its horns and a raised tail. The artist stressed that it is a male. To the left are a hunter on foot aiming a bow and a person riding a deer and holding a long spear.

### Who is the person riding a deer?

- **Spear.** The spear has a tassel behind the blade. The rear end of the shaft is thin, and a cord or a wide, tassel-like object hangs from it.
- **Deer.** It has short, three-pronged antlers and rather short legs, so it appears to be a reindeer.
- **Rare.** People riding deer are very rare in rock paintings, which makes this image special.

### Who is the archer?

One figure stands with legs apart, ready to shoot with a bow. The bow is short, and the shoulder grip looks like a composite bow. The artist emphasized the figure's male sex and drew the arrowhead as a large triangle.

## How old are the Tsagaan Utug rock paintings?

Researchers date these images to the early Iron Age. The source gives the period as the 3rd to 1st millennium BC.
