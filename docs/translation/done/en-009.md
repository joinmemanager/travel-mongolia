- **Location:** Bayankhongor Province, Bayanlig soum. It is 25 km from Khatan Suudal Mountain, which you pass on the road from Bayanlig to Bogd soum in [Uvurkhangai Province](/province/uvurkhangai).

## Where is Zuukh Neolithic Settlement?

The settlement is 25 km from Khatan Suudal Mountain, along the road from Bayanlig soum center to Bogd soum center in Uvurkhangai. One of them held Paleolithic remains, and another held remains from the early Bronze Age.

## What was found at Zuukh Neolithic Settlement?

The Neolithic layer produced a total of 8,567 stone artifacts:

- cores, blades and flakes;
- scrapers and knife blades;
- arrowheads and spearheads;
- grinding slabs and pounding stones used for processing grain.

The finds show that ancient people lived here from the 9th–7th millennium BC, when Gobi-type cores began to dominate in Mongolia, until the 3rd millennium BC.

- He commanded 40,000 soldiers under his own banner and worked for his homeland's independence.

## What is shown on the large rocks to the south?

The large, smooth rock directly south of Khad Uzuur is covered with pecked animals.

- Shikhikhutag, from the Tatar fortress.

- In return for his service, he would be forgiven nine offenses;

## Why is Ishgen Tolgoi important?

Mongolia has 5 recorded Paleolithic rock painting sites, and 3 of them are in Khovd Province. Ishgen Tolgoi is one of them. The other two are the [Khoid Tsenkher Cave paintings](</destination/landscapes/khoid-tsenkheriin-agui>) and the Khar Uzuur rock paintings in Chandman.

- **Tiger.** The tiger images led researchers to conclude that tigers once lived in Mongolia. The stripes are rendered especially well.
- **Carving.** Unlike the Khoid Tsenkher Cave paintings, these figures were carved into the rock.
- **Stone tools.** Researchers keep finding many stone tools nearby, including pebble tools from the Paleolithic.

People have lived in the Tsenkher River valley since ancient times. They hunted, and left behind tools, weapons and remarkable works of art.

## How do you know the Ishgen Tolgoi images are ancient?

- **Faded carvings.** Over time the carvings have worn down and are almost the same color as the rock. You can only find them by feeling the surface with your hand.
- **Windward side.** Most other rock paintings are on the sheltered side, away from the wind. The Ishgen Tolgoi images were carved on the windward side, which is constantly exposed to wind.
- **Free placement.** The animal figures are scattered here and there and face any direction.

## How were the animals depicted?

The artists clearly tried to show the key features of each animal.

- **Ibex.** The horns are drawn as graceful arcs that do not touch the back.
- **Argali.** The horns are drawn in a half circle, with the tips doubled and curled.
- **Technique.** The general outline of the body was pecked along its edge and the whole head was hollowed out. This was the art method of the time.
- **Missing legs.** Some figures leave the belly and legs unjoined and unfinished.
- **Male animals.** The male sex of animals such as horses, cattle and deer is shown by hollowing out the rock.

## How did the name Baruun Khuree come about?

In 1654, the Ikh Khuree (the Great Monastery Camp) moved from the Orkhon River valley to the southern slope of the Khentii Mountain. [Undur Gegeen Zanabazar](</about/history/people/undur-gegeen-zanabazar>) left the core of the monastery, the sutras, and valuable historical items with his younger brother Soivonbiliibdorj. ## Who is the Noyon Lama?

The history of this monastery is linked to the origins of the Monastery of Scriptures, also known as the Bogd's Khuree. Undur Gegeen served as the religious head of Baruun Khuree for many years. When he grew old, Tusheet Khan Vanjildorj offered his son Puntsagravdan to him as a disciple. Undur Gegeen honored the boy with the title "Noyon Lama" and installed him as the religious master of Baruun Khuree. The Noyon Lamas who followed in succession came from the golden lineage of [Chinggis Khaan (Genghis Khan)](</about/history/people/chingis-khaan>). This shows that the Mongolian state and religion were closely tied for centuries.

## What is the shrine of the Chinggis Khaan banner?

Baruun Khuree was the main monastery of the golden lineage of Chinggis Khaan and of worship for the Tusheet Khan banner. For this reason, the "banner shrine," where the black and white banners of Chinggis Khaan were enshrined and worshipped, stood within a complex of sacred sites called the "Northern Buddha Shrine." Sources mention plans to revive this tradition and build a shrine complex for the black and white banners of Chinggis Khaan in the "Sar River" valley of Shankh.

## How was the monastery restored?

Historical records say the great monastery once stood behind Tulenge Mountain, on the bank of the Chuduur River, at the foot of Nuuruts Tolgoi. It was destroyed in a difficult period. However, the "Noyon Lama Temple," Puntsagdorj's main temple, survived. Based on it, restoration of Baruun Khuree began in 1990.

- **Uninterrupted prayers.** Even in the years when religious freedom was restricted, the monastery community kept up the tsogchin prayer assembly (a major communal chanting service) first held by Undur Gegeen Zanabazar. They take great pride in this.
- **Treasured heritage.** The great Khalkha sacred image, the [Vajrapani statue](/about/culture/religion/ochirvaani-burkhan), Undur Gegeen's seal, ritual garments, and some works and Buddha images have survived.

## How did Baruun Damnuurchin get its name?

In 1778, Ikh Khuree settled in the Selbe Valley. The Chinese, who had long made a living from trade, were settled at a place called Ulaan Dov. Later they were resettled closer to the central market, and the name "Baruun Damnuurchin" came into use. West and south of the market, up to today's transport office, stood quarters of firewood sellers, coal sellers, felt makers, carpenters and blacksmiths.

## What was the "Chinese town" like?

North of Baruun Damnuurchin, a Chinese quarter stretched to Nogoon Lake. It had one long street and nine narrow cross streets. Until 1921, the entrance of every outer street had a gate. By day, the 21 gates opened for trade. At night, they were locked from the inside, and the quarter became like a walled "Chinese town."

The market was more than a place to trade:

- a meeting place for sellers and buyers;
- a gathering place for curious people;
- a meeting place for volunteer artists;
- a place foreigners visited;
- a gathering place for beggars and the poor;
- a place where craftspeople sold their products;
- a meeting place for livestock traders.

The market had separate sections selling horses, gers, tents, camel gear, saddle frames, shuzgii, dorvuljin, sheepskin deels, hats, tripods, fire tongs, meat and yellow butter. In the early 1900s, a good, gentle horse sold for 4 rubles.

The Tamsagbulag Neolithic settlement site lies 7 km southeast of the center of former [Tamsagbulag](</destination/landscapes/tamsagbulag>) soum in [Dornod Province](/province/dornod) (aimag). A cold, clear spring flows from the foot of a 10–12 m high bank. A complete Neolithic dwelling and a burial were found here. ## What is the Tamsagbulag settlement site? Quick facts

- **Location:** Dornod Province, 7 km southeast of the center of former Tamsagbulag soum.
- **Dwelling:** rectangular, 42.5 m².
- **Burial:** under the north wall of the dwelling, in a seated position.
- **Date:** 4,000–3,000 BC.

## What was found at Tamsagbulag?

On a terrace with sand ridges, stretching about 2 km west and east from the head of the spring bank, Neolithic stone tools are common. Finds include flakes, blade flakes, and cores. The terrace wall shows a cultural layer of dark humus soil. This points to traces of the walls of an ancient dwelling. Excavation of one of these spots uncovered an almost complete dwelling. It is rectangular and covers 42.5 m².

### Finds from the dwelling

- a pointed core used to strike off blade flakes;
- a bone knife with a groove for inserting a blade;
- a bone arrowhead;
- a pendant made from a deer tooth.

## What was found in the burial?

- about 200 thin, round stone pendants with a hole in the middle;
- more than 20 tube-shaped pendants made by drilling the roots of deer canine teeth;
- two bone awls with thin, sharp points, made from the marrow bones of livestock, together with a needle;
- two bone knives, each 20 cm long and 2.5 cm wide, placed near the head.

Many Neolithic flint cores, flakes, blade flakes, scrapers, and awls came from the dwelling and the burial. They share common features with sites in Eastern Siberia, the Baikal region, and the Amur River area.

## Why is the Tamsagbulag settlement site important?

Archaeologists have found that the early history of Siberia and Central Asia shared many features in the Neolithic period, roughly 5,000–4,000 years ago. Of the several Neolithic settlements and burials found in Dornod, the Tamsagbulag burial holds the richest set of finds. It was found together with a complete dwelling. So it is an important source for studying the material and spiritual culture of Neolithic people in Mongolia, including their economy, social relations, beliefs, and rituals. Based on the finds from the dwelling and the burial, researchers dated them to 4,000–3,000 BC.

## Where did the Bugat Inscription get its name?

Archaeologist Ts. Dorjsuren found it in 1956 in Bugat bag, Ikhtamir soum, Arkhangai Province. That is why it is called the "Bugat Inscription."

## What is shown and written on the monument?

- **Wolf.** At the top is a carving of a wolf nursing a child.
- **Inscription.** Below it, Mongolian words are carved in Brahmi script on three sides.
- **Content.** It tells of the first khagans who founded the Turkic state, including Nivar, Buman, Mukhan and Taspar, and of events linked to them.

## How is the Ashina clan connected to the wolf?

The scholar L. Gumilev noted that the name of the Ashina clan, which founded the Turkic Empire, may be linked to a word meaning "wolf." According to him, the Ashina were of Xianbei origin and spoke Mongolian. Around 460 they moved to the Altai Mountains. There they came among the populous Oguz tribes and began to rule them. He suggested they then gradually lost their native Mongolian and were absorbed into the Turkic language of the Oguz tribes.

## Why is the Bugat Inscription important?

By deciphering the Bugat Inscription, scholars established that the language of the ancient Rouran was Mongolian. According to the sources:

- The oldest Japanese inscription dates to 697.
- The oldest Turkic inscription dates to 713.
- The oldest Korean inscription dates to 604–606.

The Mongolian inscription at Bugat was written earlier than all of these. This suggests that Mongolian may have been written down before other Altaic languages. Mongolian researchers say this monument needs special study and protection, and should be made known to future generations.

### Theories put forward by researchers

Rock paintings and petroglyphs are works by ancient people. They have been preserved in the territory of Mongolia from the Paleolithic era to the present day. ## What are Mongolia's rock paintings? A quick overview

- **Date:** They begin in the Paleolithic era. Most are from 5,000–3,000 years ago.
- **Subjects:** Wild animals, birds, hunting, livestock herding, farming, carts, and relations between people.
- **Significance:** A source that lets you "read" Mongolia's ancient history.
- **Research:** Four volumes on the rock paintings of two mountains in [Bayan-Ulgii](/province/bayan-ulgii), by an American husband-and-wife team of scholars.

## Why are rock paintings called "letters from ancient people"?

People who lived 5,000–3,000 years ago left us letters through rock paintings. You can read Mongolia's ancient history in these images. It was a clever way for ancient people to pass their history on to later generations.

## How did rock paintings change over time?

- **The oldest paintings.** People drew wild animals and birds as best they could.
- **Later paintings.** Their skill improved, and they began to show more of their own lives, not only animals.

Later paintings show:

- hunting;
- growing crops;
- taming animals;
- transport by ox cart;
- close relations between men and women.

Researchers remain fascinated by how skillfully these paintings depict the "ancestors" of today's vehicles, such as horse-drawn and ox carts.

## What can we learn from rock paintings?

Rock paintings and petroglyphs help answer these questions:

- when people began making tools, and what kinds;

## What is Khusliin Khar Mountain? Quick facts

- **Path:** 300 m long and 2.6 m wide to the summit, with two rest stops.

## How do people worship at Khusliin Khar Mountain?

The rituals differ for men and for women.

- **Women, children, and elders.** They climb about 100 m from the foot of the mountain to a small temple halfway up. There they perform the ritual for the sacred mountain (khairkhan).
- **Men.** They climb to the sacred summit and walk around the ovoo (sacred stone cairn). They offer half of the sarkhad (a ritual offering of alcohol) they brought and whisper their wishes. The other half is considered blessed, so they carry it back with them.

### Wish papers

Every visitor should write a wish on a piece of paper and burn it at the designated place. While it burns, people whisper their wish and pray.

## What did the ovoo on the summit look like?

The summit ovoo has long been decorated with an aravnai (a sacred juniper), sunshig (ritual banners), ganjir (a crown-like roof ornament), khadag (ceremonial silk scarf), and prayer flags of khiimori (a person's vital energy and good fortune). Before 1930, the top of the ovoo had a ganjir with a jeweled crown, like a khan's hat. "Khan" is the title given to this mountain. Researchers say about 120 mountains in Mongolia have been given titles such as khan, bogd, gun, beel, beis, and zaisan.

## What legends are told about Khusliin Khar Mountain?

### The horse that came back

A poor man lived at the foot of the mountain with his only horse. One day a wild animal ate the horse. The poor man worshipped the mountain and begged for his horse back. The next morning, it is said, a horse stood beside him. People also say that seven goats with khadag around their necks always graze on Khusliin Mountain.

### The spirit of the Noyon Khutagt

Legend says that an earlier incarnation of the Noyon Khutagt was summoned to Erlig (the lord of the underworld) to be judged for his father's sins. When he returned, his body had already been embalmed. The spirit, left without a body, is said to have vowed to fulfill the wishes of all beings and merged with this mountain. The khutagt was buried at the foot of this sacred mountain, which is counted among the tall ones in the Gobi and has crackling black rock. For this reason it is said to be called "Relics Mountain."
