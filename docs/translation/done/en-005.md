Saridag Monastery (the White City)
@@ FIELD seoTitle text
Saridag Monastery: The White City, Ulaanbaatar's Origin
@@ FIELD seoDescription textarea
Saridag Monastery (White City), founded by Undur Gegeen Zanabazar in 1654 in the Khentii Range: dig, finds, history and how to get there.
@@ FIELD region text
Khentii
@@ FIELD description richText
Saridag Monastery, also known as the White City, stands at the foot of Saridag Mountain in the Khentii Range. It lies deep in a remote taiga forest that people rarely visit. This is where the foundations of the first capital of Mongolia were laid. Scientists from the Institute of History and Archaeology of the Academy of Sciences are uncovering and studying the ruins of the city. The taiga is home to wildlife such as bears, roe deer, red deer, moose and squirrels that leap from branch to branch. You can reach it only by helicopter or on horseback. Even so, many rare and valuable historical finds have come out of this wilderness.

## What are the key facts about Saridag Monastery?

- **Other names:** White City; Tugsbayasgalant Nomyn Ikh Khuree; Ribogejelin Monastery.
- **Founded by:** Undur Gegeen Zanabazar.
- **Construction began:** 1654 (Year of the Wood Horse).
- **Construction finished:** 1686 (Year of the Fire Tiger), after 32 years of building.
- **Location:** Khentii Range, between Saridag, Songino Yan and Bukh Yan mountains, 2,000 m above sea level.
- **Buildings:** nine buildings, three stupas and a stone fortress wall.
- **Destroyed:** during the Khalkha–Oirat war.
- **Excavation:** Institute of History and Archaeology of the Academy of Sciences, led by Dr. S. Chuluun.

## Where is Saridag Monastery?

The monastery sits in the very heart of three large mountains: Bukh Yan, Songino Yan and Saridag. It faces the direction of the rising sun. It lies 2,000 m above sea level. Researchers flew over the surrounding mountains by plane and reportedly found no other place with this kind of setting. Two streams flow from Saridag Mountain and join the headwaters of the [Tuul River](</destination/heritage/place/tuul-gol>). Mongols first built their capital monastery in this beautiful place.

![image](asset:tic-img-377070aa430c963e)

## How do I get to the monastery?

According to Dr. S. Chuluun, the project leader of the excavation and director of the Institute of History and Archaeology, the excavation site is very hard to reach. There are three ways:

- **By car.** A Zil-131 truck needs two days to get there, and only just makes it. If it rains, vehicles often get stuck in the marshes, so you cannot go without careful preparation.
- **On horseback.** From around [Mungunmort soum](/province/tuv), you ride a full day through marshes and forest and arrive at Saridag at dusk. If you are delayed on the way, you will have to spend the night in the wilderness.
- **By helicopter.** An Mi-8 helicopter of the Armed Forces aviation unit in Nalaikh District follows the Tuul River basin for a little over an hour and lands at the foot of Bukh Yan Mountain. From there you walk uphill for a few kilometers to the excavation site.

## How did the excavation begin?

Dr. S. Chuluun first said that there were the ruins of a city in the forest. Historical sources suggested it was a city built by Undur Gegeen, and he argued it should be excavated. But when he looked for funding, nobody believed him. People thought a city in the deep forest was impossible, and no funder was found. So he took courage, got his institute to provide what funds it could, hired horses in the fall and rode to Saridag Mountain. It was snowy and cold. The next year he chose the dig site, drew a map of the city, and confirmed the surroundings and oral history. He came back with good results. He said that at first excavating the ruins of such a large city seemed unrealistic, and he even thought about stopping the project. He had never imagined that such finds would come out of the ground. In his words, the structure and layout of the city turned out to be even more valuable than the artifacts.

## Who studied Saridag Monastery, and when?

- **1915–1916.** A Russian expedition led by P.A. Witte first examined the site.
- **1923.** Researchers appointed by the Institute of Scripts and Books visited.
- **1995.** The Institute of History of the Academy of Sciences visited together with the Tuv Province museum.
- **2014.** After the excavation, an international conference was held together with the Ulaanbaatar city administration. E. Bat-Uul, Governor of the capital, saw the work as a fascinating study that would deepen the history of the capital. He gave it his full support, which opened the door to carrying out the project on a larger scale.

With the city administration's support, the team expanded the excavation it had started two years earlier. It carried out enough research to fully establish the city's structure and layout, as well as the key finds. The excavation showed that the site was the Tugsbayasgalant Nomyn Ikh Khuree, or White City, founded by Undur Gegeen Zanabazar in the 17th century. Many historical sources confirm this.

## What were the excavation conditions like?

The institute began excavating the main assembly temple, which may have been the largest of the monastery's nine buildings, two years earlier. It is said that no excavation had been carried out in such harsh conditions in more than 70 years. This is also the first time Mongolian scientists have carried out such a large national project on their own, without foreign researchers or investment. The ruins had burned down in the wilderness more than 300 years ago and had been untouched since. The Institute of History and Archaeology led the work, and more than 30 young people took part, including students from the Bogd Khan Museum and Ulaanbaatar University. They dug out the main assembly temple site in several stages and removed trees more than 300 years old. They stacked thousands of cubic meters of stone in neat cubes and piled the soil with great care outside the wall. This shows that archaeology is work with its own procedures and strict discipline. The collapsed city ruins were covered by 300 years of soil, with vegetation growing over them.

## What did the White City look like?

The whole city was fenced with wood. A rounded earth embankment inside the fence shows that an earth wall once surrounded the monastery. Inside the earth wall, a stone wall 1.5 m wide and more than two meters high was identified. Near the wall, traces of a footpath of flat stones set into the ground were found. The wall stones were split along the natural rock seams and laid neatly with the flat sides facing out. The main wall was built of packed earth, lined on the outside with finely dressed stone, and covered with a fairly thick layer of white lime. This is probably why the city was called "white." Mongols knew of Tsogt Taij's White House and the ruins of Prince Erdenebaatar's white city. This excavation has now proven that Undur Gegeen's White City, founded in the Khan Khentii mountains in the 17th century, stood at the foot of Saridag Mountain.

- **Stupa.** Traces of a stupa were found on the city's white wall. The stupa is said to resemble the white stupa at Erdene Zuu Monastery in Uvurkhangai. Items made of ravnai (a consecrated mixture used in ritual deposits) were found inside, fully intact.
- **Gate.** People entered through a gate on a high platform in the stone wall. The excavation uncovered a wooden gate and its hinges.
- **Floor.** An area of more than 100 square meters was paved with split, flattened stones set into the soil.
- **Buildings.** Nine buildings, three stupas and a stone wall have been identified. Some buildings stood on specially prepared high stone platforms. Each temple had stairs on two sides leading from the high platform to the entrance. A large main assembly temple stood in the middle, surrounded by other buildings.

![image](asset:tic-img-7eb84feb2f4e10ad)

## What was found in the main assembly temple?

The northwest part of the excavated main assembly temple stands on a special platform and had seven rooms. At the top of the collapsed stone wall there was a ventilation window. More than two meters of wall remained above it, which shows the building had two stories. Many pottery fragments with decoration, bricks, nails made by Mongolian methods and iron wire were found. These show that between 1654 and 1687 Mongols made their own bricks, pottery, nails and iron wire. The roof was covered with tiles, and the tiles were decorated with various ornaments. The temple floor had been packed with more than ten centimeters of soil. On top of it lay square, plank-like cut timber, preserved just as it was. At a depth of more than three meters, clay Buddha figures 15 cm tall, seated cross-legged on lotus bases, began to appear in large numbers. They were identified as the Five Root Buddhas. Among the figures that were broken and crumbled when the building collapsed, about 1,000 have been restored. Hundreds more need restoration, and many more are likely to come out in the future.

## What rare finds have been uncovered?

- **Tripod and cauldron.** A bronze cauldron stood on a four-legged tripod. Inside it was a porcelain jar with a base, and next to it a solid silver bowl. The cauldron held a rice-like grain, walnuts and fruit with large pits. On top were thin silver plates, stuck together and broken into many pieces, but with clearly visible images of Buddhas. Inside and outside the cauldron, researchers found beads of many colors, fragments of small silver items, silver coins and pieces of thin gold foil.
- **Armor and weapons.** North of the tripod and cauldron, they found a warrior's iron plate armor chest piece, an iron helmet with a plated neck guard, and two swords. A flintlock pistol had been placed outside the sword on the left, but its wooden part had rotted away.
- **Eight auspicious offerings.** The most important find is the base of a Buddha statue one meter across. In front of it, on a wooden railing, were four of the clay eight auspicious offerings: the pair of fish, the vase, the lotus and the golden eight-spoked wheel.
- **The great statue.** A clay arm 130 cm long and a leg fragment 120 cm long, buried for hundreds of years, came out first. A very large head and a chest section with fine, skillful decoration were found next. This seems to have been the main statue of the assembly temple. Researchers concluded that it stood on a base decorated with lotus petals. The arms, legs, nails and fingers are very finely made. Dozens of small Buddhas were found beside it, so the first hypothesis is that it may have been a split Five Root Buddhas figure.

In this way, many precious cultural treasures from the 1600s came out of the ground, including warriors' clothing and equipment, swords, silver bowls, hammers and anvils.

## Who took part in the excavation?

The team included archaeologists E. Urtnasan and Ch. Enkhtuul, clay restorer N. Nandintsetseg, Buddhist art researcher Gunchin-Ish of the Bogd Khan Museum, oral history researcher and ethnologist G. Byambaragchaa, and architecture restoration researcher Batsooj. According to restorer N. Nandintsetseg, the finds clearly show how high the skill of Mongols was. Hundreds of Buddhas have been found, but each one has a different, unrepeatable design. Many Five Root Buddhas were found, yet their eyes and base petals differ. One has nine petals, another has 11, and so on. After about 400 years underground, the finds are covered with mold and bacteria. The team neutralizes them with alcohol and then strengthens the pieces. Student archaeologists who took part said that at first they carried stones and soil from morning till night and felt discouraged. At first, the wild taiga seemed frightening. Nothing moved except birds circling in the sky, and the nearest family lived 70 km away. Later the work settled into a rhythm. They said they were glad to take part in the largest independent excavation in Mongolian archaeology, in the hardest conditions the field has seen, and to learn so much. They also said it is no wonder the work is hard, since they are excavating a city built over 32 years by the labor of many thousands of people.

## Why is Saridag considered the original site of Ulaanbaatar?

According to Dr. S. Chuluun, many oral and written records show that Undur Gegeen Zanabazar founded Saridag Monastery here.

- **"Gegeen Toli" (The Bright Mirror).** The noted Khalkha religious figure Zaya Bandida Luvsanperenlei finished writing the biography of his teacher Undur Gegeen Zanabazar in Tibetan in 1698–1702. The work says that when Zanabazar told the Panchen Lama he wanted to go to the Ü-Tsang region to study, the Panchen Lama advised him to study there. He added that it would be more useful for religion and living beings if Zanabazar founded a monastic community in Mongolia. Accordingly, in the Year of the Wood Horse (1654), he began to found Ribogejelin Monastery at a place called Khentei Khan. It is clear that after returning from Tibet, Zanabazar laid the foundation of Saridag Monastery in 1654 at the foot of Khentii Khan, under the name Ribogejelin, or in its full name Ribogejagandanshaduvlin.
- **"Bogd Jebtsundamba's First Incarnation Record."** This later Mongolian source notes that in the 11th year of the Eeer Zasagch (Ruling by Peace) era, the Year of the Blue Horse (1654), the Bogd Jebtsundamba founded the Great Monastery of Dharma, Ribogejai-gandanshadublin, at the foot of Khentii Mountain in Khalkha land to spread Yellow Hat Buddhism.

These sources suggest that the White City, or Saridag Monastery, founded by Undur Gegeen, later moved several times. It settled as the Khuree in the Tuul River valley and finally became the capital, Ulaanbaatar. In other words, Undur Gegeen can be seen as the founder of today's [Ulaanbaatar](/province/ulaanbaatar) (formerly Khuree, or Ikh Khuree). Mongolian chronologies and the chronicles of Ikh Khuree do not treat either 1639, when Undur Gegeen was named head of Khalkha Buddhism, or 1651, when the first seven aimags of the Khuree were established, as the founding year of Ikh Khuree. Instead, by Ikh Khuree tradition, 1654 is officially regarded as the founding year of Ikh Khuree. That was the Year of the Blue Horse, when a permanent monastery was established at Khentii Khan. Today the anniversary of Ulaanbaatar is counted from this year.

- **A document from 1873.** In a letter sent by the authorities of Ikh Khuree, Khamba Nomun Khan and Erdene Shanzodba, to the lamas of the monastery's Vangai aimag, the founding year of Ikh Khuree is given as 219 years after the first spread of religion and the founding of the main assembly in the Year of the Wood Horse (1654).
- **The 1925 charter.** The preface to the officially approved charter and internal rules of Ikh Khuree also gives 1654 as the year Ikh Khuree was first founded.

Dr. S. Chuluun stresses that Undur Gegeen Zanabazar first founded the capital monastery in the remote forest at the foot of Saridag Mountain. It then moved several times before reaching the site of today's Ulaanbaatar.

## How many years did it take to build the White City?

There is evidence that Undur Gegeen built the White City over 32 years. In Zaya Bandida's "Biography of Undur Gegeen," he wrote that he returned to Mongolia in the last winter month of the Year of the Earth Sheep (1679). In the summer of the Year of the Iron Monkey (1680), he went to pay respects to the Bogd Gegeen. By then most of the monastery's outer parts were finished, and the statues were being made. He stayed about ten days and received teachings and initiation. He also wrote that in the summer of the Year of the Fire Tiger (1686), when the statues of Ribogejelin Monastery were completed, some lamas were asked to prepare ravnai. This evidence shows that building Saridag Monastery lasted 32 years, from 1654 to the summer of 1686.

## How was the White City destroyed?

Legend says that the golden roof of Saridag Monastery gleamed in the sunlight. The city was destroyed during the Khalkha–Oirat war. It is said that Oirat soldiers could not find the White City and searched the forest for several days. In the end the top of the main assembly temple glowed yellow in the sunlight. The excavation found evidence for this legend. The temple roof was made of tiles fitted together. The edges were gilded and decorated with scroll patterns, and the rest was polished with bronze, giving the roof a golden-yellow top. The ruins of this whole city, with huge amounts of evidence of Mongolian culture, history and skill, keep emerging in the Khentii Range day by day. You can see works by Undur Gegeen Zanabazar at the [Zanabazar Museum of Fine Arts](</things-to-do/museums/zanabazaryn-neremjit-durslekh-urlagiin-muzei>).

## What is the timeline of Saridag Monastery?

- **1639.** Undur Gegeen is named head of Khalkha Buddhism.
- **1651.** The first seven aimags of the Khuree are established.
- **1654.** The foundation of Saridag Monastery is laid at the foot of Khentii Khan. This year is counted as the founding year of Ikh Khuree.
- **1679.** Zaya Bandida Luvsanperenlei returns to Mongolia.
- **1680.** Most of the monastery's outer parts are built and the statues are being made.
- **1686.** The statues are completed and ravnai is prepared.
- **1873.** A letter from the Ikh Khuree authorities notes that 219 years have passed since 1654.
- **1915–1916.** A Russian expedition led by P.A. Witte first studies the site.
- **1923.** Researchers from the Institute of Scripts and Books visit.
- **1925.** The Ikh Khuree charter gives 1654 as the founding year.
- **1995.** The Institute of History of the Academy of Sciences and the Tuv Province museum visit together.
- **2014.** An international conference is held together with the Ulaanbaatar city administration.

## Why is Saridag Monastery important?

This excavation is special because it ties the history of the Mongolian capital back to the middle of the 17th century. It also proves the origin of Ulaanbaatar on the ground. It is also one of the largest national excavations that Mongolian scientists have carried out on their own, without foreign investment. The Buddha figures, bricks, tiles, nails and iron items show the skill of Mongolian craftsmen of the time. The city's layout, walls, stupas and temples on platforms give rare information about town planning in Undur Gegeen's time.

## What should travelers know before going?

Saridag Monastery is hard to reach and lies in uninhabited taiga. The nearest family lives 70 km away, so do not go alone. Travel with an experienced guide and well-prepared transport. In the rainy season the road turns to marsh, and vehicles risk getting stuck.

Kharkhorum City
@@ FIELD seoTitle text
Kharkhorum city: Capital of the Great Mongol State
@@ FIELD seoDescription textarea
Kharkhorum city was the capital of the Great Mongol State, expanded by Ogedei Khaan in 1235. Learn about the Silver Tree, the great temple and excavations.
@@ FIELD body richText
Kharkhorum city was the capital of the Great Mongol State. Today its ruins lie in what is now [Kharkhorin soum](/province/uvurkhangai/kharkhorin). Travelers and historians from countries thousands of kilometers away recorded the history of the Mongols, and their accounts made Kharkhorum famous around the world. This is what makes the city unusual: much of Mongolian history is confirmed through the notes of others.

## What are the key facts about Kharkhorum city?

- **Former names:** Karakorum, Kharkhorum; today Kharkhorin.
- **Founded:** 8th century.
- **Became a capital:** In the time of Chinggis Khaan (Genghis Khan); expanded by Ogedei Khaan from 1235.
- **Time as capital:** 32 of its 140 years of existence.
- **Main buildings:** Tumen Amgalant Palace (1236), the Tsogt Great Temple (completed in 1256).
- **Destroyed:** By Ming invasions in 1380 and 1466.
- **Research:** From 1889; excavations in 1948–1949 and 1976–1980; Mongolian-German expedition from 1999.

## What does the name Kharkhorum mean?

In ancient times the city was called Karakorum, later Kharkhorum, and today Kharkhorin. Karakorum is a Uighur name of Altaic origin. From the time of Chinggis Khaan it came to be called Kharkhorum. The name comes from the Mongolian words "khar khurem," and Karakorum also means "black khurem" in Mongolian. According to the scholar Ts. Shugel, a kind of hard stone formed by volcanic eruption is called "khorum" in Uighur and "khurem" in Mongolian. So the name would mean "Khar Khurem" rather than "Kharkhorum." Many scholars take this view, but they have not yet reached a common conclusion.

## How did Kharkhorum city begin?

The city's foundation was laid in the 8th century. In the 13th century, Wang Khan of the Kereit was based here, and for a time it served as a Mongol military camp and weapons depot. Later Chinggis Khaan, ruler of the Great Mongol State, made it the center of his state's capital. [Ogedei Khaan](</about/history/people/ugedei-khaan>) expanded the city from 1235 and had a wall built around it. Inside the wall, on a specially prepared high platform, he built a large palace called Tumen Amgalant. It had a stone foundation and a great hall with 64 columns.

## What was Tumen Amgalant Palace like?

- Its floor was made of green tiles, and its walls were decorated with various paintings.
- The palace is said to have been 55–80 meters wide.
- Each column was decorated with golden ornaments, and the roof was covered with red and green tiles.
- The Yuan chronicles record that a ceremony marking the completion of Tumen Amgalant Palace was held at Tsagaan Sar (Lunar New Year) in 1236, the Red Monkey year.

After this, many palaces and mansions were built in Kharkhorum for Ogedei Khaan's children and relatives.

![image](asset:tic-img-5f14718600b0680c)

## What is the Silver Tree?

In Kharkhorum, it was common to carve stone for palace steps, column bases, memorial steles and the turtle-shaped bases beneath them. About 20 kilometers west of the city, in a gorge of the Orkhon River called Elstei, there was a deposit of fine, high-quality granite. Finds show that in the 13th century a large workshop there prepared building stone. In the time of Munkh Khaan, the French craftsman William of Rubruck's countryman Guillaume Boucher made a silver tree at the palace entrance that poured four kinds of drinks. At the top of the tree stood an angel blowing a trumpet. During feasts the trumpet sounded, and four drinks poured from the mouths of four dragons facing the four directions, collecting in silver basins below. The four drinks were wine, airag (fermented mare's milk), mead and rice beer, and servants offered them to guests. In 1253 the famous traveler Guillaume de Rubruck came to Kharkhorin, was received by Munkh Khaan and visited Tumen Amgalant Palace. He wrote about it in his book _Journey to the Eastern Parts of the World_.

## What was Kharkhorum city like?

Kharkhorum city was surrounded by a clay wall four kilometers long on each side, with four gates, one on each side. Historical sources note that different goods were traded at each gate:

- **East gate** — grain;
- **West gate** — sheep and goats;
- **South gate** — cattle and ox carts;
- **North gate** — riding horses and horse carts.

At the four corners of the great wall stood four turtle guardians, and the imperial banner was raised above them. The great Mongol khaans did not discriminate by religion. So in Kharkhorum, 12 temples and shrines of Buddhist, Christian and Muslim faiths held services. There was also a tall five-story stupa, begun by Ogedei Khaan and completed in the time of Munkh Khaan. The city had two large quarters: one for craftsmen and one with a large Islamic-style market street. Using craftsmen and smiths brought from conquered lands, the Mongol khaans built palaces, temples, monasteries and monuments that combined Western, Eastern and Mongolian styles. Russians, Georgians, Arabs, Chinese, Italians and French lived in Kharkhorum. The city became a hub linking the countries of East and West in politics, economy and culture.

## What was produced in Kharkhorum?

Production in the city was limited, but people grew grain, fired glazed brick and tiles, and cut and polished mountain stone. There were also workshops for iron, gold and silver. Thanks to tribute collected from all subject lands, the city's residents lived in wealth and luxury. One third of the city's total area was reserved for officials who ran the empire. They included scribes and translators from all the nations of the empire.

## How was Kharkhorum destroyed?

The city burned in 1215 and 1268, but each time it was rebuilt. In 1380 and 1466, however, it suffered heavy damage from invasions by China's Ming state. After that, it was destroyed completely during repeated Ming military attacks and the unrest among domestic lords. In its 140 years of existence, Kharkhorum was the capital of the Mongol Empire for 32 years.

![image](asset:tic-img-6b939b7fb3050efc)

## What is the Tsogt Great Temple?

In 1948–1949, a Mongolian-Soviet research team led by Kh. Perlee and S. Kiselev partly excavated the remains of a large building at the center of a walled complex in the southwest corner inside the city's great wall. They identified it as Ogedei Khaan's "Tumen Amgalant Palace." However, research by the joint Mongolian-German archaeological expedition showed with much evidence that this building was not a royal palace but a huge Buddhist temple. The temple's foundation was laid in 1235–1236 and completed in 1256. In 1346, by decree of Togoon Temur Khaan, it was given the name "Pavilion Glorifying the Yuan State," or "House of the Tsogt Great Temple." The Yuan chronicles and the 1346 Kharkhorum inscription record this temple as one of the city's earliest buildings. The 1346 stele inscription also mentions that the temple was restored in 1311 and 1346. In 1311, Buyant Khaan (1311–1320) heard that the temple in Kharkhorum was damaged. He gave the necessary funds to Chogsum, an official of the department in charge of Buddhism, and had it repaired. Later, under Togoon Temur Khaan (1333–1368), repairs lasted four years. At the end of 1346 the temple was given the name "Pavilion of Prosperity of the Yuan State, Established by Decree," or "House of the Tsogt Great Temple."

## What was found in 2014?

In 2014, the joint Mongolian-German "Orkhon" expedition found a vase under the temple's platform and foundation. It held a foundation deposit made by the great khaans of the 13th century. This was a new discovery for ancient Mongolian history and archaeology. The deposit contained grain, various fruits, and "nine treasures" wrapped in silk with gold thread: two gold coins and three silver coins of the great khaans of the Mongol state, coral, pearls and turquoise. They were placed in a large ceramic jar, and its mouth was sealed with a clay lid. One of the gold coins was minted in Samarkand for Chinggis Khaan. The silver coins were minted in Kharkhorum in the time of Munkh Khaan. The items, made of nine kinds of materials, carry a careful symbolism meant to give life and support to a stupa or sacred object. This find fully proved that the building was a Buddhist temple of the 13th–14th centuries.

## How has Kharkhorum been studied?

- **1889.** The Russian scholar N. M. Yadrintsev found the remains of a large city near [Erdene Zuu Monastery](</destination/heritage/place/erdenezuu-khiid>).
- **1890.** V. V. Radlov studied the monastery wall and found a stone slab with a royal decree linked to Kharkhorum. This confirmed the city's location.
- **1948–1949.** A Mongolian-Soviet expedition led by Kh. Perlee, Mongolia's first archaeologist, and Soviet professor S. V. Kiselev carried out the first excavation since the city's fall. They found many iron household tools, clay items and porcelain. Craftsmen of that time left their handprints on their work as a guarantee of quality. Building materials, roof parts and monuments were also uncovered.
- **1976–1980.** A team from the Institute of History of the Academy of Sciences found a Mongolian burial and a Muslim burial near the city.
- **1995–1996.** With UNESCO support, Mongolian and Japanese scientists defined the city's area and made a detailed topographic map.
- **From 1999.** Under an agreement between the presidents of the two countries, the Institute of Archaeology of the Mongolian and German Academies of Sciences and the Institute of Central Asia of the University of Bonn are running a joint project. They are studying all finds in detail by laboratory methods.

In front of Ogedei Khaan's 64-column Tumen Amgalant Palace, remains of a kiln used to fire clay and porcelain were found. The remains of a stone-paved street showed that the city's main street had a stone road. In an excavation covering 6,000 square meters, the soil reached a depth of 7 meters, and a large part of the palace foundation was uncovered. About 230 coins were found in total: 10 from the palace excavation, 54 from the city's east gate and 129 from the main street intersection. Studying them showed that the city traded with more than 10 countries.

## Can you see the Tsogt Great Temple remains?

Under the patronage of the presidents of Mongolia and Germany, and as a result of long-term cultural and archaeological cooperation between the two governments, work was carried out in Kharkhorin soum, Uvurkhangai Province (aimag), to protect the remains of the Tsogt Great Temple of Kharkhorum and open them to visitors. The project was funded by Germany's Federal Foreign Office, the Gerda Henkel Foundation, the German Archaeological Institute and Mongolia's Ministry of Education, Culture, Science and Sports. As part of it, the temple's archaeological remains were protected. Based on research, the platform wall and the stairs leading up to it were rebuilt to UNESCO standards. The work was led by the project head of the joint Mongolian-German "Kharkhorum Expedition," Professor Dr. Christina Franken, and Professor Dr. U. Erdenebat. For about the past 20 years, this expedition has excavated around Kharkhorum. It has clarified many questions raised by scholars and information from written sources. By identifying many stages of settlement in the capital, it also holds an important place in research on the urbanization and settlement history of Central Asian nomads. The project began in 2014 as part of the 40th anniversary of diplomatic relations between Germany and Mongolia. It aims to turn the temple remains into an open-air museum for the public.

## Was Kharkhorum the center of the khaans' policy?

Kharkhorum city is known as the capital of the Mongol Empire. However, many scholars, relying on historical sources, believe it was not the center of the khaans' political decision-making.

- **Plano Carpini.** Plano Carpini set out for Kharkhorum, the capital of the world, on July 22, 1246. When he arrived, a feast was under way to enthrone Guyug. But Guyug Khaan held the feast in the steppe, in a large tent camp, not in the capital. The khaan of such a vast land could feast anywhere. Even so, Carpini is said to have wondered what the khaan actually did in his famous capital.
- **Guillaume de Rubruck.** On December 26, 1253, the French priest Guillaume de Rubruck came to see Munkh Khaan. The khaan was not in the capital then either. As Rubruck learned, the Mongol khaans came to Kharkhorum only twice a year to hold feasts.

By their observations, the center of Mongol state policy was the Mongolian steppe, not Kharkhorum. Kharkhorum was a city where merchants from every land gathered. It was a peaceful city where followers of many religions debated in harmony. It was also a treasury for the gold and jewels the khaans gathered while conquering the world, and a workshop for the craftsmen and smiths they brought with them.

## Why is Kharkhorum important?

Kharkhorum was the capital of a world empire built by nomads, and a center of trade and culture linking East and West. ## What is the history of the museum building?

The building was constructed in 1905 as a trading house, funded by the Russian merchant Gudvintsel. At the time, it was the tallest building in Khuree. ## What is in the Ancient Art gallery?

This gallery has six rooms. It holds many monuments, including stone and bronze weapons, deer stones, Xiongnu (Hunnu) pottery, and wall paintings from a Uighur temple.

- **Rock paintings.** A copy by the artist O. Amgalan of the rock paintings in the Khoid Tsenkher Cave in Mankhan soum, Khovd Province. These are among the oldest rock paintings found in Mongolia. - **Karakhoja and Turfan temple paintings.** Four-armed Bodhisattva, the Buddha Vajravarahi, figures of bodhisattvas, a stone head of the Buddha, a portrait of a clergyman, and a portrait of a nobleman. ## Who was B. Sharav?

Works such as "The Birth of Offspring," "The Winter Palace of the Bogd Khan," and "Green Tara" still amaze people today.

## What is the composition of Galt's spring water?

In 2007, a joint expedition studied the spring's composition. It included the Institute of the Earth's Crust of the Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences and the Institute of Chemistry and Chemical Technology of the Mongolian Academy of Sciences. The research found that the water is sulfate and sulfate-carbonate sodium, contains 0.83 grams of minerals per liter, and reaches +38 °C to +44 °C. The main features named in the source are summarized below.

- **Fluorine.** The spring contains 9.4 mg of fluoride ions per liter, which is a high level. Both too much and too little fluoride affect the teeth, so the hot spring water is not taken internally.
- **Alkaline environment.** Alkaline water with a pH of 8.8 is said to interact with the skin and increase its elasticity. For this reason, it is described as suitable for many skin conditions.
- **Sulfide.** The source says that when sulfide water is applied to specific parts of the body, blood supply to the mucous membranes increases and the function of the ciliated epithelium becomes more active. - **Silicic acid.** The high silicon content is one of the main reasons the spring is used for treatment. Silicon compounds are described as affecting metabolic processes, neutralizing harmful compounds, and having an antiparasitic effect.

## What roles do potassium and calcium play in the body?

When the source explains the value of minerals in the spring water, it highlights potassium and calcium. Many body functions depend on potassium ions. Each time a muscle contracts, potassium ions leave the muscle, and when the contraction stops, they return, which gives the muscle strength. A lack of potassium shows up as loss of appetite, slowed growth, weaker nerve impulses, joint pain, weight loss, and poor heart function. Calcium takes part in blood clotting, reducing poisoning, bringing down fever, calming nerve irritation, strengthening the body, and protecting against infection. It also reduces the toxicity of excess potassium, sodium, and magnesium, and plays an important role in improving iron metabolism and absorption. A lack of calcium makes bones soft and brittle, and causes muscle and joint pain.

Demchog Monastery is in [Khanbogd soum](/province/umnugov/khanbogd) in [Umnugovi Province (aimag)](/province/umnugov). It is one of the eight monasteries that Noyon Khutagt D. Danzanravjaa founded in the Gobi. Together, the three monasteries he built, Demchog, Ereet and Tsagaan Tolgoi, are called the "[Galbyn Three Monasteries](</destination/heritage/place/galbyn-gurvan-khiid>)." The word Demchog means "supreme bliss."

## What are the key facts about Demchog Monastery?

- **Location:** Khanbogd soum, Umnugovi Province. It lies 14 km northeast of Khanbogd Mountain.
- **Founder:** Noyon Khutagt D. Danzanravjaa.
- **Built:** The Galbyn Three Monasteries were built between 1830 and 1836.
- **Purpose:** pilgrimage and worship, purification, and meditation.
- **Highlights:** the Avid Stupa, the eight stupas, the White Stupa of Narkhajod, Norov Rock (Beleg Rock) and the Energy Seat.
- **Model of the monastery:** kept in the Khanbogd soum local museum.

## How was the site of Demchog Monastery chosen?

Two legends tell how the site was chosen. In the first, a "purev" was launched from Khamar Monastery, and the monastery was built where it pointed. The purev was a compass-like object that weighed 350 grams and had a sharp iron tip. In the second legend, in 1828 Danzanravjaa and his disciples were holding a ceremony to worship the mineral spring "Chu Meg Jarz" near the Gurvan Bayan spring. A thick, brown, spotted snake crawled up to them. The khutagt (high lama) took this as a good omen and said, "The lus (water spirit) of Galbyn Mountain has invited me." After the ceremony, he had his disciple Choimbol carry the snake back to Khanbogd Mountain. As the snake left, he told the disciples, "Observe the land around Galbyn carefully and bring back a map." While drawing the map, the disciples marked an oddly shaped rock outcrop. The khutagt studied the map closely and said, "I must go to the Galbyn mountains." Soon after, he reached Khanbogd Mountain. Danzanravjaa meditated for a week near Khanbogd Khairkhan (sacred mountain) and rode around the area for thirty days. Then he gathered the local people. He praised the land as a pure and blessed place that does not stir bad thoughts in people and where wishes come true. He also described it as a sacred mountain with abundant medicinal plants on its surface and rich minerals beneath it. He said it was a land that would keep people and animals alive even in the hardest times. He added, "This is not a place I will visit and leave. I must spend part of my life here." He then composed a praise poem for Khanbogd Mountain called "Wondrously Formed."

## How are the Galbyn Three Monasteries arranged?

For the benefit of all future beings, the khutagt studied the land and built three monasteries between 1830 and 1836. They stand 5–6 km apart, arranged in the shape of a wish-fulfilling jewel.

- **Demchog Monastery.** The khutagt spent autumn here and held pilgrimage, worship and meditation. The "Demchog assembly" was held on the equinox days. The "Khanjid Demchog" assembly was held on the 15th and 25th of the first spring month.
- **Tsagaan Tolgoi Monastery.** The khutagt spent summer here. This monastery was meant for the lay faithful. It is said that passing through the Kharankhui Gorge and the Gegeetiin Gorge on the way there has a special meaning.
- **Ereet Monastery.** The khutagt studied the scriptures of the Red tradition and gave teachings here.

Danzanravjaa spent about twenty years of his life in this area.

## What did Demchog Monastery look like?

The ruins show that the monastery had an outer wall. Inside it, a second wall enclosed the **main temple, also called Demchog**. The temple was a two-story building with a balcony in Tibetan style. Its architecture was unusual. The floor was insulated, and channels ran through the walls so that hot steam could warm the building. Assemblies were held on the first floor of the main temple, and a library was on the second floor. The main gate had stone steps on both sides and was decorated with many kinds of patterns and scrolls. Four small buildings stood in front of and behind the main temple. Above it stood a building with two windows and a small wall with stupas on both sides. Noyon Khutagt himself spent the winters in the small building to the northeast. There was even a special enclosure for resting the horses and camels of pilgrims who came from far away, along with herders to look after them. It is said that Demchog Monastery was one of three monasteries in Mongolia that kept relics of the Buddha.

## What do the Avid Stupa and the eight stupas symbolize?

North of the temple, inside a walled courtyard, stood a large multi-story stupa with eight small stupas around it. The large stupa is the **Avid Stupa**. Avid is a buddha of the paradise realm and is regarded as a form of [Maidar Burkhan](/about/culture/religion/maidar-burkhan), the future Buddha. People worship him in the belief that he helps souls leaving the human world to be reborn well. The temple on the lower floor of the Avid Stupa held an image of [Lovon Badamjunai](/about/culture/religion/lovon-badamjunai), the founder of the Red tradition, or Nimavag. When praying, people usually recite Lovon Badamjunai's heart mantra, "Om ah hum vajra guru padma siddhi hum." After that, they walk clockwise around the eight surrounding stupas. The tradition of eight stupas comes from India. Two hundred years after the passing of the [Buddha](</about/culture/religion/burkhan-bagsh>), the Indian king Ashoka built eight stupas representing the eight petals of a lotus flower. They survive to this day. The eight stupas symbolize eight important events in the Buddha's life:

- Descending from the Gandan heaven to the continent of Zambuu
- Entering his mother's womb
- Being born
- Mastering the arts and sciences
- Delighting his queen and companions
- Leaving home to become a monk
- Practicing austerities
- Attaining enlightenment under the Bodhi tree

Some legends say the eight stupas were built to symbolize the Buddha's eight great wishes.

## What is special about the White Stupa of Narkhajod?

The stupa that stands alone to the northwest is called the **White Stupa of Narkhajod**. Unlike the others, you walk around it counterclockwise. People divide sins into three kinds: those committed by body, by speech and by mind. When walking counterclockwise around the stupa three times, they pray to purify these three kinds of sins. Narkhajod is a wrathful female deity of the Red tradition and the female form of [Demchog Burkhan](/about/culture/religion/demchog-burkhan).

## What is Norov Rock, or Beleg Rock?

In the northern part of the monastery stands **Norov Rock**, also called Beleg Rock, which is shaped like a man's genitals. Demchog Monastery was founded on the southern slope of this very rock. The outcrop is 27 m tall. It is one of three phallic rocks of this kind that have been studied in Mongolia. In 1830, Danzanravjaa found the rock and honored it. He read scriptures, burned juniper incense and spent a whole day on top of the outcrop. Then he said, "I will worship this rock with a lotus flower." He had the main temple of Demchog Monastery built in the shape of a lotus flower, which represents a woman. When his disciples asked about the rock, the khutagt said it gives men strength and happiness. He said it gives women good children and a good partner, and that it balances out what is lacking in a person's fate. According to legend, he once threw a vessel of water to the top of the rock. When it fell back down, it had turned into vodka, and he gave it to the gathered people to taste.

## How are the Energy Seat and the song "Ulemjiin Chanar" connected?

Noyon Khutagt often climbed a rock called Adistai next to the Demchog temple. He sat there to restore his khiimori (a person's vital energy and good fortune), take in the scenery and gaze at Khanbogd Khairkhan. Locals passed this story along by word of mouth, and today the rock is called the **Energy Seat**. It is said that if you meditate on this seat and sing the khutagt's song **"Ulemjiin Chanar,"** you gain merit equal to reciting the mantra of Mother Dara 1,000 times and the Khiimori Offering 100 times. People believe the voice is a key sign of health, so singing this song is said to develop the throat chakra. While meditating on Khanbogd Mountain, the khutagt saw in a dream a beautiful young woman riding a white camel and playing a flute. He took her to be the guardian spirit of the mountain. He said, "A person need not be born with magic. Just come here and you will find it." He then composed the song "Ulemjiin Chanar" for her. Each verse has five lines, which represent the five elements. The song's lyrics (translated) are: Perfect is the supreme quality, Its color like a clear mirror, khu. Your beautiful, lovely face, khu. I could look and look and never have enough, ya. It captures my heart, ze. You bloom from the middle of the lotus. I am drawn to the taste of honey, khu. Your joyful, lovely face, khu. I could admire it and admire it and never have enough, ya. It captures my heart, ze. You stir a stubborn heart. Your voice is like the cuckoo's call, Your cheerful, lovely face, khu. I could long for it and long for it and never have enough, ya. It captures my heart, ze.

## What do people say about the energy point at Demchog Monastery?

Locals say an energy flow runs through the area between Khanbogd Mountain and the monastery. In 2007, a team of scientists led by Dr. Dashnyam visited the monastery to identify its energy points and zones. They identified the rock platform where the khutagt set up his writing desk as the central energy point. They also determined that an energy zone extends within a 15 km radius from it.

## How was Demchog Monastery restored?

Demchog Monastery was the most memorable site in terms of fine architecture. The restoration therefore aims to keep its original layout and distinctive form. The restoration was funded by Ivanhoe Mines Mongolia Inc., which ran the Oyu Tolgoi project, along with Tavan Tolgoi LLC and Ajnai Trade LLC. The Buddhist lama G. Purevbat built the stupas and led the restoration of the monastery.

## How do you make a pilgrimage at Demchog Monastery?

Today, pilgrims who come to the monastery usually follow this order.

- Visit the Avid Stupa first. It holds the image of Lovon Badamjunai.
- Walk counterclockwise three times around the Narkhajod stupa, which bears a Soyombo symbol.
- Walk around Beleg Rock and entrust your wishes to it.
- Walk clockwise around the eight small stupas, starting from the one to the northeast, the stupa of the Buddha's birth. Then finish by walking clockwise around the large Avid Stupa, which symbolizes the paradise realm.
- Go to the Energy Rock, also called Danzanravjaa's Seat. One spot on the seat is called the "energy center point." Here, people sing the full song "Ulemjiin Chanar."
- Walk clockwise around the foundation of Ravjaa Khutagt's ger, which lies below the rock.
- Finish with "energy protection." Place both palms on the rock with white, yellow and red khadag (ceremonial silk scarves) and meditate for one minute with your eyes closed. People also lie, sit or lean on nearby rocks for one minute. They meditate and ask for healing of ailments of the head, neck, back, kidneys and brain.

On a trip to Demchog Monastery, you can also visit the other two Galbyn monasteries, Ereet and Tsagaan Tolgoi. You can see the model of the monastery in the Khanbogd soum local museum.

The Rare Books Museum is part of the [National Library](</things-to-do/museums/undesnii-nomyn-san>) in [Ulaanbaatar](/province/ulaanbaatar). It was founded in 1981 as the "Rare Books Mini Museum" to show Mongolia's ancient written heritage to Mongolian and foreign visitors. Its treasures include a sutra said to exist in only one copy in the world, the longest and smallest books, and a sutra with silver pages.

![image](asset:tic-img-39f5315852595925)

## What is the Rare Books Museum? Quick facts

- **Founded:** 1981.
- **Location:** National Library, Chinggis Avenue-4, Ulaanbaatar.
- **Main collection:** the two great sacred collections, the Ganjuur (108 volumes) and the Danjuur (226 volumes).
- **Registered with UNESCO:** the Mongolian Danjuur written in cinnabar and the Lu. Altan Tobchi (2011).
- **Please note:** the exhibits are registered in the state's unique treasure fund, so photography is prohibited by law.

## What are the Ganjuur and the Danjuur?

The core of the collection is two great sacred collections: the Ganjuur, the teachings spoken by the Buddha himself, and the Danjuur, its commentary. They hold a major place in the religious and cultural history of Mongolia and the world. In Mongolian, Ganjuur means "Collection of Commands." It contains the ancient Indian Tripitaka, or "Three Baskets," and consists of more than 1,260 separate works. The museum keeps several rare editions of the Ganjuur:

- Narthang Ganjuur: 102 volumes
- Derge Ganjuur: 100 volumes
- Urga (Khuree) woodblock Ganjuur: 105 volumes
- Mongolian manuscript Ganjuur: 76 volumes
- Golden Ganjuur: 101 volumes
- Silver Ganjuur: 102 volumes

## What special manuscripts can you see in the museum?

### The Nine Jewels Ganjuur

This sutra is said to exist in only one copy in the world. In 1819 it was written on black paper measuring 56×22 cm, using nine jewels: gold, silver, copper, coral, pearl, turquoise, lapis lazuli, steel and iron. It has 142 volumes in total.

### The Mongolian Danjuur in cinnabar

The Danjuur is a great sacred collection of 3,427 works by Indian and Tibetan scholars on the ten major and minor sciences. It has 107,839 pages, each measuring 23×72.5 cm. Around 200 translators, led by the Second Janjaa Khutagt Rolbiidorj and Shireet Khutagt Luvsandambiinyam, translated it from Tibetan into Mongolian in 1741–1742. In 1742–1749 it was printed from woodblocks in Beijing in 226 volumes, a collective work by many people. In 2011 it was inscribed on UNESCO's international documentary heritage register.

### The memorial stone of the Danjuur

On October 28, 1924, Jamyan Gun, the first director of the Institute of Scripts and Books, signed a nine-article agreement with Prince Nayant to keep the Mongolian Danjuur in Mongolia forever. Four copies were made in Mongolian and Chinese. The 226 volumes were brought to the capital in 1925. In 1929, the text of the agreement was carved on a stone monument to commemorate this.

- **Material:** granite. A lotus flower is carved on the base, and the inscription is carved on the stele.
- **Size:** base 71 cm long and 38 cm wide, total height 248 cm.
- **Seals:** the seal of Setsen Chin Wang Nayant in the upper left, and the seal of the Ministry of Public Enlightenment in the upper right.
- **Decoration:** the patterns on the base and top (ugalz, a curled ornamental motif) are carved up to 13 cm deep.
- **Craftsman's inscription:** on the lower back of the monument it says, "Made by Dendev. 1929."

Because it is a monument dedicated to a book, it is considered extremely rare in the world.

### The Lu. Altan Tobchi

This book, titled "The Golden Summary, an Abridgment of the Royal Chronicle Founded by Ancient Khans," was written by the historian Luvsandanzan in 1651. It is a sutra of 352 pages, written with a bamboo pen on paper measuring 19×63 cm. In 1926, Jamyan Gun obtained it from Tayj Dari of the Yunsheebuu clan in Bayantumen banner (khoshuu) of Khan Khentii Mountain Province. The Lu. Altan Tobchi includes 233 of the 282 sections of The Secret History of the Mongols almost word for word. For this reason, it is regarded as a version of the Secret History that survives in Mongolian script. In 2011 it was inscribed on UNESCO's "Memory of the World" register.

### The Jambaltsanjid

This is one of the museum's oldest books. It is a folded sutra measuring 25×9 cm, printed from woodblocks. At the order of Bayaguud hero Dai Khuntaij, grandson of Altan Khan of the Tumed, his son Choijamts Toin had it printed in four languages: Sanskrit, Tibetan, Mongolian and Chinese. It is a monument of classical Mongolian written language and a unique example of the folded book. It is important for the history of Mongolian script and book studies.

### The sutra of the Yuan state

In 1368, 16 Chinese officials compiled it by order of Zhu Yuanzhang, the emperor of the Ming dynasty, under the direction of Song Lian (1310–1381) and Wang Wei (1322–1373). It is one of the main sources for studying Mongolian history of the 13th–14th centuries. The scholar Demchigdorj (also known as Dandaa) translated the full text into Mongolian in 210 notebooks. It is written in black ink, 15 lines per page, on soft paper measuring 26×26 cm, and bound in red cloth.

### The palm-leaf "Jadamba"

Legend says that the ancient Indian scholar Nagarjuna (1st century CE) wrote the "Great Sacred Sutra of Eight Thousand Verses on the Perfection of Wisdom." It is written on palm leaves in the ancient Lantsa script. Two holes were made in each leaf and the leaves were tied together with string. This makes the book easy to turn and keeps the pages from getting lost or mixed up. It measures 31.5×5.2 cm and has wooden covers.

### The Erdenyin Tobchi: the longest book

Sagan Setsen wrote the "Erdenyin Tobchi" (Precious Summary) in 1662. It is a scroll 71.1 meters long, written in black ink in Mongolian script on soft paper. It is a unique type of Mongolian manuscript. It is easy to carry in a bag or to hang from the top of a wall for storage.

### Mongolian history from Chinggis to Toghon Temur "the Wise"

This is a manuscript carefully written with a bamboo pen and black ink on three-layer paper called "muuvin," made from rice-straw pulp. The author is unknown.

### Praise of Green Tara: the smallest book

The well-known Mongolian scholar, lexicographer and Tibetologist S. Shagj wrote it in Tibetan in 1914 on paper 5.4 cm long and 5 cm wide. The text itself measures 5×4.5 cm, so it cannot be read with the naked eye. You need a double lens or a microscope. It is said that S. Shagj darkened his ger by covering its roof, made a hole the size of a needle's eye in the roof, and wrote it with a single hair on days of bright sun.

### The star map

This map was printed from stone blocks in black ink on soft paper in the 16th century. It shows 1,375 stars in total that are visible to the naked eye from Mongolia: 773 in the northern half of the sky and 602 in the southern half. The Mongolian name of each star is written beside it in Mongolian script.

### The largest Jadamba sutra

This is the largest sutra-format book, written in the 17th century. It measures 81×32 cm and has 500 pages. It is written in black ink on thick paper and printed from Chinese blocks. The edge of each page is decorated in red cinnabar. On both sides, the five senses are shown together with flower patterns and framed by horn-shaped ugalz.

### A text naming various medicines

It was written in 1847. The first part lists the names of medicines in a table in Tibetan and Mongolian. The next part includes drawings of medicinal plants with explanations. It also describes in detail, with pictures and explanations, the vessels of the human body that can be pricked or cauterized. It has red cloth covers and measures 40.5×55.5 cm.

### The genealogy beginning with Burte Chino

This genealogy of the Mongol khans covers 42 generations, from [Chinggis Khaan's](</about/history/people/chingis-khaan>) remote ancestors Burte Chino and Goa Maral to the children of Tusheet Khan Gombodorj and Setsen Khan Sholoi. It is one of the oldest genealogies with information on many khans. It measures 100×352 cm and is written with a brush in black and red ink on layered soft paper. The names are separated by generation with dotted red lines and written in columns from top to bottom. For some princes, the banner (khoshuu) they ruled is mentioned instead of the names of their children.

### Sanduin Jud: the book with silver pages

The "Root of the Secret Summary," or Sanduin Jud, was made in the late 19th century by Mongolian craftsmen led by Dagva. Pure silver was hammered by the Mongolian blacksmith method into pages of 59×15 cm. The letters were embossed and gilded with gold. The opening and closing sentences are inlaid with solid pure gold. Each page has an embossed gold image of a deity, set with diamonds, coral and pearls. In total, 1,460 lan, or 52 kg 560 g, of pure gold and silver were used. It has 12 chapters of the highest Tantric teachings, 111 pages and 220 sides.

### Itgel: a sutra wrapped with needle and thread

This sutra measures 20.5×8.5 cm. It was made in the late 19th century by an embroiderer named Dolzhin of the Ar Doloon Gureechin clan in Tusheet Khan Province. The letters were made by wrapping needle and gold thread without a single knot.

### The Jadamba copper sutra

This is a copper block of 1,494 pages carved in the early 20th century by craftsmen led by the famed engraver Bor Chultem and Tungiin Shar. It is kept together with 747 pages of the book printed from this block. It is the only copy and has fine, elaborate decoration. It is a masterpiece of Mongolian metalwork. Many images related to the content of the book are carved on its pages.

The National Museum of Mongolia is in the center of [Ulaanbaatar](/province/ulaanbaatar), west of the Government Palace. It presents the history of Mongolia in order, from the Stone Age to the present day. The museum has more than 50,000 items and 10 halls.

## What is the history of the National Museum of Mongolia?

The museum was founded in 1924 to introduce the history, culture and natural wealth of Mongolia to the public. Its main stages of development were:

- **1941.** It became the central state museum.
- **1956.** It moved into the building now used by the Natural History Museum, and began to show exhibits on history, nature, paleontology, culture and art.
- **1964.** A new ethnography hall opened.
- **1991.** Based on the collection of the Revolution Museum, founded in 1971, it became the National Museum of Mongolian History. It separated from the earlier museum and moved into that building.
- **2008.** It was renamed the "National Museum of Mongolia."

## What is there to see in the Ancient Mongolian History hall?

The hall has a map showing where Stone Age and Bronze Age finds were discovered. Tools, weapons and rock paintings from the Early, Middle and Late Stone Age found in Mongolia are displayed in chronological order. About 5000–3000 years ago, in the Bronze Age, Mongolians kept livestock and also made wide use of bronze items in daily life. The main monuments that show this are rock paintings, khirigsuur (burial mound), rectangular graves and deer stones. Here you can see bronze helmets, arrowheads and hunting knives, which are commonly found in rectangular graves and khirigsuurs.

## What special finds are in the Xiongnu hall?

The hall on the [Xiongnu (Hunnu)](</about/history/khunnu>), the first state in Mongolia, holds artifacts found in the huge tombs of Xiongnu rulers (shanyu) on Noyon Mountain in [Batsumber soum](</province/tuv#batsumber>), Tuv Province, in the 1920s. There are grain storage jars, chopsticks, knives and other household items. The most important find is the remains of a felt rug from tomb number six. In the upper part of the rug there is a cloud pattern, with a fine line pattern outside it. In the center, a yak, a deer, a maned tiger and winged, tailed creatures are shown fighting each other, in very skillful embroidery. It is said that no rug with such imagery has been found anywhere else. Read more about the [Noyon Uul tombs](</destination/heritage/place/noen-uulyn-bulsh>) on a separate page.

## What is kept in the Turkic hall?

This hall displays finds from the mausoleums and complexes of Turkic khans and nobles in the Orkhon and Tuul river basins.

- **Bilge Khan's gold crown.** From the tomb of the Turkic Bilge Khan came gold and silver decorations, gold and silver vessels, cups, a woman's gold chest pendant, earrings, belt ornaments, bracelets, gold and silver plates, and a deer figure cast in silver and covered with gold. Among them, the gold crown is considered the most valuable exhibit.
- **Copy of the Kul Tegin monument.** A copy of the monument erected in 732, 3.3 meters high and 1.3 meters wide. About 10,000 letters in 68 lines are carved on it, telling the life of the general Kul Tegin and the wars of that time. Kul Tegin (685–731) gave great support to his elder brother Bilge when he took the throne. For the original, read the [Kul Tegin monument](</destination/heritage/place/kuliteginy-khushuu>) page.
- **Monument remains.** Headless marble statues said to be of Bilge Khan and his queen, the head of the general Kul Tegin, and a statue fragment with part of the face of his wife.
- **Excavation finds.** Bridle bits, arrowheads, belt ornaments, shagai (anklebone games), jars, whetstones, stirrups and building materials found in the 2000–2003 excavations.

At the start of the hall is the Stele of Chinggis. In 1919, the Russian scholar Spassky discovered it on the bank of the Argun River. Khugnu Khan Mountain (also spelled Khugnukhaan or Khugnukhan) lies in [Bulgan Province (aimag)](/province/bulgan), in the area of [Gurvanbulag](/province/bulgan#gurvanbulag) and [Rashaant](</province/bulgan/rashaant>) soums. It rises 1,962 m above sea level. Local people have long worshipped it as a fierce sacred mountain. Erdenekhamba Monastery stands on its slope, and the ruins of Ovgon Monastery sit on a ledge above. Forest, taiga and steppe meet here, and the mountain is rich in history and legend. Many travelers find it fascinating.

## What are the key facts about Khugnu Khan Mountain?

- **Location:** Bulgan Province, Gurvanbulag and Rashaant soums.
- **Height:** 1,962 m above sea level.
- **Protection:** In 1997, 470 km² was declared a nature reserve. In 2003 it was expanded to 844 km².
- **Monastery:** Erdenekhamba Monastery (the Ovgon and Zaluu monasteries, also called Khugnu Tarni Monastery), restored in 1994.
- **Mineral springs:** springs said to help the stomach, liver, gallbladder, kidneys and eyes.
- **Minerals:** rock crystal, smoky quartz, and black and white mica.

## What is the nature like on Khugnu Khan Mountain?

The mountain is unusual because two or three vegetation zones meet here: forest, taiga and steppe. It lies more than 200 km from the Khangai and Khentii pine forests. Even so, larch, birch and pine grow on its back slopes. Shrubs are also common, including rhododendron, juniper, wild apricot, caragana, spirea and similar plants. A rare plant called the tarni rhododendron grows here. The mountain holds minerals such as rock crystal, smoky quartz, and black and white mica. To protect this distinctive landscape, 470 km² of the mountain was made a nature reserve in 1997. It was expanded to 844 km² in 2003. On the southern slope there is a hidden valley that faces southwest. You can enter it only over a pass from the southwest. Rock cliffs surround this scenic valley, which holds springs and monastery ruins. Springs flow out of the rocks that are said to help the stomach, liver, gallbladder, kidneys and eyes. Many valleys on Khugnu Khan Mountain carry its name:

- Rashaan Valley (Spring Valley)
- Khiid Valley (Monastery Valley)
- Khudag Valley (Well Valley)
- Byatskhan Bulag Valley (Little Spring Valley)
- Tsetserleg Valley (Garden Valley)
- Khankhar Valley
- Samart Valley
- Bayalag Valley (Rich Valley)

For another natural area with a similar name, read the [Khugnu Tarna](</destination/protected/khugnu-tarna>) page.

![image](asset:tic-img-306e41c61eaabc38)

## How do people worship Khugnu Khan Mountain?

People have long honored Khugnu Khan Mountain as a very fierce sacred mountain. Cutting trees or moving stones on the mountain was traditionally forbidden. On the slope there is a place called Khadagt Khoshuu. Anyone passing by is expected to stop, dismount, greet the mountain and offer food and drink. The mountain has three worship sites: the Takhilgyn ovoo (sacred stone cairn), the Santyn ovoo and the Baga Khany ovoo. Local people say there are golden fish in a small lake on the summit ledge.

## What historical sites are there on Khugnu Khan Mountain?

Besides the monastery ruins, many ancient sites survive on the mountain.

- **Moilt Valley rock painting.** A Bronze Age rock painting.
- **Yamaat White Hollow painting.** Images of ibex, wolves and deer.
- **Elsen Tasarkhai statue.** A granite statue.
- **Maani rock.** Mani mantras carved into the rock.
- **Tashuu winter camp burials.** Many ancient graves.
- **Ruins of the Ovgon and Zaluu monasteries.**

## Why is it called Khugnu Khan Mountain?

The name comes from the wars between the Khalkha and the Oirat. Legend says that Undur Gegeen [Zanabazar](</about/history/people/undur-gegeen-zanabazar>) of the Khalkha built a wonderful temple on the mountain ledge with his disciples. The temple had a golden ganjir (roof finial). In summer, lamas held prayer services there, and hermit lamas meditated. When war broke out between the Khalkha and the Oirat, the soldiers of [Galdan Boshigt](</about/history/people/galdan-boshigt>) came looking for Ovgon Temple, where Zanabazar's disciples lived. The lamas had wrapped the golden ganjir in rags to hide it. The soldiers could not find the temple and turned back. Then an old woman said, "The soldiers are gone. Now let me take this ugly bundle of rags," and she unwrapped the ganjir. The soldiers had come down from the sacred mountain and were walking along the ridge of a neighboring mountain. In the morning sun they saw the golden ganjir gleaming. They came back and found the temple. There are two versions of the legend:

- In one version, the soldiers captured about 100 lamas, killed them by tying their necks together with a hobble, and burned the temple.
- In the other, they tied the lamas together by their feet and heads with rope (Mongolians hobble livestock this way) and pushed them off a high cliff.

Because of this tragedy, the mountain came to be called "Khugnu Khan", the sacred mountain of the hobbled lamas. The word "khugnukh" means to tie together or to hobble. It is said that "Khaan" refers to the Oirat Galdan Khan.

## How did Erdenekhamba Monastery begin?

According to legend, the Tibetan lama Lkhalambaldorj meditated on the deity Yamantaka for many years in remote mountains and gained spiritual powers. Hearing that the Tibetan king Langdarma, who opposed Buddhism, was destroying religion and culture, he is said to have killed him. He then fled Tibet and stayed for a while on Khugnu Khan Mountain in Mongolia. Legend says he founded the monastery in 1640 and spread the Red Hat tradition there. In 1693, Undur Gegeen Zanabazar restored and expanded the monastery. He had the Ovgon and Zaluu monasteries built in honor of his teacher Erdene Tsorj. During the Khalkha–Oirat wars the monasteries were destroyed, and more than 100 lamas were killed. About 200 lamas fled north and settled near Lake Baikal. This is believed to have helped spread Buddhism among the Buryat. The few surviving lamas continued their prayers at Khar Bukh ruins. Later the monastery was moved and rebuilt on a terrace on the inner slope of Jargalant Khairkhan Mountain. It lay in the banner (khoshuu) of Baruun Soo Gun (Bishrelt Gun) of Tusheet Khan Province. History records it as the upper and lower Khugnu Tarni monasteries.

![image](asset:tic-img-7dcf6470b5fa91b8)

## How big was Erdenekhamba Monastery?

By the early 20th century the monastery had more than 1,000 lamas. It was divided into four colleges: Puntsaglin, Tegchilen, Sangai and Toislin. It was a large monastery with the finely built Tsogchin Temple, many temples for the deity Jas, a temple for printing sutras, and a lama quarter.

- **Reincarnations:** Erdene Khamba and Khalzan Shireet.
- **Main protector deity:** the Red Protector, or [Jamsran Burkhan](</about/culture/religion/jamsran-burkhan>).
- **Tsam (masked ritual dance):** held on the 29th day of the middle summer month.
- **Khailan prayer assembly:** 50–60 nuns held prayers for 45 days in the last summer month.
- **Ganjuur reading:** in the first month of autumn.
- **Teaching sessions:** sessions on dom and gavj were held, and the monastery had two large Jas temples, the southern and northern sunduis.

In the time of the Eighth Bogd Gegeen, Erdene Khutagt Khamba led the monastery. It is said that after the 13th Dalai Lama came to Mongolia, spent the winter and left, he sent a secret messenger to Erdene Khutagt. The message he carried said: "His Holiness has come and gone. Mongolia's good fortune may follow His Holiness, so it is for you, Erdene Khutagt, to keep Mongolia's good fortune here." This shows how high the monastery's standing was at that time.

## What remains of Erdenekhamba Monastery today?

In 1937, more than 30 lamas with the gavj title were arrested. After that, the temple's fine architecture, valuable sutras and the tsam masks of the main protector, the Red Protector, were all lost. The ground where the tsam dance was held has survived to this day. It was paved with colored stones, laid out mainly from the Tsogchin Temple's entrance. The circular dance ground is edged with a stone border about a meter wide. Inside are 29 sections inlaid with red, blue and light-colored stones. The foundations of stone, clay and brick walls, as well as traces of stupas and prayer wheels, also remain. In 1992, the nun Davaa revived the prayer services of the monastery and kept them going without a break. She rebuilt the Temple of the Five Kings and the protector deity temple of Erdenekhamba Monastery, and had two meditation pavilions and two stupas built. The monastery was restored in 1994 and is now called Erdene Khamba Monastery.

## How do I get to Ovgon Monastery?

The ruins of Ovgon Temple are on the ledge of Khugnu Khan Mountain. From Erdenekhamba Monastery you climb on foot for more than 1 km up the mountain. The route is rough and the path is narrow, so you can reach it only on horseback or on foot. The temple's entrance faces southwest, and the mountain seems to spread its arms to catch the sun. Around the climb there are rocky hills and unusual layered cliffs that look like stacked sutra pages. There is an ovoo on the summit behind the temple. Along the way to the ovoo you will see birch, pine, elm, bird cherry, juniper and many kinds of shrubs. Ovgon Monastery is said to have been built to imitate the Potala Palace in Tibet. The ruins of more than 10 temples, built by carefully stacking stones up the mountain slope, show that this place was a major early center of Buddhism in Mongolia.

## What should I know before visiting Khugnu Khan Mountain?

Khugnu Khan Mountain is a sacred mountain worshipped by local people, and it is also a nature reserve. Travelers should keep the following in mind.

- **Respect the mountain's taboos.** Cutting trees or moving stones has long been forbidden. As this is a nature reserve, it is also right not to disturb plants or rocks.
- **Stop at Khadagt Khoshuu.** When passing this place, local tradition is to stop, dismount, greet the mountain and offer food and drink.
- **Honor the ovoo.** Treat worship sites such as the Takhilgyn ovoo, Santyn ovoo and Baga Khany ovoo with respect.
- **Go on foot to Ovgon Monastery.** The narrow, rough path to Ovgon Temple on the ledge climbs more than 1 km. Wear suitable shoes and bring water.
- **Protect the tsam ground.** The tsam ground inlaid with colored stones at Erdenekhamba Monastery and the wall foundations are fragile historical monuments. Do not climb on the ruins or take stones.
- **Follow monastery rules.** The restored Erdene Khamba Monastery still holds prayer services, so follow its customs when you enter the temple.

The Shivertiin Am deer stones are a Bronze Age site 45 km west of [Battsengel soum](</province/arkhangai#battsengel>) in [Arkhangai Province (aimag)](/province/arkhangai) and 45 km north of Tsetserleg. Slab graves, khirigsuur (burial mound), deer stones, and Turkic-period graves stand together here. The most distinctive feature is that deer stones were reused in the walls and stone coffins of slab graves. This is important evidence for dating these monuments.

## What is there to see at Shivertiin Am?

- **Location:** Arkhangai Province, 45 km west of Battsengel soum and 45 km north of Tsetserleg. It is 6 km north of the center of the former Bayanuul soum (now a bag (the smallest administrative unit)).
- **Monuments:** many rectangular and round graves, deer stones, two large khirigsuur, and a Turkic-period grave with a stone box.
- **Deer stones:** 22, plus one carved stone figure.
- **Tallest stone:** 3.55 m (stone no. 7).
- **First reported by:** Galsandagva, a resident of Battsengel soum.

## What monuments are at Shivertiin Am?

Galsandagva, a resident of Battsengel soum, first told researchers about the many graves and stones here. On the west side of the stream that runs down below Shivertiin Am, there are many rectangular and round graves and deer stones. Two large khirigsuur lie 500 m to the northwest. A Turkic-period grave with a stone box lies 300 m to the east. Researchers made a general site plan and excavated two slab graves built with deer stones. They also excavated one deer stone buried under the ground and one carved stone.

## Which deer stones were used in the first grave?

Four deer stones were laid flat in the wall of the first slab grave.

### Deer stone 1

This stone forms the north wall of the grave. It is made of pale, fine-grained granite. A sun appears at the top of the front face. Below it, a row of small hollows runs around the stone. Ten stylized deer wind around the body, a classic deer stone design. Below the deer, a wide belt is carved, with weapons shown hanging from it: an axe and a knife on the front, a knife on the left side, and a dagger on the back. Below the belt, a shield appears on the right side.

- **Size:** 2.7 m high, 0.32 m wide, 0.5–0.3 m thick.

### Deer stones 2 and 3

The second stone was used at the front of the grave and the third in the west wall. Both are heavily worn by time and weather. The carvings on them have faded beyond recognition.

- **Stone 2:** 1.7 m high, 0.4 m wide, 0.3 m thick.
- **Stone 3:** 2 m high, 0.4 m wide, 0.3 m thick.

## Which stones were used in the second grave?

### Deer stone 4

This stone forms the west part of the front wall of the second grave. It is a natural stone used without shaping. On the wide back face, two deer are carved in the classic style with their muzzles pointing to the top. On the other side, two more deer point their muzzles up. It is white, pale, fine-grained granite.

- **Size:** 1.8 m high, 0.7 m wide, 0.2 m thick.

### Deer stone 5

This stone was used in the east part of the front wall of the second grave. On the front, three large and three small deer are carved with their muzzles pointing up, and they are clearly visible. The carvings on the other three sides are worn and faint. It is blue-gray granite.

- **Size:** 3 m high, 0.75 m wide, 0.25 m thick.

### Deer stones 13–22

The walls, lid, and floor of the stone coffin beneath the second slab grave were made from deer stones numbered 13 to 22. Two of them are especially notable:

- **Stone 18 (the picture panel).** It covered the west part of another stone coffin. Three sides have no carvings. On the front, two narrow parallel lines, or a road, run down the center. Above them, three people leading horses, 18 horses, and one dog are carved in hollowed marks. This resembles the rock paintings common in Mongolia and Central Asia, so it is especially valuable for research. It is 1.25 m high, 0.55 m wide, and 0.15 m thick.
- **Stone 22.** It forms the east part of the north wall of the grave. Two deer are carved on the front and five on the back. Four of them are paired, with their antlers aligned side by side, which makes the composition especially interesting.

## What makes the buried stone 6 special?

The sixth stone was buried 10 m west of the second grave, with only its corner showing. Researchers dug it out but left it without raising it. It is made of reddish-yellow marble. It seems to have been buried early, and its carvings are very well preserved.

- At the top of the front face are a sun and a moon (a large and a small ring, with two rays hanging down from the larger one). There is also a sun at the top of the right side.
- Below them, a row of hollows runs around the stone.
- Ten large deer wind around the body. The hooves and legs of the deer are shown with three prongs, like a bird's foot, which is unusual.
- In the middle of the back, between the deer, a round mirror is carved. On the lower right side, a shield is carved.
- **Size:** 2.7 m high, 0.8–0.9 m wide, 0.4 m thick.

The lower part of the stone is broken, but the broken piece was not found nearby. For this reason, researchers think the stone may have been brought from another place.

## What do the stones at the third, fourth, and fifth graves look like?

### Deer stone 7

This stone lies near the front wall of the third grave. It is made of blue-gray, fine-grained granite, unshaped. It is the tallest stone at Shivertiin Am.

- At the top of the front face is a sun. Three large deer on the body point their muzzles to the top. Below them is a dagger with a sheath.
- At the top of the right side is a row of small hollows. On the body are six small deer with muzzles pointing up. Below them are a bow with a trigger and a quiver. In the lower corner is one small deer with its muzzle pointing down.
- The carvings on the back and left side have worn away.
- **Size:** 3.55 m high, 0.7 m wide, 0.27 m thick.

### Deer stone 8

This stone lies on the east side of the fourth grave. It has the richest animal images.

- On the front are four large deer with muzzles up. Between them are three small deer and two ibex pointing up, and three more small deer pointing down.
- At the right edge of this face, two large snow leopards with open mouths are placed head down. Two small snow leopards are placed crosswise.
- On the back are two large deer and one ibex with muzzles to the top. On the narrow right face, a shield with a pointed top is carved.
- It is dark gray, fine-grained marble.
- **Size:** 1.9 m high, 0.95 m wide, 0.32 m thick.

### Deer stones 9 and 10

These two stones form the northeast and northwest walls of the fourth grave. Both are heavily worn.

- **Stone 9:** only the body of one deer remains on the front. Blue-gray marble, 2.8 m high, 0.48 m wide, 0.3 m thick.
- **Stone 10:** only the antlers of one deer remain on the front. Blue-gray marble, 1.8 m high, 0.38 m wide, 0.3 m thick.

### Deer stone 11

This stone stands at the northeast corner of the fifth grave. It is carved from yellowish granite without shaping.

- At the top of the front face are a sun with a single ray and two horses. In the center, one deer points up and two point down. Between the two upper deer is one horse.
- The left side has no carvings. On the back, four deer point down.
- On the right side, in the middle, is a shield with a pointed top. In the lower part, two horses have their muzzles pointing to the top.
- **Size:** 1.2 m high, 0.7 m wide, 0.6 m thick.

### Deer stone 12

This stone stands side by side with stone 11, to its northeast.

- On the front are a sun with two rays and four deer with muzzles up. On the right side, four deer point to the top. On the left side, three deer do the same.
- At the top of the back is a sun. In the middle is a round mirror. In the lower part is a knife with a sheath and a round head. Four deer on the body point to the top.
- **Size:** 1.7 m high, 0.4 m wide, 0.35 m thick.

## What is carved on the stone figure?

In the northwest part of the site, 14 m east of one slab grave, stands a carved stone figure. It was set up facing west, toward the grave. It shows the lapel, belt, and apron of a garment. It is 1.6 m high, 0.6 m wide, and 0.2 m thick.

## Why is Shivertiin Am important?

Here you can see clearly that deer stones were used in the walls and coffins of slab graves. You can also see a picture panel showing a road and people leading horses, used as a coffin lid. This is important evidence for deciding which came earlier or later among deer stones, slab graves, rock paintings, and petroglyphs. It is also valuable for comparison with other [deer stones](</destination/heritage/place/altan-sandal-uulyn-bugan-khushuu>) in Mongolia.

Deer stone 7 is 3.55 m high.

The Bogd Khan Palace Museum is in [Ulaanbaatar](/province/ulaanbaatar), in [Khan-Uul District](/province/ulaanbaatar/khan-uul), on the west side of the road to Zaisan. Every gate and temple in this complex has its own symbolism and history. It is the winter palace and monastery of the last [Bogd Khan](</about/history/people/bogd-khaan>) of Mongolia, the 8th Bogd Javzandamba Khutagt. For 285 years, from 1639 to 1924, eight successive "Bogd" (living Buddhas) ruled in Mongolia. This palace is linked to the last of them.

## What are the key facts about the Bogd Khan Palace Museum?

- **Location:** Khan-Uul District, west side of the road to Zaisan.
- **Built:** 1893–1903.
- **Complex:** seven temples (palace buildings) and the winter palace.
- **Historic event:** On December 29, 1911, the 8th Bogd was enthroned as khan.
- **Highlights:** Zanabazar's 21 Taras, a ger covered with 150 snow leopard skins, and a ceremonial vest with 22,000 pearls.
- **Website:** bogdkhaanpalace.mn.

## What is the history of the Bogd Khan Palace?

The palace complex consists of seven temples built between 1893 and 1903. On [December 29, 1911](</stories/news/12-dugaar-saryn-29>), the 8th [Bogd Gegeen](/about/culture/religion/bogd-gegeen) Javzandamba Khutagt was proclaimed "Supreme Sovereign Khan, who unites state and religion in Mongolia," and a ceremony was held.

![image](asset:tic-img-33326a93eaad2c7d)

## What is there to see outside the palace?

- **Yanpai wall.** In Tibetan, the name means "closed gate." It symbolically protects the temple from unseen harm. The wall shows a dragon, Mount Sumeru, and the ocean around it. Evil spirits are said to fall into the ocean and drown.
- **Asart Gate.** The name means "open gate." Only the Bogd Khan, the queen, and close relatives passed through it. The plaque above is written in Uighur-Mongolian, Tibetan, and Chinese script: "Year 33, month 3. The 8th Bogd Khan." Guards, state nobles, musicians, envoys, and visiting guests entered through the two small side gates.
- **Chiigan.** Two wooden poles stand in front of the gate. Religious banners and the Mongolian state banner were raised on them.
- **Amgalan-Enkhiin Gate.** It was built to commemorate the enthronement of the 8th Bogd as ruler of both state and religion. It has eight pillars and a seven-tiered roof. It was assembled with 108 types of joints, without a single nail.
- **Ravsa, or cool pavilion.** It has a roof with upturned tips. A bell hangs on the east side and a drum on the west. The Bogd Khan reportedly rang the bell for the gods of the sky and beat the drum for the masters of the land and water.

## What is kept in the palace temples?

### Makhranz Temple

Built in 1903. Inside are the four great kings of Makhranz, who protect the four continents from danger. They are considered among the largest works made by the papier-mâché technique.

### Silk Painting Temple

This was the Bogd Khan's reading hall. The Buddhist teachings and historical scriptures kept here were moved to the State Central Library in 1924. Today it holds silk paintings and appliqué works made by famous artists and craftsmen of the time. The deities depicted include:

- Gongor, who brings great wealth
- Tsambagarav, who protects work and endeavors
- Jamsran, protector of the faith
- [Bogd Zonkhov](/about/culture/religion/bogd-zonkhov), founder of the Yellow Hat school
- Manzushir, the deity of wisdom and knowledge
- Dorjpagma, who symbolizes meeting one's destined partner
- Damdindorlig, patron of carpenters and blacksmiths

### Fine Art Temple

Paintings made with mineral pigments in the early 19th century are kept here. They include "The Thought of the Saint," the thought of the Jigjid Buddha; "The Thousand Buddhas of the Good Age"; "The Life of the Buddha," which shows the Buddha's biography; "The Buddha and the Eight Bodhi Stupas"; and [Ayush Buddha](</about/culture/religion/ayuush-burkhan>), who grants long life and happiness.

### Naidan Temple

On the birthday of the 8th Bogd Khan, 16 lamas painted images of deities here for three days. The temple holds items the Bogd Khan used:

- The "Tiger's Tail," used to clear a path through crowds who gathered to receive his blessing when he left the palace.
- Various religious musical instruments.
- A gungervaa (temple spire ornament) decorated with gems and covered in gold.
- Khadag (ceremonial silk scarves) the Bogd Khan used to give blessings, and a sandalwood incense burner.
- The "Three Deities of Longevity": White Tara, Ayush, and Jugdernamjil. Also gilded copper-cast statues of Zonkhov and his two disciples.

### Buddha Temple

This temple has Otoch (the eight Medicine Buddhas), Maitreya, and a gilded statue of Vanchinbogd, head of Tibetan Buddhism, all made by Mongolia's finest craftsmen. It also has a thangka (Buddhist scroll painting) of Tsogdogmarav.

### Lavrin Temple

The 8th Bogd Khan worshipped in this temple. It holds sculptures made by the first Bogd, [Undur Gegeen Zanabazar](</about/history/people/undur-gegeen-zanabazar>).

- **21 Taras.** These figures express the inner feelings of a woman. At first glance they look the same, but each has a different facial expression and gaze.
- **Green Tara.** She expresses the dignified look of a mother and calm peace.
- **Statue of Javzundarnat.** Zanabazar is believed to be the reincarnation, born in Mongolia, of this Indian lama.

## What is there to see in the Bogd Khan's winter palace?

The winter palace is a two-story building built in 1903 from the design of a Russian architect.

### Upper floor

This floor has a waiting hall for visiting guests, the Bogd Khan's reception hall, and rest rooms for the khan and queen. During audiences, the Bogd Khan sat on a seat with 25 layers of cushions. High-ranking lamas of the state sat on red cushions, and ministers sat on blue ones. The portraits of the Bogd Khan and queen on the wall were painted by B. Sharav, the founder of modern Mongolian painting. Queen Dondogdulam was born in 1874, was given the title "Mother of the Nation" in 1911, and died in 1923 at age 49. Her belongings include a silk deel (traditional robe) with a nine-dragon pattern and a deel with a "ten thousand years" pattern. They also include a headdress called "rin-a," a pipe, a rosary, a vest decorated with peacock neck feathers, a black sandalwood bed, a chair, and a dressing table. The Bogd Khan's belongings include:

- Ritual garments and implements, amulets that guard against evil, ivory decorations, a coral rosary, a silver bell, and a vajra.
- A deel with a nine-dragon pattern, a hat trimmed with mink, a vest woven with sable thread, and a silk vest with a Soyombo pattern.
- A crown set with pearls on its four sides and a golden vajra on top.
- A jancha (ceremonial garment) made from 80 sable skins, passed down from Zanabazar, and a deel made from 164 sable skins, worn at his enthronement as khan.
- A ceremonial vest with a pearl pattern. A single motif on it has 22,000 pearls strung together.
- A snuff bottle, a flint knife, writing tools, the great throne of the khan and queen, and the state seal, kept in its box.

The bedroom of the khan and queen was decorated with embroidery and paintings by Chinese craftsmen. The sandalwood bed was adorned with silk, brocade, and precious stones. The room also holds two chairs that play music when you sit on them, a gift from the Russian tsar. It also has a pure silver tea set, jade and agate cups, a teapot, ceremonial airag (fermented mare's milk) cups, and porcelain vases.

### Lower floor

The lower floor holds the penalty cups. Anyone who arrived late to a feast or Naadam hosted by the Bogd Khan's palace was fined by being made to drink airag. If the person could not explain the delay well, they drank from the large 10-liter cup. If the explanation was good, they drank from the small 5-liter cup. Other exhibits:

- A music box bought from Russian merchants in 1910, which plays 8–10 classical tunes.
- A picture of the elephant the Bogd Khan kept, along with clothing, hats, and a back saddle.
- Copies of B. Sharav's paintings "One Day in Mongolia," "The Festival of Foal Separation," "The Winter Palace," and "The Lavrin Temple."
- The Bogd Khan's childhood toys, a carriage, and a palanquin he rode in when traveling outside.

## What is the story of the Bogd Khan's elephant and the snow leopard ger?

Mongolian nobles who visited Russia in 1913 bought the Bogd Khan's elephant for 22,000 rubles and presented it to him. It was carried by train to the Mongolian border. From there, it is said, it was walked for eight months to the Bogd Khan's summer palace. Two lamas cared for and fed it. It is said that the elephant died two months after the Bogd Khan passed away in 1924. Besides the elephant, the Bogd Khan kept living animals such as parrots and monkeys at his palace. In 1901, he set up an animal exhibition hall with stuffed rare animals collected from all over the world. Another valuable exhibit in the museum is a ger whose roof and walls are covered with snow leopard skins. In 1893, Prince Sanligdorj of [Khentii Province (aimag)](/province/khentii) presented this ger, covered with 150 snow leopard skins, for the Bogd Khan's 25th birthday.

The museum is in Khan-Uul District, on the west side of the road to Zaisan. Opening hours differ between winter and summer. Opening hours, ticket prices, photo and video fees, and phone numbers may change. Please check the museum's official website (bogdkhaanpalace.mn) before you go.

Aglag Buteeliin Temple is a Buddhist temple museum on the slope of Garid Mountain, in a place called Dalyn Am, in Nart bag (the smallest administrative unit), Bornuur soum, Tuv Province (aimag). To get there, drive 92 km northwest of Ulaanbaatar on a paved road, then 7 km on a dirt road. The temple was founded by G. Purevbat, known as the "Burkhanch lama" (a lama devoted to the Buddha). It is said that he declared the place would become "a monastery that makes good fortune flourish."

![image](asset:tic-img-309c0096d4aa130d)

## What are the key facts about Aglag Buteeliin Temple?

- **Location:** Dalyn Am, Nart bag, Bornuur soum, Tuv Province.
- **Road:** 92 km of paved road and 7 km of dirt road from Ulaanbaatar.
- **Founder:** the Burkhanch lama G. Purevbat.
- **Highlights:** sculptures shaped from rock formations, a museum of curiosities, and a hike along a rope-guided trail.
- **Good to know:** you can drive to the foot of the temple, then continue on foot along a steep uphill path.

## How did Dalyn Am and Garid Mountain get their names?

Long ago, a spring called "Dalan Khar" flowed here. Many flowers called "Daliin Yagaan" grew around it, so the place was named Dalyn Am. The mountain above the valley is called Garid. The temple museum for prayer and worship was built using the natural formations of this grand mountain, where rocks and trees mix together.

## What is on top of the Asart Gate?

The first thing you meet at the temple is the [Asart Gate](</about/culture/religion/asart-khaalga>). On top of the gate stand the "Stupas of the Three Root Deities." Each of the three colored stupas represents one deity:

- **The blue stupa** — [Ochirvaani](/about/culture/religion/ochirvaani-burkhan), the protector deity of the Mongols.
- **The white stupa** — Chagshi Janraisig, who rescues people from danger.
- **The orange stupa** — [Manzushir](</about/culture/religion/manzushir-burkhan>), who honors wisdom.

In some cases, red, yellow and white stupas are used to represent the "Three Deities of Long Life": Ayush, Jugdernamjil and White Tara.

## What is there to see at Aglag Buteeliin Temple?

Visitors usually stop at the following places to complete their pilgrimage:

- Stupa of the Water Spirits' Spring, the Sacred Tree, the White Lotus Prayer Wheel
- Aglag Temple, the Museum of Curiosities
- The Earth-Pressing Turtle, the Golden Egg, the Eight-Legged Lion, the Hairy Fish
- The Blessed Tree, the Gate Rock, the Turning Gorge, the Shrine of Skillful Means, the Mother's Vessel
- The Tree of Life Gate, the Elephant Rock, the Yembuu Rock, the Lama Rock, the Heavenly Fist Rock
- The Footprint Rock, the Seat Rock, the Prayer-Wheel Tree, the Sliding Tunnel, the Farewell Seat Rock
- The portrait of Ochirvaani

### Which animals are carved from rock?

An unusual rock formation on the slope of Garid Mountain was shaped, with a lot of imagination, into a creature with a lion's body and a garuda's head. It became "the Eight-Legged Lion, son of the lion and the garuda." In Buddhist legend, the lion and the garuda are guardian creatures of temples and monasteries, so this one was made to protect the temple. The "Earth-Pressing Turtle" is a symbol of long life and peace that Mongolians have honored since ancient times. On the east side of the monastery, a stone carving called the "Hairy Fish" has a fish head and a whale's body. According to the lama's explanation, combining animals that do not belong together in this way is a symbol of good fortune. Many kinds of rose bushes have been planted in the square in front of the temple, making the surroundings even more beautiful.

## What is inside Aglag Temple?

Inside the temple stands a large stone that looks as if it grew out of the ground. It is called the "Self-Arisen Table." When the foundation was first being prepared, workers tried to split this stone, but a fire suddenly broke out. That night the stone appeared clearly in the lama's dream. He understood that it must not be moved, so he left it in the temple and carved on it the [six-syllable mantra of Om Mani Padme Hum](</about/culture/religion/maaniin-zurgaan-useg>), the heart mantra of Janraisig. At the rear of the temple, in the place of honor, he created the "Land of Shambhala, or the Land of Wisdom." It holds statues of compassionate deities, including the Buddha Teacher, the Five Root Buddhas and White Tara, along with many colorful offering cakes (baling) made for them.

- **On the west wall:** [Green Tara](/about/culture/religion/nogoon-dari-ekh), the merciful protector of women, especially mothers and children.
- **On the opposite side:** portraits of Baldanlham, the protector oath-bound to shield living beings from illness and suffering in this life and to guard the faith until they find a good path in the next, and of the King of Law Erlig, also known as [Damdinchoijoo](/about/culture/religion/damdinchoijoo-burkhan), who judges a person's deeds in this life.
- **Sacred texts:** the Ganjuur, a summary of the great vehicle sutras, and the Danjuur, the detailed teachings, are kept here with respect.

The deities here are made in every way that Buddhist figures are made: as thangka (paintings with mineral pigments), appliqué, gilded copper castings and paper sculptures. You can also see dragons, arvaalj (mythical creatures), water spirits and guardian spirits (savdag) of strange, primeval shapes, made by the lama G. Purevbat and his students. The lama decorated the temple with creatures from legend that he saw in his meditation dreams. He made them from stones of all shapes, tree roots and unusual natural objects found all across Mongolia.

## What is on the second floor?

You reach the second floor by the stairs on the east side of the temple. The main hall holds portraits of the 8th [Bogd Khan](</about/history/people/bogd-khaan>) Jebtsundamba, the last king of Mongolia, and Queen Dondogdulam. It also has portraits of the two great masters of G. Purevbat's craft and wisdom. Here you will find the figure of the "master spirit" of Garid Mountain: a being with bird wings and a human body and an odd body part. It is said that when G. Purevbat meditated here for a long time, he saw the masters of the surrounding mountains and their messenger creatures in his dreams.

### What is in the Museum of Curiosities?

In the farthest room on the second floor is a display of "real" strange animals collected by the lama. For example:

- An anaconda skin and the skin of a giant crocodile
- The skull of a one-horned horse and a four-horned ram
- A roe deer with six horns, an ibex with one horn, and a deer with flower-like antlers
- A pig with six tusks, a hare with horns and a fish with five heads

Each exhibit is shown with the name of the place where it was found, which greatly interests visitors.

## How does the hike work?

The temple's "adventure hike" begins at the "Blessed Tree" behind the temple. A new branch has split off from one dried-out branch of this tree and grown green leaves, so people pray here for long life and good health. The path is marked and a rope is fixed along it, so you follow the rope. The soil is soft, so the trail can be slippery in snow or rain and dusty in wind. After the Blessed Tree, you pass natural formations such as the "Narrow Crevice," the "Shrine of Skillful Means" and the "Turning Gorge." You walk around them, squeeze through them and sometimes crawl. The meanings of some of the rocks are:

- **Shrine of Skillful Means.** In a gap between rocks that looks like a woman's body from a distance, the lama made a male symbol. People pray here, wishing to be born into a higher life in the next rebirth.
- **Mother's Vessel.** You squeeze in from the left side of the rock and "are born" out of the hole on the right. It is believed that this clears away the sins and ignorance of this life. Once you go in, you cannot turn back, and you have to crawl.
- **Yembuu Rock.** People walk around it three times clockwise and wish for good fortune and wealth.
- **Lama Rock.** From a distance it looks like a person sitting cross-legged. People bow to it as if to a monk and whisper a wish that Buddhism will flourish.
- **Heavenly Fist Rock.** A stone shaped as if struck by a fist. People worship it as a symbol of strength.
- **Footprint Rock.** People step on it with one foot and wish for a safe journey.

## What should I know before visiting Aglag Buteeliin Temple?

- **Steep path.** Cars go as far as the foot of the temple. The path up the slope of Garid Mountain is quite steep, so it may be hard for travelers with weak legs and for older people.
- **Spring water.** On the way up there is a spring with a tap, protected by a small shrine. It is said to be good for the stomach, and you can drink from it on a hot day.
- **No offerings.** You do not need to bring khadag (ceremonial silk scarf), vodka or milk, juniper incense, or food. This rule prevents litter and unpleasant smells. The main idea is to breathe clean air, rest your mind and take in good energy.
- **Clothing and shoes.** The hike has many places where you squeeze between rocks, crawl, or climb while holding a rope. Wear clothes that do not restrict movement and shoes with good grip. Note that on snowy, rainy or windy days, the soft soil is more slippery and dustier.
- **Tickets.** When you enter through the Asart Gate, adults and children buy separate tickets. Prices may change, so please check on site.

Southwest of the temple is the residence of the lama G. Purevbat. Southeast is the "Ikh Khishig" restaurant. In front of it are the lamas' quarters and the staff's gers. Around the monastery, animals such as hares, snakes, geese, chickens and dogs live without fear of people.

No. You do not need to bring khadag, vodka or milk, juniper incense, or food. This rule was set to prevent litter and unpleasant smells around the temple. The lama advises that what matters is to breathe clean air and rest your mind.

Gandantegchinlen Monastery, whose name means "Great Place of Complete Joy," is the center of Buddhism in Mongolia, in Ulaanbaatar. The first temple was founded in 1809 on Dalkh Hill as the "Yellow Temple." Today the monastery has six temples, and it came under state protection in 1994.

## What is Gandantegchinlen Monastery? Quick facts

- **Founded:** 1809, on Dalkh Hill, as the "Yellow Temple."
- **Protection:** Under state protection since 1994.
- **Temples:** Six.
- **Main sacred statue:** The 26-meter Megjid Janraisig (opened in 1996).
- **Library:** Founded in 1925, with more than 50,000 books and sutras.
- **Buddhist university:** Founded in 1970.

## What is the history of Gandan Monastery?

The Yellow Temple later became the Choir datsan (monastic college), and the monastery was expanded in stages:

- **1824.** The Lamrim temple.
- **1838.** The Great Temple of the Mandala for the 5th [Bogd Gegeen](/about/culture/religion/bogd-gegeen), along with Dashchoinkhor and Yagdachoilin.
- **1938.** The monastery was closed and some temples were destroyed.
- **1944.** Worshippers never stopped coming. Under strict Party and state control, the monastery held its first service on a day in early summer under the name "Pilgrimage Temple" and resumed its work.
- **1994.** It came under state protection.

The monastery's buildings follow Mongolian, Mongolian-Chinese and Chinese-Tibetan styles. They were built of wood, stone, brick and blue glazed ceramics. They are decorated with gilded finials, wheels of the Dharma, deer figures, roof animals and bells.

## What sacred treasures are in Gandan Temple?

Gandan Temple is the main temple of the monastery. Skilled craftsmen built it by decision of the 5th Bogd Gegeen. The main relics kept here include:

- A bronze cast of the Elder Khutagt, made by [Undur Gegeen Zanabazar](</about/history/people/undur-gegeen-zanabazar>).
- 108 volumes of the Kangyur, written in gold on black paper by Mongolian lamas in the 19th century.
- A portrait of the Buddha made by Gandan lamas in 1956 for the 2,500th anniversary of the Buddha.
- A cast Buddha made more than 2,000 years ago, presented in 1957 by Jawaharlal Nehru, the former Prime Minister of India.
- A silver stupa brought from Sri Lanka.
- A figure of [Bogd Zonkhov](/about/culture/religion/bogd-zonkhov) made of 15 kg of pure silver.

Of the "seven treasures" made by G. Zanabazar, the figures of Vajradhara, Ayush of the Kangyur, Manal of the Noyon statue, Dari Ekh, Namsrai and Maidar are also kept here.

## What is kept in the Gandan library?

The library was founded in 1925. It holds more than 50,000 books and sutras from the 19th century, when scholarship and culture flourished. They include:

- The Derge-print Kangyur and Tangyur, and the Urga-print Kangyur.
- Works by Tibetan scholars such as the Dalai Lama, Wanchin Bogd and Bogd Zonkhov.
- Works by Mongolian philosophers Agvaanbaldan, Agvaantuvden, Damtsagdorj and [Zava Damdin](</about/history/people/zava-damdin>).
- Sutras written in gold, silver and nine precious materials.
- Rare manuscripts in Lanydza, Sanskrit and Tibetan scripts.

These are important sources for studying Mongolian philosophy, written heritage, history and culture.

## What role does the monastery's school play?

Inside the walled courtyard of Gandan Temple stands a statue of Bogd Zonkhov. It was damaged and was restored in the 1950s and 1960s. In 1970, a Buddhist university was founded northwest of Gandan Temple. Teaching in choir (philosophy), manba (medicine), astrology and the arts, which had stopped after 1938, was revived. The "gavj" degree, awarded by the philosophy datsan, was awarded again, and Buddhist philosophy studies developed further.

## What is the Wishing Tree?

The monastery has a pillar known as the "Wishing Tree." It was one of the four main pillars of the original Gungaachoilin Temple. When the monastery was burned during the Great Repression, this pillar survived whole and unburned. People came to believe it grants wishes, much like a lone stone on the steppe that is worshipped until it becomes a deity. When Mongolians raise the main pillar of a temple, they traditionally survey the land, choose a young, clean tree with no knots and consecrate it. Worshippers believe the power of the consecration made more than 200 years ago still remains today.

![image](asset:tic-img-66b430dcf642ce4e)

## How was the Megjid Janraisig Temple built?

The 30-meter Megjid Janraisig Temple rises north of the Gandan Temple courtyard. According to sources, it was built around 1911, and at that time it was the tallest building in the capital's Khuree. For the four main pillars of the temple, young pine trees more than 80 elbow-lengths long were chosen and brought from [Noyon Mountain](</destination/heritage/place/noen-uulyn-bulsh>), where Xiongnu (Hunnu) kings are buried. It is said that worshippers sat in two rows from the bank of the [Tuul River](</destination/heritage/place/tuul-gol>) to the temple foundation. They passed water by hand in silver, copper, brass and sandalwood vessels brought from home, so that the construction water never touched the ground. The first statue was made around 1911 for Javzandamba Khutagt, the last [Bogd Khan](</about/history/people/bogd-khaan>) of Mongolia. In 1938 it was taken down and carried away to be used as raw material for Soviet weapons and ammunition. From then until 1990, the temple stood empty, without its main sacred statue.

## What makes the current Megjid Janraisig statue special?

The current 26-meter statue was built with donations from the people over six years from 1990, and opened to the public on October 27, 1996.

- **Body:** Made of 19 tons of copper.
- **Inside:** Precious stones from every province and historic site of Mongolia, samples of hot and cold mineral springs, rare medicinal plants, incense such as juniper and agar, grain samples, sacred sutras, historic texts such as "The Secret History of the Mongols," "Geser" and "Jangar," and a morin khuur (horsehead fiddle) strung with the tail hair of a Przewalski's horse (takhi) stallion. The books and sutras alone weigh 17 tons.
- **Gold plating:** 75,926 gold leaves, each 5×5 cm. Gilding the four hands in the traditional Eastern way took 1,356 g of gold.
- **Precious stones:** The "treasury hair" setting on the forehead holds 304 stones of 21 types collected from 22 provinces, and the crown holds 2,286 stones.
- **Clothing:** The chest garment (dodig) and apron (modig) were made from about 100 meters of fine silk. Gurdavaa Renbuuchii personally selected and brought this silk from Varanasi, India. A five-color khadag (ceremonial silk scarf) draped across the hands, 50 meters long, was woven in Varanasi.
- **Offering table:** The renowned carver L. Chuvaamid made a table 5 m long, 2 m wide and 1.55 m high. It is considered the largest such table ever made. On it stand the seven state treasures, more than an elbow-length tall, and the eight Buddhist offerings, crafted in copper and silver and decorated with gold.

A thousand Ayush Buddha figures line the wall around the statue. According to sources, half of them were ordered from Poland in 1911. Ayush is the Buddha of long life, and in his hands he holds a vase of medicinal nectar that heals 404 illnesses.

## What does the name Janraisig mean?

Janraisig means the one who looks with wide-open eyes at how the six kinds of beings, who were once our mothers, suffer. His heart mantra is the six syllables of mani that everyone knows. People learn this mantra without being taught and never forget it. No one asks "what comes after Om mani?" Those who know it do not boast to others that they know a lot of teachings. It is said that "Om mani padme hum" should be spoken not once but three times. Each hand of the statue carries meaning and symbolism:

- **The right lowered hand** holds a vase of nectar. The eternal water inside purifies all things, and it symbolizes that whoever can drink it gains eternal life.
- **The left lowered hand** holds a magic mirror. It means that Janraisig reflects people's bad deeds and corrects their sins.
- **The two meditation hands on the chest.** They show the eternal nectar gathered in the palm of the left hand being pressed with the right index finger and given to good people.

When the monastery was burned during the repression, this pillar of the Gungaachoilin Temple survived unburned, so people believe it grants wishes.

Erdene Zuu Monastery stands in [Uvurkhangai Province (aimag)](/province/uvurkhangai), in [Kharkhorin soum](/province/uvurkhangai/kharkhorin), next to the ruins of ancient [Kharkhorum (Karakorum)](</destination/landscapes/kharkhorum-khot>). It was the first major Buddhist monastery of the Khalkha. Builders brought stone from the ruins of Kharkhorum to construct it. The walled compound with 108 stupas is the monastery's most recognizable feature.

![image](asset:tic-img-313f36c3e1bf41c3)

## Erdene Zuu Monastery: What are the key facts?

- **Location:** Uvurkhangai Province, Kharkhorin soum, next to the ruins of Kharkhorum.
- **Founder:** Abtai Sain Khan.
- **Wall:** 108 stupas, each side 420 meters long.
- **Peak period:** By 1792, 62 temples, about 500 buildings and 10,000 lamas.
- **Today:** 18 temples remain.
- **Treasure fund:** 18 works from the monastery are registered in the national treasure fund.

## How was Erdene Zuu Monastery founded?

In 1580, Abtai Sain Khan met the Third Dalai Lama together with his younger brother, Prince Tumenkhen. On the Dalai Lama's advice, the Tahai Balgas temple, one of the old temples of Kharkhorum, was restored. This laid the foundation of Erdene Zuu. The monastery was built in stages over many centuries:

- **1586–1674.** The Three Temples (Gurvan Zuu) and the Rigsum Gombo Temple.
- **1675.** The Dalai Lama Temple, dedicated to the Dalai Lama of Tibet.
- **1701–1705.** The Ochirdari Temple, the Avid Temple and the Great Stupa complex.
- **1771–1792.** The Tsogchin Main Temple and the Lavrin Temple.
- **1782.** The Bodi Mor school with 50 novice monks.
- **1796.** The first major renovation. The Seventh Shiret Lama Dagvadarjaa led it, and the Chinese firm Yun-da-a carried it out. At this time, 50 stupas were built on the foundation of the collapsed old Tahai Balgas wall.
- **1802–1813.** Nobles, lamas, monks and devout people funded 56 more stupas between the existing ones, and the wall was completed.

Mongolian nobles and aristocrats considered it an honor to have their own temple inside the wall. As a result, many temples were built, including those of Tusheet Khan and Prince Tsevenjav, and the Ochirdari Temple. By 1792, the monastery had grown to 62 temples, about 500 buildings and 10,000 lamas.

## What remains of Erdene Zuu Monastery today?

Today 18 temples remain. Of the original buildings, the Three Temples, the Zuu Temple, the Great Stupa and the Cool Lavrin Temple have survived. In the monastery courtyard, you can see stone statues found in the ruins of Kharkhorum.

- **Dalai Lama Temple.** Built in 1675. It displays 13 wrathful forms of Mahakala, the figures of six incarnations of the Dalai Lama, and the figure of Undur Gegeen [Zanabazar](</about/history/people/undur-gegeen-zanabazar>). The Khurden temples stand on its two outer sides.
- **Two tombs.** Inside a wall made of special bricks, there are two tombs. One belongs to Abtai Sain Khan. The other holds the remains of his son, Tusheet Khan Gombodorj.

## What makes the Three Temples special?

The Three Temples stand behind the tombs. They were built without a single nail, with timber joined by fitting. They have double walls. The double walls insulate against heat and cold, and they also allowed worshippers to walk around the inside in prayer. The Three Temples were places of pilgrimage, not assembly halls for chanting.

![image](asset:tic-img-439f48d7f18cec22)

### West Temple

Abtai Sain Khan, his son Gombodorj and the principal queen of Erkhii Mergen Khan commissioned it. In the place of honor sit Odser, the past form of [Buddha](</about/culture/religion/burkhan-bagsh>), the present Shakyamuni, and the elderly form of Maitreya, the future Buddha. The west wall shows [Bogd Zonkhov](/about/culture/religion/bogd-zonkhov) with his disciples. The east wall shows the Buddha with his disciples.

### Central Temple

Tusheet Khan Zungen and the official Tsogt built it. On both sides of the entrance are ten guardian deities, Lham, and Gombogur, the main deity of Erdene Zuu. In the place of honor sit Medicine Buddha Manal, the Buddha in his youth, and a statue of Avid. There are also standing and seated statues of the Buddha's two disciples Modgoljibuu and Shaaribuu, the sun deity Nyam, the moon deity Davaa, and the Eight Wise Ones. On the central table are Zanabazar's brass, repoussé, embossed and cast Buddhas. The second floor is called "the Realm of the Thousand Buddhas of the Good Age, descended from the sky."

### East Temple

Erkhii Mergen Khan, his son Gombodorj and Khanjamts, queen of Tusheet Khan, commissioned it. In the place of honor are Janraisag, the Buddha in childhood, and a statue of Bogd Zonkhov. The west wall shows the Buddha, Manal and the 35 Buddhas of Confession. The east wall shows Bogd Zonkhov and his disciples. On the central table are a model of the east temple made of brown sandalwood, gilded repoussé, copper embossed and brass cast figures, wood-carved Buddhas, bodhisattvas and guardian deities.

## What is inside the Tsamba and Ayush temples?

### Tsamba Temple

It stands slightly below the Three Temples, on the west side. It has three chambers. In each chamber, four lamas sat and read scriptures.

- **Chamber 1:** Ganzai (the wish-granting jewel), Damdinsamduv and Jigjid.
- **Chamber 2:** Janraisag, Luvenjalbuu (lord of the water spirits), Jamsran, Lham, Mahakala, Vandanshavaraba (the hunters' deity) and Gongor.
- **Chamber 3:** Erleg Nomun Khan, Demchig (main deity of secret tantra), Gombogur's Jinkhor, Ochirdari, Shanbol's Shangod, Sendom (companion of Baldanlham), Gombogur (one of the 75 Mahakalas) and Tsamba.

Mongolians believe that if you worship the Tsamba deity, all your undertakings will flow like a hundred springs.

### Ayush Temple

It faces the Tsamba Temple, on the east side.

- **Chamber 1:** Paintings in appliqué and mineral pigment depicting Jigjid, Manal, Avid, Khayankhyarvaa, Damdinsamduv, the Fifth Dalai Lama and Baldanlham. On the walls, ganzai offerings are painted for the deities and for paradise. They show the five senses, the eight religious offerings and the seven treasures of the state as offerings.
- **Chamber 2:** Ganzai offerings, Medicine Buddha Manal, Damdinsamduv and Damdindorlig.
- **Chamber 3:** Narkhajid, a wrathful tantric deity of the red stream, Ochirdari, Ayush and the Three Longevity Deities, along with other deities who grant long life and good health.

## What are the Golden Stupa and the Lavrin Temple?

East of the Three Temples stands a large white pilgrimage stupa called the Golden Stupa. It was built in 1799 for the Fourth Bogd Gegeen. Inside are more than 70 bodhi stupas, more than 2,500 tsatsa (votive clay figures) and 100,000 Buddhas, along with other religious items. The stupa is 13 meters tall. Beside it stands the three-story Lavrin Temple, restored in 1969–1973. It is a monument of pure Tibetan-style architecture. Inside are statues of deities, portraits, appliqué works, embroidered Buddhas, tsam masks, horns and trumpets, bells, vajras and drums.

## What was Ulziit Khutagt Square used for?

The large square at the center of the monastery is called Ulziit Khutagt Square. Legend says that Abtai Sain Khan's ger once stood here. Later, in 1658, a huge ger called "Bat-Ulziit" was built for the assembly of Khalkha nobles.

- **Size:** Its circular frame was 45 m in diameter and 15 m high. Its traces still remain.
- **Structure:** It had 35 wall sections and 1,700 roof poles.
- **Capacity:** 200 people.
- **Banners:** Outside the hall, the banners of the four provinces were raised on four banner bases.
- **Sacred lake:** In a hollow in front of the ger, an artificial lake was made by drawing water from the Orkhon River through a narrow channel.

Inside the walled compound, there is an area for ceremonies such as tsam dances. Here, 108 tsam deities, divided into six large groups, used to dance. In the monastery grounds, there is a Shurguun fence made of black wood without a single nail. It symbolized protection from enemies. During religious ceremonies and tsam dances, it also served as a barrier to keep livestock and crowds out.

## Why is Erdene Zuu Monastery so valuable?

The monastery's temples have preserved the work of 17th-century Mongolian sculptors, painters and metalworkers. These include hand paintings, scroll patterns, carvings, sculptures, cast Buddhas, stupas, cauldrons, treasury vessels, bowls, bells, vajras, ritual implements, mandalas set with precious stones, tsam masks and costumes. By government resolution, 18 works from the monastery were registered in the national treasure fund and ranked as priceless. The book "Mongolian Historical and Cultural Heritage," published by UNESCO, describes Erdene Zuu as the only monastery built according to traditional Mongolian city planning, with architecture like that of the palace of Kharkhorum. Visitors from Mongolia and abroad come in every season. According to the source, more than 20,000 people visit each year on average.

### How many stupas does the Erdene Zuu wall have?

The Choijin Lama Temple Museum is in Sukhbaatar District of [Ulaanbaatar](/province/ulaanbaatar), behind the Government Palace. It was built in 1908 for Luvsankhaidav, the younger brother of the Eighth [Bogd Khan](</about/history/people/bogd-khaan>), who became famous as the "[Choijin Lama](/about/culture/religion/choijin-lam)." Its Mongolian name means "Temple that Spreads Compassion." The temple was built for one person and follows the Red Hat tradition. It has been a religious museum since 1942 and today consists of five temples.

![image](asset:tic-img-9d0ed139a3baad39)

## What are the key facts about the Choijin Lama Temple Museum?

- **Built:** 1908.
- **Religious services:** held until 1936, closed in 1938.
- **Became a museum:** 1942.
- **Protection:** placed under first-class state protection in 1939.
- **Temples:** the Main Temple, Zankhan Temple, Zuu Temple, Yadam Temple, and Enkh Amgalan Temple.
- **Address:** Sukhbaatar District, east of the UB Mart shopping center.
- **Website:** templemuseum.mn.

## Who was the Choijin Lama?

Luvsankhaidav was born in 1872 in a place called Shola in Tibet, the fifth son of Gonchigtseren. He came to Mongolia in 1874, at the age of two. In 1883–1884, Yonzon Khamba Baldanchoimbol, the teacher of the Bogd Gegeen, determined that the Choijin protector spirit resided in him. Choijin Setev Lama was then invited from Tibet to set Luvsankhaidav on the path to becoming a Choijin. A Mongolian lama of Khuree named Luvsanpeljee taught him all the recitations for the [Choijin trance ritual](/about/culture/religion/choijin-buukh). This lama later became his interpreter, known as Gombo or Soivon Lama, during the trance rituals. When Luvsankhaidav performed the Choijin ritual for the first time, the Eighth Bogd Gegeen came in person and honored him with a khadag (ceremonial silk scarf). After the Bogd Khan state was founded, Luvsankhaidav was awarded a long honorific title, a hutagt's seal, the seal of the Choijin protector spirit, and the seal of the Temple that Spreads Compassion. The Choijin ritual is one in which the Choijin Lama calls the spirits of wrathful deities into his body and performs their actions and movements. The Zankhan Temple was built for him, and he performed the ritual at least six times a month. The Eighth Bogd belonged to the Yellow Hat tradition but accepted the Red Hat tradition, so he visited this temple 4–5 times every month. The Choijin Lama collapsed and died in 1918 while performing the ritual.

## What is there to see in the Main Temple?

At the back of the hall, [Buddha](</about/culture/religion/burkhan-bagsh>) sits with his two disciples, Modgoljibuu and Shaaribuu. On the right-hand seat is the temple's owner, the Choijin Lama, and on the left-hand seat is his teacher, Yonzon Khamba. The Choijin Lama's body was completely burned, so it could not be embalmed. His statue was therefore made in papier-mâché, using his ashes. His teacher, who died in 1910 while seated, was embalmed. The Main Temple also holds the following items, most of them made by Khuree craftsmen in the 19th century:

- A seat with four tiers for the senior lamas who ranked after the Choijin Lama.
- Religious musical instruments used in tsam (masked ritual dance) and daily services.
- A stone tiger painting depicting the 16 Naidan disciples.
- A thangka of the Three Deities of Longevity, and tsam masks.

### The Jamsran and Gombo tsam masks

One of the museum's most valuable exhibits is the Jamsran deity tsam mask and costume made by Puntsag-Osor, a famous craftsman of Khuree. The mask is set with 7,000 red coral beads. It weighs 30 kg by itself and 70 kg together with the costume. The eyebrows, beard, and belt were made of gold, and the costume is decorated with fine embroidery. Opposite it, the Gombo deity tsam mask is another valuable exhibit, with white decorations on the costume made of elephant ivory. Other rare works include:

- A ganlin trumpet made from the shinbone of an 18-year-old girl, and a damar drum made from a skull.
- Copper repoussé statues of the 16 Naidan disciples.
- A thousand clay statues of Buddha and Bogd Zonkhov.
- A statue of Erlig Nomun Khan and a painting of the "hot and cold hells."
- Two wood carvings, "The Realm of Maitreya" and "The Realm of Avid."
- "Otoch Naiman Manal," embroidered with khadag threads.

## What was the Zankhan Temple used for?

The Zankhan Temple adjoins the Main Temple. At its back stands the throne of the Eighth Bogd Khan. The Bogd Khan came 4–5 times a month and had the Choijin perform the trance ritual to ask about future matters of state and religion. On the right side of the temple stand statues of Choijin, or wrathful deities, each about 2 meters tall:

- Ochirvaani and Naijinchoijin, protectors of the faith
- Damdinsamduv, protector of fast horses
- [Bogd Zonkhov](/about/culture/religion/bogd-zonkhov), founder of the Yellow Hat tradition, and [Lovon Badamjunai](/about/culture/religion/lovon-badamjunai), founder of the Red Hat tradition
- Jigjid, embracing his consort
- Perenleijalbuu, who brings work to completion
- Zemerchoijin, a war deity on horseback
- Dashtsermee, a goddess who protects women from all evil
- Dorjugden, the most powerful giver of wealth
- Regdendagva, a hero who subdues enemies of the faith

Paintings of the realm of hell hang from the ceiling. They remind visitors that anyone who commits sins suffers there.

## What is in the Zuu Temple?

The Zuu Temple is dedicated to Buddha. At its back sit Odser, Buddha, and Maitreya, his past, present, and future forms. On the offering table are the seven treasures of a ruler. On the walls are papier-mâché statues of the 16 Naidan disciples in meditation. At the entrance is Baldanlham, one of the ten wrathful guardian deities. On the right stands a statue of Naijinchoijin, a protector of the faith.

## Why was the Yadam Temple kept secret?

Yadam is a secret tantric temple for a sacred deity. The Choijin Lama entered it alone to meditate. It contains paired deities that represent the union of method and wisdom.

- **At the back** is the Choijin Lama's main deity, Duvchinnagvajudeg, one of the 84 Indian mahasiddhas.
- **Above**, in a wooden gungervaa shrine, are the longevity deities [White Tara](</about/culture/religion/tsagaan-dari-ekh>), [Ayush](</about/culture/religion/ayuush-burkhan>), and Jugdernamjil.
- **On the offering table** are the eight religious offerings, and to the left is a wood carving of the Jamsran deity.
- **In the upper right** hang figures representing people of the five continents. Their mouths are tied with string, symbolizing that people are united by mind and speech.
- **On two pillars** hang guns, symbolizing the power of sound to drive away evil.

## What is there to see in the Enkh Amgalan Temple?

This temple is dedicated to the First Bogd, G. Zanabazar. At its back sits a statue that Zanabazar made in his own likeness, with Ayush and Buddha and two disciples on either side. Above is a papier-mâché figure of the Three Deities of Longevity. In the center of the temple are rare items, including statues made by Zanabazar and his disciples, Chinese and Tibetan statues, a Japanese-style Bogd Zonkhov, and a "bronze stupa" of Indian make that Undur Gegeen brought himself. Along the walls, papier-mâché statues of the 16 Naidan deities show 16 human traits: angry, calm, stingy, gloomy, lazy, compassionate, cunning, hardworking, cheerful, flattering, kind-hearted, and more.

## How are the temple and the Government Palace connected?

The temple was placed under first-class state protection in 1939. The Government Palace was built right behind it in 1951. As a result, the temple's Choijin protector spirits came to be seen as guarding the Mongolian state. The State Flag on top of the palace and the golden finial of the Main Temple stand on one line. Until 1960, the museum was closed. It served only for research and for foreign delegations with special permission, by appointment. The museum is unique because it preserves original works of religious art made for one influential hutagt who lived at a particular time. The grandeur of the temple reflected the hutagt's influence and power and inspired awe in many people.

## How do I get to the Choijin Lama Temple Museum?

The museum is in Sukhbaatar District, east of the UB Mart shopping center. Opening hours differ between winter and summer, and it closes on some days of the week. Opening hours, ticket prices, photo and video fees, and the phone number may change. Please check the official website (templemuseum.mn) before you go.

- **Founded:** 1820, by D. Danzanravjaa.
- **Tradition:** Red Hat Buddhism.
- **Before 1938:** More than 80 temples and more than 500 lamas.
- **Main sights:** Old Man Stupa, Shambhala, Eyes of the Buddha, Mother's Womb Cave, Wish Black Mountain, Dragon Ravine.

## What was Khamar Monastery like before 1938?

The monastery had four parts: Zuun Khuree, Baruun Khuree, Tsokhon and Duinkhor. It was a large Red Hat monastery with four divisions: the Lamas, the Khamba, the Bagsh and the Nomch. It had the Avga, Choir, [Lamrim](/about/culture/religion/lamrim), Duinkhor and Children's datsans (monastic colleges), more than 80 temples and more than 500 lamas. Danzanravjaa believed that Red Hat Buddhism suited the Mongolian body and mind best. He aimed to criticize and reform the society of his time.

- **The first theater.** In the 1830s, a theater called "Namtar Duulakh Datsan" was founded at the monastery. Researchers consider it the first theater in the history of Mongolian theater arts.
- **The Children's Datsan.** He set up a school that supported not only children but also women in learning to read and write.

## How was the heritage of Khamar Monastery preserved?

When the monastery was destroyed, the lama G. Tudev rescued 64 chests of items from the temple holding the remains of the Noyon Khutagt. They were buried in the ground or hidden in caves. Generations passed them down for 52 years, until 1990. The monastery's activities revived after these items were returned to it. According to the sources, more than 10 lamas now hold prayer services at the monastery.

## What makes the Old Man Stupa special?

In 1823, the Noyon Khutagt built this stupa to help the Mongol state flourish, to keep people free from illness and suffering, and to keep the land free from drought and dzud (a harsh winter that kills livestock). The stupa blessed worshippers until 1937, when it was torn down. The new [Old Man Stupa](</destination/heritage/place/uvgun-suvarga>), built next to the monastery, opened on September 10, 2011. The sources call it the largest stupa in Mongolia.

- **Shape:** It is shaped like a Mongolian ger, has five sides and is 32 meters tall.
- **Sacred images:** On its five sides are the five forms of the [Buddha](</about/culture/religion/burkhan-bagsh>), the five root Buddhas. A soyombo symbol sits at the top.
- **Sacred contents:** Many bricks of tea, 7 tons of juniper, two five-wall gers with full furnishings, Buddhist statues, a clay figure of a lama performing a ritual with Red Hat texts, and silk and brocade. In the top shrine, the true sacred contents were prepared and sealed according to the Noyon Khutagt's teaching.
- **Life tree:** The main central pole was brought from Minjiin Khangai in Batsumber soum, Tuv Province.

A rope was lowered into the chamber below the stupa, with a large vajra tied to its end. The vajra was placed on the table in the place of honor of the chamber, where portraits of the Buddha and Guru Rinpoche stand. It is said that bowing to the vajra is the same as bowing to the Buddha.

## What is the land of Shambhala?

In 1851, Danzanravjaa and his disciples held the Kalachakra teaching, also called the Duinkhor service, at the Duinkhor datsan of Khamar Monastery. After a cham dance, he marked the path to the land of Shambhala and gave a blessing for many believers to follow. The monastery grounds are considered full of energy, with a vein of life force beneath them, and he identified the point from which to travel to Shambhala. Shambhala is a realm of the mind and a land of the Buddhas. It can be reached only through spirit and thought. Its master is said to be the Red Hero. Shambhala has 25 kings, and each year the monastery's lamas make offerings and read scriptures for them. Today a courtyard with stupas surrounds the energy center, and 77 volumes of the Gaadamba scriptures are kept there. It is said to be best to visit the energy center at sunrise.

### Eyes of the Buddha

On the wall known as the gate of Shambhala, the eyes of the Buddha are painted. They are said to be the eyes of the person who gave wisdom to the third world, which reached great enlightenment before the world changed. People believe that gazing at the eyes and entrusting them with cleansing the wrongs done by body, speech and mind works as a ritual for someone who has not had a ritual read for them. When you focus your thoughts and gaze, you may feel the ring between your eyebrows turn, your eyes blink, and the dark frame of your nose change with different lights. It is said that if you feel this, your energy is good. If not, you should gather good energy from Shambhala.

### The circuit order

First, pray three times at the ovoo (sacred stone cairn) on the northern terrace of the energy center. Next, offer food to the cups on the altar. Then go to the stupa on the south side, which is carved with the words of the "Ulemjiin Chanar" song, and sing the whole song with all five verses. The pilgrimage ends when you leave through the silver gate of Shambhala.

## What is the legend of the Sakura Valley?

It is said that the Noyon Khutagt debated and shared his learning not only in Mongolian but also in Manchu and Tibetan. Legend says he mastered the star-shaped weapons that ninjas use today. A samurai once saw him cut a tree branch by throwing a star. He admired him and became his disciple. He stayed three years in Shambhala, but it is said he could not learn the skill. When he returned home, he planted sakura as a gift for his teacher. The samurai's sword is kept in the Khutagt's museum.

## What is the Mother's Womb Cave?

People believe that entering and leaving this cave makes you feel reborn and pure like a newborn child. Those who are "reborn" from the womb go to another rock and rub the sick part of their body on it, hoping for healing. Two km northeast of the monastery are many caves where lamas used to meditate. There are also worshipped rocks, such as the mother's womb rock and the rock that heals illness and misfortune.

## How do I pay respects at Wish Black Mountain?

[Wish Black Mountain](</destination/landscapes/khusliin-khar-uul>) lies more than 20 km northwest of the monastery. Gobi people have worshipped it since ancient times. Its official name is Khan Bayanzurkh. Locals also call it Wish Mountain, Black Mountain and Remains Mountain.

- There are two routes up the mountain and two rest stops.
- About 100 meters up from the base is a small temple. Women, children and elders come this far and perform the sacred mountain rituals here.
- Men climb to the peak, where worship takes place. They circle the ovoo at the top and whisper their wishes.

In the past, the ovoo at the top was decorated with offerings, ganjir (a roof finial), khadag (ceremonial silk scarves) and khiimori (a person's vital energy and good fortune) prayer flags. Before 1930, the top of the ovoo had a ganjir with a crown set with gems, like a king's hat. "Khan" is a title given to this mountain. Researchers believe that about 120 mountains in Mongolia carry titles such as Khan, Bogd, Gun, Beel, Bees and Zaisan.

## What is the Dragon Ravine?

The Dragon Ravine lies 1.5 km north of the monastery and covers an area of 2 hectares, stretching from north to south. Researchers found bones of a giant plant-eating dinosaur called Iogundant in this ravine, which has red, yellow and blue clay hills. It also holds many fossilized bones of other dinosaurs and remains of sea animals and plants, such as shells.

### Where is Khamar Monastery?

It lies 45 km south of Sainshand, the provincial center of Dornogovi.

### Who founded Khamar Monastery?

The Gobi Noyon Khutagt D. Danzanravjaa founded it in 1820, at the age of 17.

### Where was Mongolia's first theater founded?

In the 1830s, Danzanravjaa founded a theater called "Namtar Duulakh Datsan" at Khamar Monastery. Researchers consider it Mongolia's first theater.

The stupa, built in 2011, is shaped like a Mongolian ger and has five sides. It is 32 meters tall.
