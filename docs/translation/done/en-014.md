## What are the key facts about Bayanzag?

- **Location:** Umnugovi Province, in the valley along the Arts Bogd mountains.
- **Size:** 5 km wide and about 8 km long, stretching from south to north.
- **Cliff height:** 20–50 m.
- **Foreign name:** "Flaming Cliffs."
- **Finds:** fossilized dinosaur bones from 60–80 million years ago.

## What are tsav and tsonj?

Mongolians call a place where red, sandy clay has hardened "tsav," and a slightly higher part "tsonj." The cliffs of Bayanzag are 20–50 m high. Locals also call some formations "table clay" and "camel clay" because of their shapes.

## Why is Bayanzag called the "Flaming Cliffs"?

In the evening sun, the red clay soil looks like glowing fire. That is why the place is called the "Flaming Cliffs." It is said that American scientists, who did research at Bayanzag in the 1920s, gave it this name.

## Why is Bayanzag important for paleontology?

During the research by American scientists, many new discoveries of fossilized dinosaur remains from 60–80 million years ago were made. Because of this, Bayanzag became a world-famous site in the field of paleontology.

## What can you find in the saxaul forest at Bayanzag?

The northern part of Bayanzag has a saxaul forest. Traces of ancient people who lived here 40,000 years ago, and even earlier, are still being found in this forest.

Because saxaul trees grow here in large numbers.

It is the name given by American scientists to the red cliffs of Bayanzag, which look like they are glowing in the evening sun.

- **Location:** Uvurkhangai Province, about 10 km southwest of Zuunbayan-Ulaan soum.

Argali and Siberian ibex make up most of the images. The rest include:

## What is mare's milk made of?

- protein: 2.2 percent;
- lactose: 6.6 percent;
- vitamin C: 2.5–8.5 mg.

One source also says mare's milk contains 6–8 percent sugar.

## How is airag fermented?

Mongolians have made and drunk [airag](</about/traditions/airag>) for more than 2,000 years. To make it, herders strain and clean the fresh milk, mix it with a starter, and churn it 3,000–5,000 times. During fermentation, the milk sugar breaks down into alcohol and carbon dioxide.

- It contains almost all vitamins, including A, C, B, and E.
- It has more minerals than cow's milk, such as calcium, phosphorus, cobalt, and copper, and it is easy to digest.
- It is considered healing, restorative, and nutritious, and it can kill bacteria.
- One liter of airag contains 20 g of fat, 23 g of protein, and 35 g of sugars, and it provides 453.7 kcal.

Mongolians milk mares to make airag, and at Naadam they also race six-year-old horses. To read about other works by the same sculptor, see the page on the [four statues of the Airag Complex](</destination/heritage/place/airag-tsogtsolboryn-durvun-barimal>).
