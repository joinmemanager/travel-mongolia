Nalaikh District is a district of the capital, 36 km southeast of Ulaanbaatar, famous for the Nalaikh coal mine. This settlement, which began with black stones dug out of marmot burrows, has long played an important role in supplying the capital with coal and electricity.

## Where does the name Nalaikh come from?

The place was once a low ridge stretching from north to south at the southeastern edge of the city, called "Naluu Ukhaa" (Sloping Hill). It was land with good pasture and many marmots and squirrels. It is said that when Chinese traders noticed the black stones coming out of marmot burrows and began hauling them by donkey cart, the people of Khuree learned what burning coal was. The Chinese kept mispronouncing the name "Naluu Ukhaa" until it became "Nalaikh".

## What is the history of the Nalaikh mine?

- **1912.** Chinese traders began mining coal here and hauling it to the capital, Niislel Khuree.
- **December 25, 1922.** The Nalaikh mine officially became a state enterprise under Niislel Khuree.
- **1926.** A 60-kilowatt power station was built in Ulaanbaatar, increasing demand for coal from Nalaikh.
- **1939.** The power station was expanded.
- **October 11, 1940.** The Ulaanbaatar–Nalaikh railway opened.
- **1954–1958.** The large Nalaikh mine was built.
- **1962.** A decision was made to found the Nalaikh committee around the mine.
- **1992.** Nalaikh became a district.

## Where is Nalaikh District?

The district is 36 km southeast of the capital, 1,050–2,060 meters above sea level. It borders [Bayanzurkh District](/province/ulaanbaatar#bayanzurkh) and Sergelen and Erdene soums of Tuv Province. The northern part lies in the forest-steppe zone and the southern part in the steppe zone. For the capital's other districts, read the [Ulaanbaatar page](/province/ulaanbaatar). Its area is 68,800 hectares, or 68,764 hectares exactly, and it is divided into 6 khoroos (sub-districts). The 2011 count found 31,422 people and 39,709 head of livestock.

## What natural resources and infrastructure does Nalaikh have?

Nalaikh is rich in natural resources such as coal, gold, tin, crystal, lime, mineral water, medicinal plants, sand, clay and gravel. Its infrastructure is fairly well developed. It is linked to Ulaanbaatar by rail and road and has reliable sources of heat and clean water. The 220 kV high-voltage power line and the fiber-optic communication cable that supply southeastern Mongolia pass through the district, making it an economic hub. It is directly connected to Ulaanbaatar's upper source of clean water. For communications it has international and intercity digital telephone exchanges, email and internet networks, and Mobicom and Skytel mobile networks. It also has a heating plant that can produce 60 Gcal of heat per hour. All this makes it a district that can quickly connect with any country in the world.

## What are its prospects for the future?

Today Nalaikh is seen as having strong potential for small-scale mining, garment making, small and medium food production, and tourism. In recent years foreign organizations and investors have shown interest in working here.
