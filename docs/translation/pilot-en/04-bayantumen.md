Bayantumen soum lies just 12 km from Choibalsan, the center of Dornod Province (aimag), and grew up supplying the city with food, milk and vegetables. The soum center is the settlement of Tsagaan Ders. Many locals call this soum "Khan Uul".

## Where is Bayantumen soum?

The soum center, the settlement of Tsagaan Ders, is 645 km from Ulaanbaatar and 12 km from [Choibalsan (Kherlen soum)](/province/dornod/kherlen). The soum gets its electricity from Choibalsan's power grid. For other soums and sights of the province, read the [Dornod Province page](/province/dornod). The soum covers 832,080 hectares in total, of which 478,411 hectares are pasture, 33,523 hectares are hayfields and 324 hectares are cropland.

## How did the soum come about?

The soum grew out of the "Khudulmur" cooperative, founded in January 1950. It was started by S. Batmunkh, then head of the Choibalsan city administration, with seven people including O. Gansukh, D. Danzan, Tumur and Namst, in the city's 6th district. The cooperative's aim was to supply the provincial center with vegetables. In 1952 the cooperative moved from Choibalsan to a place called "Tsagaan Ders". In the 1940s this had been the vegetable fields and supply farm of the Soviet 17th Army, and the settlers moved into the few small buildings left behind. In 1958 the "Bayan" cooperative of Kherlenbayan soum merged with the "Khudulmur" cooperative. Then, by decision of the Presidium of the Little Khural, Bayantumen soum was founded in March 1959, and the cooperative was renamed "Bayasgalant Khudulmur". S. Batmunkh served as head of both the soum and the cooperative, and J. Ulziikhutag as secretary. In the year the soum was founded, a primary school (principal Yu. Sumiyaa), a clinic (physician assistant A. Jambaa), a kindergarten (head B. Nadmid) and a trade and procurement unit (head Ishjamts) opened. In 1960 a state artificial insemination station and a veterinary branch were set up.

## Why is it called a suburban farming soum?

Bayantumen developed as a farming soum serving the nearby city. Between 1974 and 1990, large farm facilities were built here, including a mechanized dairy farm for 400 cows and a 1,000-hectare irrigation system. The soum grew silage and green fodder on 500–1,000 hectares and supplied one million liters of milk a year to Choibalsan's food plant. It also grew grain, potatoes and vegetables on 1,000–1,500 hectares. The population and livestock grew year by year, and in 1990 the cooperative's fixed assets reached MNT 41.4 million. At the exchange rate of the time, it earned MNT 7.8 million a year. During the transition to a market economy, the cooperative, some institutions, and the livestock and property of the dairy farm passed to companies and individuals.

## What are the nature and climate like?

The soum is mostly flat, with low mountains such as Galyn Shil (893 m), Mankhtai (856 m) and Bor Undur (850 m). The soils are brown steppe soils and saline soils. The Kherlen River and small lakes such as Tsaidam and Khotont are in the soum. The climate is continental. January temperatures average −22 to −24 °C, and July temperatures +20 to +22 °C. Average annual precipitation is 250 mm. Wildlife includes the Mongolian gazelle, wolf, fox, corsac fox and Pallas's cat. The dense steppe vegetation is dominated by feather grass, wild rye, wild onion and tana onion. Locals worship mountains and ovoos (sacred stone cairns) such as Enkh, Munkh, Khutag and Khan Uul.

## How many people and livestock does the soum have?

Bayantumen soum has 2,100 people and 3 administrative bags. It has 95,000 head of livestock in total, including 670 camels, 15,400 horses, 9,800 cattle, 43,000 sheep and 26,700 goats.
