Dadal sum lies in the far northeast of Khentii Province (aimag), in the basin of the Onon River, and is remembered in history and legend as the birthplace of Chinggis Khaan (Genghis Khan). The sum was founded in 1923. Today visitors can see pine forests, clear lakes, sacred mountains and many sites linked to legends of Chinggis Khaan's childhood, all in one place. You can also get to know Buryat daily life and culture, traditional food and handicrafts.

## Where is Dadal sum and how far is it from Ulaanbaatar?

Dadal sum is in the far northeast of Mongolia. It is 600 km from the capital, Ulaanbaatar, and 270 km from the provincial center, Undurkhaan. The road is rough, so prepare well for a long trip. To the north the sum borders Russia. It borders Bayan-Uul sum of Dornod Province to the east, Norovlin sum of Khentii Province to the south, Bayan-Adarga to the southwest and Binder to the west. For other sums and sights of the province, see the [Khentii Province page](/province/khentii). Geographically, the sum covers the eastern mountains of the Khentii Range, the Ereen Range and the Onon River basin. The sum center is in Bayan-Ovoo bag. It sits at the foot of the scenic Bayan-Ovoo Mountain, near the legendary Deluun Boldog, south of the Balj River.

## How many people live in Dadal and how is it divided?

Dadal sum has four bags: Bayan-Ovoo, Agats, Onon and Balj. In 2017 the sum had 890 households and 2,891 residents. Most residents are Buryat, and this shows clearly in the sum's daily life, customs, food and handicrafts.

## What are the nature and climate like?

The sum lies 800 meters above sea level. Its lowest point is the confluence of the Onon and Balj rivers, and its highest is Bugat Mountain (1,513 m) at the head of the Suugt, in the bend of the Balj. Here the southern edge of the Siberian taiga gives way to the Daurian steppe. Thanks to this border position, the area has many species of animals and plants, but its nature is very fragile. The climate is humid and cool. Averaged over the last 10 years, precipitation is 3.2 mm in the winter months, 12.2 mm in spring, 104.8 mm in summer and 17.6 mm in autumn. Winter snow cover is about 40 cm deep. Very rare animals include the white-naped crane, the taimen and the Eurasian otter. Gray wolves, foxes, corsac foxes, lynx, wild boar and mountain hares also live here. Rare plants include the large-flowered lady's slipper and the white peony. Berries and medicinal plants are plentiful: Asian bird cherry, wild strawberry, dog rose, hawthorn, currant, honeysuckle, lingonberry, blueberry and cowberry. The sum's eco-tourism plan is based on the nearby [Onon-Balj National Park](</destination/protected/onon-balj>).

## What is Buryat culture and the Altargana festival?

The people of Dadal sum continue to develop the traditional herding, handicrafts and cultural heritage of the Buryat people. They have long used horse carts and wooden troughs in daily life, and they carve cups, ladles, spoons and plates from wood and tree roots to sell as souvenirs. To support Buryat folk culture and art, the Altargana Buryat song festival was first organized in this sum in 1994. It has since grown into a major international festival. When the festival's 20th anniversary was celebrated in Dadal sum, more than 10,000 guests and delegates took part, and contestants competed in 13 arts and sports events showcasing Buryat culture and traditions. That year, A. Saikhantamir, a student of the Mongolian University of Science and Technology from Dadal, won second place and a silver medal in the Dangina contest. In the Buryat song category, singer N. Ganzolboo also took second place and a silver medal with the song "Galttaimni".

## What products is Dadal sum known for?

**Buryat bread.** This bread, famous across the country, is special because it rises on its own without yeast. It has been named the best of the best three times at Khentii's autumn fair. **Cream, clotted cream and urum.** The herders' main source of income is selling milk and dairy products. Dadal's cream has twice been named the best of the best at the Khentii Province fair. Separated cream (zuukhii) is the signature product of Buryat herders. Urum (clotted cream) is made by mixing separated cream with bird cherry or other berries, and "Dadal urum with bird cherry" is well known in the sum and province. **Cattle herds.** Since 1964 the sum has worked to improve its cattle breeds and now raises Simmental cattle. Today cattle make up 33.1 percent of all livestock in the sum.

## What farming and jobs are there?

Dadal sum has 7,119 hectares of crop rotation land in total. Three companies farm it. Two of them, Dadal Buudai LLC and Enkh Nogoon Tal LLC, provide seasonal jobs for 25–30 people and permanent jobs for 8. Potatoes and vegetables are also grown on 25–30 hectares. Forty-six residents grow fruit and berries on 12 hectares to meet their households' needs. The vegetable needs of the sum's public institutions are fully met by local harvests, which give seasonal work to 20 people.

## What does the sum aim for in the future?

The sum's overall development goal is to really reduce unemployment and poverty and to improve people's livelihoods. It aims to become a model sum that develops agriculture, small and medium businesses and tourism, uses its natural resources wisely and keeps its nature in its original state. This goal is broken down into several directions. **Green economy.** Support green production and services based on modern eco-technology. Develop a diverse economy combining agriculture, production of natural products, tourism, and spa and sanatorium services. Develop intensive agriculture with technology that produces few greenhouse gases and little waste and poses no risk to human health or the environment, and increase the output of natural products. In this way residents are expected to earn a steady income from business. **Eco-tourism.** The sum plans to develop eco-tourism in three directions, "History – Nature – Spa and Wellness", with standard services that meet environmental and hygiene requirements, and to promote the Dadal travel brand at home and abroad. It has set the goal of developing at least five types of responsible tours and routes, based on the resources and carrying capacity of Onon-Balj National Park and its surroundings, without harming nature and with the participation of local people. These include photo tours to watch flowers, butterflies, rare insects, birds and animals; nature and history tours of historical sites; health tours; sport hunting tours; tours to learn about Buryat life and customs; and tours with cultural and sporting events. It also aims to build tourist camps, campsites and camping spots with nature-friendly technology and services and to expand the eco-zone. **Community participation and a green lifestyle.** Believing that every resident's awareness, knowledge and skills are key to becoming a model green sum, the sum will train its people step by step. In raising citizens who are proud of the Great Khaan's birthplace and live a green lifestyle, it will ensure the joint participation of government, citizens and non-governmental organizations and build new partnerships. Advisory teams on health, education and business will work with residents. The goals include introducing healthy habits to extend life expectancy, running lifelong learning programs, offering training and outreach suited to every age, and starting a movement to learn a green lifestyle. The sum will also support work with technology that is safe for human health and the environment and reduce the number of unemployed and low-income households. **Environment, settlements and waste.** The aim is to become a green land that keeps its nature intact by building nature-friendly attitudes and habits among the public and by using and protecting resources efficiently. The sum will develop its network of national and local protected areas, improve their management, and sustainably preserve biodiversity and ecosystem services. It will reduce the impact of climate change and strengthen its ability to prevent dangerous weather, natural disasters and forest and steppe fires. In the sum center and other settlements it will support sustainable production and consumption with advanced technology and innovation and increase investment in clean technology. It will strengthen community-based nature conservation, create a healthy living environment and improve waste management.

## What is there to see in Dadal sum?

The sum has many sights: places from the legends of Chinggis Khaan and his ancestors and from "The Secret History of the Mongols", museums, religious monuments, mineral springs and caves. The GPS coordinates of each place are given in brackets.

### Places linked to Chinggis Khaan

- [**Deluun Boldog**](</destination/landscapes/deluun-boldog>)**.** On July 20, 1990, for the 750th anniversary of "The Secret History of the Mongols", a sky worship ceremony was held on Ereen Hill at Boldog and a memorial stele was erected. The stele says in traditional Mongolian script: "Chinggis Khaan was born here at Deluun Boldog on the 16th day of the first summer month of the Water Horse year, 1162." (N49°03’04.7 E111°38’36.3)
- **Chinggis Khaan memorial.** Built on May 31, 1962, on the northern terrace of Middle Lake of the Three Lakes, for the 800th anniversary of Chinggis Khaan's birth. The work was led by S. Tumur-Ochir, a member of the Political Bureau of the Mongolian People's Revolutionary Party, and designed by the sculptor L. Makhbal. The memorial is shaped like a blazing flame, 11.7 m tall, with a base 10 m wide. Its front shows a standing figure of Chinggis Khaan and is carved with his teaching: "Though my body may tire, let my nation never fall." It is 2 km east of the sum center. (N49°02’00.40 E111°39’16.70)
- **Temujin Toono Gate.** A gate in the shape of a stupa, built in 2012 with donations from the sum's residents. (N48°59’33.27 E111°36’41.21)
- **Energy Ovoo.** An ovoo (sacred stone cairn) where people pray, believing they receive energy from the heavens. It is 0.5 km southwest of Deluun Boldog. (N49°02’58.3 E111°38’28.6)
- **Khajuu Spring.** Found at the eastern side of the Khar Tug Ovoo, northeast of the sum. Legend says that when Temujin was born he was washed with water from this spring, and that Mother Hoelun used it to make tea and to drink. It is 2 km northwest of Deluun Boldog. (N49°02’45.9 E111°36’53.0)
- **Tunkheleg Stream.** By this stream, often mentioned in "The Secret History of the Mongols", Queen Alan Gua is said to have met Dobun Mergen. Bodonchar the Holy is said to have spoken his famous saying here, "It is good when a body has a head and a robe has a collar", and the last queens of Mongolia's last Khan, Dondogdulam and Genenpil, lived in this area. It is 4 km west of Khajuu Spring. (N49°02’50.82 E111°38’28.69)
- **Dobun Mergen's monument.** A very ancient monument made of several standing stones. Locals have long called it the monument and grave of Duwa Sokhor and Dobun Mergen. It is 1.5 km northwest of Tunkheleg Stream. (N49°03’17.18 E111°33’09.11)
- **Bodonchar's grass hut.** Paragraph 24 of "The Secret History of the Mongols" tells how Bodonchar the Simple, founder of the Kiyad Borjigin clan of Chinggis Khaan's Golden Lineage, lived in a grass hut on Balj Island, which is this place. He was the first to say the wise words "A person needs an elder, a robe needs a collar." It is 1.5 km northwest of the khirigsuur (burial mound) of Dobun Mergen and Duwa Sokhor. (N49°03’31.5 E111°31’07.0)
- **Chinggis Khaan's Khar Tug Ovoo.** It is said that before setting out on campaigns, Chinggis Khaan raised his great black banner on top of this mountain. (N49°02’11.4 E111°35’54.50)
- **Tamga Stone.** According to "Altan Tobchi" (the Golden Summary), when a small bird sang "Chinggis, Chinggis" on a large black rock on the mountain, the rock split open and the swastika seal of the Mongol state came out of it. The split rock is still called "Chinggis's Seal Stone". (N49°06’17.63 E111°39’18.12)
- **Santkhaan Mountain.** A sacred mountain north of the meeting of the Onon and Agats rivers, with the "Santkhaan Ovoo" on top. Legend says two male foals were born here to a gray mare of Chinggis Khaan's horse herd. Foals born here are said to become fast racehorses, so before training horses for Naadam people worship the Santkhaan Ovoo and make offerings. Herders still graze their horses and let them foal on this mountain. (N49°12’40.9 E111°50’28.9)
- **Degen Jigen (Zeeren Gorge).** A historic place recorded as the Zeeren Gorge of the Onon River in paragraph 129 of "The Secret History of the Mongols". (N49°14’23.0 E112°01’13.3)
- **Burkhan Khaldun – Bayankhaan Mountain.** Locals consider Bayankhaan Mountain, with its three peaks Burkhant, Shurgadai and Orgoit, to be Burkhan Khaldun Mountain, where Chinggis Khaan hid from the Merkits, survived, and vowed to worship it "for generations to come". The forested and rocky back slopes are rich in fruit trees and home to rare animals such as moose, red deer, bears and musk deer, as well as many kinds of birds. (N49°01’11.00 E111°18’46.08)

### Museums and religious sites

- **Temujin's Childhood Museum.** Opened in 2006 for the 800th anniversary of the founding of the Mongol state. Exhibits include stone, bronze and iron artifacts of ancient people of the Onon and Balj basins, straw collages showing Temujin's childhood, a seated figure of Chinggis Khaan, the black banner, and a palace tent on a cart. (N49°01’12.86 E111°37’26.81)
- **Local History Museum.** First founded in 1959 on the initiative of the sum's young herders to share their experience. It has expanded its collection since 1971 and now holds 346 historical exhibits. (N49°01’12.80 E111°37’30.03)
- **Hunter Baavai's Museum.** A ger museum with the lifelong collection of works and stuffed animals of D. Zunduidagva, a local veteran, hunter and holder of the Order of the Red Banner of Military Valor. It also has historical exhibits on Buryat customs. It is 1 km southwest of the site of Queen Genenpil's ger. (N49°01’53.98 E111°33’10.37)
- **Site of Queen Genenpil's ger.** Land of the Kiyad Borjigin Golden Lineage, famous as the home of the keepers of the Mongol state's sacred white horse herd. Genenpil, the last queen of the Eighth Bogd Khan, who held both religious and state power, spent her summers on this terrace until she was arrested on false political charges. It is 2 km southeast of Bodonchar's grass hut. (N49°02’16.04 E111°33’12.71)
- **Three Stupas of the Lineage.** Stupas topped with the three lineage deities Avalokiteshvara, Manjushri and Vajrapani. They were built to increase merit and cleanse sins, in memory of ancestors who died before their time in the political repressions of the 1930s. (N49°01’05.18 E111°36’45.09)
- **Ruins of Gilber Monastery.** The remains of a monastery that was a major center of Gelug Buddhist culture with about 300 monks from 1700 to 1937. It is said that on September 20, 1920, the Russian general Baron Ungern passed by this monastery with his Asiatic Cavalry Division as he came down the Gilber River into Bogd Khan's Mongolia. (N49°11’24.46 E111°24’09.07)

### Nature, mineral springs and caves

- **Gurvannuur (Three Lakes).** Three small lakes close together on a flat area inside a pine forest, famous as the "Gurvannuur mineral springs". The lake water and mud are used to treat stomach, liver, gallbladder and eczema problems. Middle Lake has leeches and is said to help joint diseases, while the water of East Lake is drunk for internal and bowel conditions. Read about the sanatorium here on the [Gurvannuur Spa Sanatorium page](</things-to-do/wellness/gurvannuuryn-rashaan-suvilal>). (N49°01’54.94 E111°39’15.38)
- **Tsagaanchuluut.** A place that could be called a natural physiotherapy complex, believed to have the power to heal all kinds of illness by traditional and natural methods. (N49°05’23.70 E111°03’19.80)
- **Nukht Cave of Suugt.** Hidden from view on a short back slope of a rocky mountain on the east fork of the Jivkheestei River, on the west side of the valley of the Suugt River, a tributary of the Galttai River. It is a vertical, well-like cave 72 m deep. (N49°10’20.78 E111°58’35.61)

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Dadal sum from Ulaanbaatar?

It is 600 km from Ulaanbaatar and 270 km from Undurkhaan, the center of Khentii Province. The road is rough, so we recommend preparing well for a long trip.

### Which places linked to Chinggis Khaan can I see in Dadal?

You can see Deluun Boldog, the Chinggis Khaan memorial built in 1962, Khajuu Spring, Tunkheleg Stream, Chinggis Khaan's Khar Tug Ovoo, the Tamga Stone and Temujin's Childhood Museum.

### Where did the Altargana festival start?

The Altargana Buryat song festival was first held in Dadal sum in 1994. It has since become an international festival.
