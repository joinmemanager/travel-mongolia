Tes soum lies in the north of Zavkhan Province (aimag), between the Tannu-Ola Range and the branch mountains of the Khan Khukhii Range. It once had a large state farm that supplied flour and fodder to the western provinces, and today it is an interesting place for its mountains, rivers, forests, many kinds of wildlife and birds, and many monuments that have not yet been studied.

## Where is Tes soum?

The soum lies more than 1,300 meters above sea level. It borders [Bayantes](</province/zavkhan/bayantes>) and [Bayankhairkhan](/province/zavkhan/bayankhairkhan) soums of Zavkhan Province, Zuunkhangai soum of Uvs Province, and the Republic of Tuva in Russia. Its total area is 87,229 hectares. The soum center is at a place called Zur, 301 km from the provincial center. For other soums and sights of the province, read the [Zavkhan Province page](/province/zavkhan).

## How many people live in Tes and what are its bags?

Tes soum has four bags: Doono, Niseg, Bayan-Uul and Zur. The soum has 3,386 people in 894 households and 49,200 head of livestock.

## How did Tes become a soum?

The administrative history of this area begins in the 17th century. In 1655 the banner (khoshuu) of Erdeneduuregch Van of Zasagt Khan aimag, one of the Khotogoid banners, was founded here. Under Qing rule it was called the left-wing banner of the middle left of the western road of Khalkha. From 1924 to 1931 it was Delgerkhan Uul banner of Khantaishir Uul aimag, and from 1931 to 1959 it was part of Bayan-Uul soum. The present soum dates back to 1958. That year the people of Doono, Bayan-Uul, Berkh and Niseg bags of Bayan-Uul soum proposed a change, saying their land was too far from the soum center and did not fit well. After the proposal was checked on the ground, a resolution of the Presidium of the People's Great Khural of the Mongolian People's Republic on May 31, 1959, separated the land and people of these bags into a new soum named Tes, based on the "Delgerekh" cooperative. In this way a new soum was created in 1959 on the site of the Tes fodder farm of Bayan-Uul soum. At first the new soum had 341 households, 1,507 people, 52,400 head of livestock and 2,298 hectares of land. Soon after, as part of the nationwide virgin lands movement, a state farm was founded in Tes soum in 1969. But because livestock pastures and crop fields were considered a poor fit side by side, in 1974 the "Delgerekh" cooperative of Tes soum was dissolved and merged with the "Jargalant-Amidral" cooperative of Bayan-Uul soum into the "Delgerekh-Amidral" cooperative under Bayan-Uul soum. The status of Tes soum was then cancelled, and it was renamed the Tes State Farm of Bayantes soum, or Tes Committee, with Ch. Yadamjav appointed head of both the committee and the state farm. At that time Tes had 205 households, more than 700 people and 23,000 hectares of crop rotation land. This did not last long, however. Decree No. 138 of the Presidium of the People's Great Khural of the Mongolian People's Republic in 1977 made the Tes Committee a soum again. In the 1990s Doono and Niseg bags of Bayantes soum, which had once belonged to the old Tes soum, moved back to Tes soum, and the soum took on its present borders.

## How was the "Delgerekh" cooperative founded?

The "Delgerekh" cooperative, which worked on the land of Tes, was founded in 1956 on the initiative of G. Ish, G. Ichinnorov, I. Damdinbazar and L. Magsar. It began with 23 members from 17 households of bags I, II, III and IX of Bayan-Uul soum pooling more than 2,000 head of livestock, and I. Damdinbazar was elected its first chairman. Later the cooperative grew to three livestock brigades, more than 500 members, fixed assets of MNT 3 million and an annual income of MNT 1 million. By 1972 it had more than 2,500 members in more than 500 households, 67,700 head of livestock, fixed assets of MNT 4 million and an annual income of MNT 2 million. When the soum was dissolved in 1974 because of the Tes state farm, the cooperative was merged into the "Jargalant-Amidral" cooperative of Bayan-Uul soum.

## What was the Tes state farm?

As a result of the virgin lands movement of the 1960s, and under Resolution No. 157 of the Council of Ministers of the Mongolian People's Republic in 1969, construction of the Tes state farm began with help from the Soviet Union. At that time the livestock machinery station of Bayan-Uul soum was closed, and more than 20 machines worth MNT 2 million in total, including 9 tracked and wheeled tractors, farm trailers and dump trucks, were transferred to the Tes state farm. In 1969 the first plowing was done at Tevkhen, Tsagaanchuluut and Tuunt, 3,056 hectares were cultivated and 3,100 tons of hay were made, and production began. In 1972 the Soviet side handed over the farm, with fixed assets of MNT 35 million. It included the following facilities:

- a fodder plant able to produce 10,000 tons of fodder a year;
- a farm machinery repair shop able to service 120 tractors;
- a garage for about 70 vehicles;
- a 900 kW power station;
- a steam boiler producing 10,000 Gcal of heat a year;
- a 35-seat workers' canteen, a bakery and a bathhouse;
- apartments for 50 families.

At first the Tes state farm had no livestock at all and only grew crops. In 1973 it received 200 cattle and 220 horses from the Baruunturuun state farm in Uvs Province, and in 1975 more than 8,000 sheep and goats, 37 herders and 13 winter camps from Bayantes soum. Soon it also ran side businesses raising pigs, chickens and rabbits. The farm combined crop growing, livestock and fodder production: 88.6 percent of its total output came from crops, 5.2 percent from livestock and 6.2 percent from fodder. In 1987 the Tes state farm had 2,900 people in 529 households and 32,000 hectares of crop rotation land. Each year it sowed grain on 19,500 hectares and harvested 19,000–28,000 tons. This harvest not only met the region's flour needs but also supplied wheat to the flour mills of Ulaangom and Murun. Each year it also prepared about 10,000 tons of compound livestock feed and delivered it to the five western provinces. This farming came to a stop during the hard years of the market transition in the 1990s.

## What are the climate and soil like?

Tes soum has a harsh continental climate. Summer temperatures average 23–32 °C, and winters drop to −35 to −42 °C. Annual precipitation is 180–210 mm. The soils are fertile dark brown and sandy soils.

## What nature will I find: mountains, rivers, plants and animals?

**Mountains.** Ikh Berkh, Baga Berkh, Baruun Doono, Zuun Doono, Khalban, Zur, Zos, Dormon, Nart, Navkhan, Ongon, Kholboo, Talyn Uvuljin, Aambagaa, Jaambagaa, Arts Suuri, Argalant and Suman Rock. **Rivers and streams.** Tes, Zaigal, Tsereg, Mogoi and Khatguurantai. You can read more about the Tes River on the [Tes River Area page](</destination/protected/tesiin-gol-orchmyn-nutag>). **Plants.** Plants include burnet, yellow sweetvetch, wild garlic, lily, wild onion, mangir onion, wild chives, peony and tagsh. Berries and nuts are plentiful: pine nuts, sea buckthorn, crowberry, currant, wild raspberry and bird cherry. Trees and shrubs include Siberian pine, larch, aspen, spruce, willow, dog rose, cinquefoil and ephedra. **Wildlife.** Red deer, wild boar, roe deer, badger, jirkh, mountain hare, hare, wolf, lynx, fox, corsac fox, Pallas's cat, marmot, squirrel, ermine, Siberian weasel, steppe polecat, nokhoi zeekh and steppe marten live here. **Birds.** Black grouse, hazel grouse, raven, golden eagle, vulture, magpie, turliakh, hawk, sar, owl, capercaillie, lark, hoopoe, Daurian partridge, cuckoo, swan, goose, ruddy shelduck, crane and bustard are seen here.

## What is there to see in Tes soum?

The soum has natural and historical sites. The best known are **Suman Rock**, **Dormon Mountain**, **Khuulga Cave** and the **Zur Turkic inscription**. Besides these, dozens of monuments here have not yet been studied.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Tes soum from the provincial center?

Zur, the center of Tes soum, is 301 km from the center of Zavkhan Province. The soum lies at the northern edge of the province, on the border with the Republic of Tuva in Russia.

### When was Tes soum founded?

It was separated from Bayan-Uul soum by a resolution of the Presidium of the People's Great Khural of the Mongolian People's Republic on May 31, 1959. It was dissolved in 1974 and became the Tes Committee, but Decree No. 138 of 1977 made it a soum again.

### What was the Tes state farm known for?

In 1987 it sowed grain on 19,500 hectares a year and harvested 19,000–28,000 tons. It supplied wheat to the flour mills of Ulaangom and Murun and livestock feed to the five western provinces.
