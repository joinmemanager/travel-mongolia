Nomgon soum is one of the largest soums of Umnugovi Province (aimag), and Orvog Gashuun Bor Tolgoi, the southernmost point of Mongolia, lies within it. It is a Gobi land of the last mountains of the Mongolian Altai Range, sacred sand dunes, more than 70 springs, ruins of old monasteries and rock paintings.

## Where is Nomgon soum?

The soum is in the far south of Mongolia and covers 19,000 km². It borders China to the south, Khurmen soum of the same province to the west, Khankhongor to the north and [Bayan-Ovoo](/province/umnugov/bayan-ovoo) to the east. It is 670 km from Ulaanbaatar and 106 km from the provincial center. For other soums of the province, read the [Umnugovi Province page](/province/umnugov).

## How did Nomgon soum come about?

In 1924 two soums were founded at the same time on the land of today's Nomgon soum. One was Bulgan soum of Delgerkhangai Uul banner, founded at a place called Tsokhiogiin Tsagaan Del, and the other was Mandal soum, founded the same year at Gashuun Boom. Both belonged to the newly reorganized Shankhai Uul banner. From its founding, Bulgan soum moved through places such as Sult, Bosgokh, Bilgekh, Narin Tsokhio and Altai, and in 1928 it settled at today's Kharmagtai. In the administrative reform of 1931, when Umnugovi Province was created, Bulgan soum, centered at Kharmagtai, was renamed Nomgon soum. This is how today's Nomgon soum took shape, with 13 districts, more than 300 households, 1,200 people and 110,000 head of livestock.

## What is the land like: mountains, Gobi and sand dunes?

The land of Nomgon soum is rich in scenic ranges at the end of the Mongolian Altai Range, wide plains and valleys, Gobi desert, and gorges and hills. High mountains of the Mongolian Altai uplands include the Buur, Nomgon, Bulgan, Tsokhio, Khalzan and Dalan ranges, Khachig Khurkh and the Gurvan Gedreg Range. The southern part is desert-like Gobi. Here are sand dunes that have been worshipped since ancient times, such as Zalaa Khoshuu, Bargin Sersheg, Khongor Khar Tsav, Ikh Baga Uul and Duut Els (Singing Sands).

## What plants and wildlife live here?

Trees such as elm, poplar, saxaul, tamarisk and jujube grow here. Many kinds of plants are plentiful, including wild almond, red and golden peashrub, sondool, zagsgal, shavag, wormwood, tana onion, khumuul, wild onion, kherenii nud, licorice and cistanche. The mountain and desert zones are home to many kinds of wildlife, including protected argali, Siberian ibex, Mongolian wild ass (khulan), goitered gazelle and Mongolian gazelle, as well as snow leopard, lynx, wolf, fox, corsac fox, Pallas's cat and stone marten.

## What is there to see in Nomgon soum?

**Springs and mineral springs.** More than 70 springs, such as Borzon, Bayanbulag, Erdenebulag and Tsatgalan, have open water. There are scenic places such as Sangiin Dalai, Elgen and Urtyn mineral springs, and Altan, Khaich, Tsokhiot, Ulaan Bulag, Ulaan Ereg, Shiluustei, Borzon and Morin Tolgoi. **Historical monuments.** Historic places include the ruins of the Sangiin Dalai, Ekhen Zag and Urt Ulaan Ereg monasteries, the rock inscriptions and paintings of Urtbichigt Kholboo Tulga, the Urtyn stone figure, and the Kheremen Road. **Gobi mountains.** The famous Borzon Gobi, Khurkh, Buur, Ikh and Baga Nomgon mountains, Khurvuugch and the Guramsan Valley are in this soum. **Mongolia's southernmost point.** Orvog Gashuun Bor Tolgoi, the southernmost point of Mongolia, lies at the southern edge of the soum.
